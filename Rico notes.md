## 2026-08-31 — The play is the reward, not the unlocks

A Deep Rock Galactic reviewer (`85232716`, 2021-01-23), unprompted:

> Some games end up feeling like work after you grind for a while realizing that you're playing to
> get stronger to play to get stronger... and then it all just gets stale and feels pointless. This
> game is actually fun and exciting though — every mission is different and you never know how things
> will shake out. You have assignments to earn unlocks and chase upgrades, but the missions
> themselves are intrinsically rewarding and worthwhile — **I'm never playing just to reach a goal.**

**Rico:** *"I feel this way playing Helldivers 2. Very fun to play."*

Tagged in the review tree as `game-design.replayability.worth-playing-without-a-reward` (built round
77). Its opposite is `game-design.progression.unlock-pace.nothing-left-to-chase` — the same subject,
built round 69 from four Deep Rock players who ran out of reasons to play.

**The distinction:** `keeps-pulling-you-back` is about returning. This is about a single session
being worth it with **nothing to earn**. Two different design claims, and the second is the one both
Rico and this reviewer are naming.

## 2026-08-31 — Fix the playstyle mismatch with Discord, not with code

**Rico, on open gap 6** — teammates who want different things from the same run:

> *"I think there's just a miscommunication issue. People just not typing into chat what they want, or
> maybe we can just have, like, a way for people to better communicate. It could be on Discord. Like,
> hey — if you're looking for fun, if you're looking for a group, looking to max out rewards and
> points, looking to do as many missions as possible. I think we can fix this with Discord."*

**The observation behind it** (`178648003`, 1,790 hours, thumbs up):

> Says some players play for fun and rewards and others only care about the mission, and he seeks out
> players who want the same thing he does.

**What Rico named that the reviewer did not:** the reviewer describes the collision and works around
it by hand-picking teammates. **Rico's read is that the collision is a signalling failure, not a
design failure** — the players already know what they want, and nothing in the game or around it lets
them say so before the run starts.

**The cheap version is outside the game.** Named channels or a role picker — *for fun* · *for
rewards* · *for mission clears* — costs no engine work and no patch. **The expensive version is a
lobby flag inside the game**, which is the same idea with a build behind it.

**For Dominion:** if extraction runs can be entered for different reasons — a clear, a full sweep, a
laugh — decide **where the player says which one**, and prefer the free answer first.

Tagged in the review tree as `community.player-conduct.players-want-different-things-from-a-run`
(built round 111, on Rico's ruling that this is player conduct rather than co-op design —
*"it's how people behave"*).
