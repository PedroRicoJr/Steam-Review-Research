## What they said

- Says they avoid early access, but with 200-plus hours in the first game they bought this on impulse and it is better - the problems inherent to the first game's engine were resolved, and it translated to 3D smoothly.
    → marketing.positioning.successor-framing-accepted       (good)
- Says solid, impactful gameplay that feels great, especially late game.
    → game-design.game-feel.controls.responsive-and-clear    (good)
- Says movement is a little rough in the early game.
    → game-design.game-feel.movement.unknown                 (~)
- Says well thought-out, nuanced items - almost every item is good solo, and with friends deciding who gets what item makes the difference between a successful run and a frustrating one.
    → game-design.progression.build-and-customisation.deep-and-varied (good)
- Says every character feels unique and useful, and pretty much any combination of characters is viable.
    → game-design.role-design.any-team-mix-works             (good)
- Says solid multiplayer - everyone connects well and the game keeps up.
    → engineering.netcode.smooth-online                      (good)
- Says phenomenal atmosphere - the music and level design are to die for.
    → art.atmosphere.draws-you-in                            (good)
- Says for an early access game at 20 dollars it feels complete, well put together and full of content.
    → production.early-access.good-value-while-unfinished    (good)
- Says active, transparent development - the devs are clear with their plans and have gone beyond expectations; they have stuck by the schedule.
    → community.developer-communication.open-about-what-it-is-doing (good)
- Says like the first game there is a lack of hard incentives to progress; the repetitiveness is up front.
    → game-design.progression.unlock-pace.unknown            (~)
- Says the starting character is one of the hardest to use early, especially with friends.
    → game-design.role-design.unknown                        (~)
- Says some item effects are not intuitive and are misrepresented by their descriptions - bleed stacks linearly and caps at seven daggers, while Tough Times does not stack linearly at all and takes source digging and maths to understand.
    → game-design.progression.complexity.requires-outside-research (bad)
- Says most of the story is hidden in item descriptions and logs - compelling environmental storytelling, but the complete absence of cutscenes, voiceover or text transitions may turn off players who want structure.
    → narrative.story.no-cutscenes-to-carry-the-story        (bad)
- Says some really good, even essential items are locked behind hard challenges.
    → game-design.progression.unlock-pace.unknown            (~)
- Says the easiest difficulty is seriously easy - a few levels in it takes planning to kill yourself.
    → game-design.difficulty-tuning.the-lower-settings-are-not-worth-playing (bad)
- Says no way to stop a good run and resume later, even solo, even with the console - it removes the temptation to cheat, but it does suck sometimes.
    → game-design.session-flexibility.cannot-save-and-come-back (bad)
- Says the first game's art had more charm and magic; they like the new look but it does not feel as inspired - lumpy models and muddy texturing, though lighting, colours and character designs are still fantastic.
    → art.fidelity.rough-in-places                           (bad)
- Says the levels are small; they would like more expansive ones.
    → game-design.level-design.the-levels-are-too-small      (bad)
- Says 10/10 - they became a god and had to end a 2.5-hour run because the office was closing.
    → game-design.game-feel.combat.makes-you-feel-superhumanly-strong (good)
- Says for 20 dollars it is worth it, even in early access - especially in early access if the devs continue as they have.
    → publishing.price.fair                                  (good)
