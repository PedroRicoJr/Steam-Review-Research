- Says the main con: it ends up feeling shallow - every item is available to everyone, a long run gives you several copies of everything, so there are almost no 'builds' and successful runs feel very similar even across different survivors.
    → game-design.progression.build-and-customisation.shallow-options (bad)
- Says it lacks longevity compared to most roguelikes, and does not have quite the same replayability as comparable games, especially given the price.
    → game-design.replayability.runs-out-fast                (bad)
- Says enemy and boss difficulty fluctuates wildly and is at odds with how early they are met.
    → game-design.difficulty-tuning.swings-wildly-within-a-run (bad)
- Says elite common enemies are often the most dangerous thing on the field, eclipsing bosses - some end runs in a single hit and bypass the built-in one-shot protection, whose mere presence they read as a design problem.
    → game-design.enemy-design.one-hit-kills                 (bad)
- Says several unlockables sit behind challenges that practically force you to play on the easiest difficulty, and some items barred behind them are among the most powerful; several alternate abilities are direct upgrades.
    → game-design.progression.unlock-pace.the-strongest-options-are-locked-behind-challenges (bad)
- Says item rarity is sometimes at odds with usefulness - critical chance and bleed are common, while uncommons include slight slowdown on hit and pitiful turrets - so an uncommon can be the unlucky outcome.
    → game-design.power-balance.rarity-does-not-match-usefulness (bad)
- Says lunar coins go from a limited resource at the start to virtually unlimited and easier to get than per-stage money.
    → game-design.power-balance.resources-too-plentiful      (bad)
- Says a fun game they can recommend, held back by frustrating elements; if you are not certain, wait for a sale.
    → publishing.sale-dependency.buy-on-sale-only            (bad)
