usually halved because people wrote less, not because they liked it less. **Compare modes within a

**How to read the words below.** A **bullet** is one thing one person said. A **tag** is where that
bullet was filed. Tags have three levels: a **division** (who at a studio owns it — game design, art,
engineering), a **subject** (the thing being talked about), and a **mode** (what actually happened).
`unknown` means the person raised a subject and never said what about it.

## 1. The short version

**The grind is the game, and the players know it.** The top complaint by far is
`unlock-pace.grindy` — **264 bullets, 8.2 per 100 reviews**, flat across all thirteen years (6.8 to
9.6). **223 of the 264 sit under a thumbs up (84%).** The grind is named as the cost of a game people
keep recommending. A reviewer at 1,131 hours (200 found it helpful) calls it very grindy, especially
for new players, and says *"Money is purely for cosmetics. (Or to reduce the grind out of some stuff)"*.
The thumbs-down at 6,720 hours (456 found it helpful) names *"an extremely heavy grind and a general
disrespect to the player's time"* — and still lists great visuals.

**Free-to-play is read as fair, not as a trap.** `price.fair` is **198** (the third most common tag),
`everything-earnable` **133**, `a-fair-business-model` 37, `cosmetic-only` 35, `players-buy-in-to-
support-the-studio` 30, `currency-earnable-by-playing` 22, and the player market works (46). Against
them: `what-you-can-own-is-capped-unless-you-pay` 21, `pay-affects-play` 16, `you-end-up-paying-
sooner-or-later` 15, `the-shop-charges-far-too-much` 12, `feels-like-a-cash-grab` 10. **Monetisation
praise outnumbers monetisation complaint 134 to 103**, and the biggest single monetisation tag is the
neutral one: **`paying-only-shortens-the-grind`, 73** — money buys time, not power.

**The pull is praised and feared in the same breath.** `keeps-pulling-you-back` is **221**;
`warns-that-it-takes-over-your-life` is **74 bullets in 73 reviews, and 60 of the 73 are thumbs up.** *"Relationships are temporary. The Void's embrace lasts forever."* (131 found it helpful). *"You never
quit warframe, just take longer breaks"* — `interest-comes-and-goes-in-waves` is **44**. Nine more say they keep playing while hating
it. **Those three modes exist only in Warframe** (section 6).

**The first hours are the weak point, and it never goes away.** 267 bullets are about learning the game
(`new-player-experience` and `progression.complexity`): *overwhelming at first* 72, *requires outside
research* 43, *poorly explained* 42, *slow start* 36, against *rewarding once learned* 34 and *easy to
start* 15. **A reviewer at 1,389 hours jokes they are almost done with the tutorial.**

**What it does not have is a technical problem.** `engineering` is **3.9%** of everything said;
*crashes repeatedly* is 4 bullets in 3,233 reviews, and `runs-well-on-modest-hardware` (29) outnumbers
`demanding-hardware` (12).

## 3. ⭐ The grind: the complaint that everyone makes and almost nobody leaves over

**Four modes describe the same loop.**

| Mode | | Bullets | Under a thumbs up |
|---|---|---|---|
| `unlock-pace.grindy` | − | 264 | 223 (84%) |
| `unlock-pace.grind-feels-earned` | + | 111 | — |
| `monetisation-practice.paying-only-shortens-the-grind` | ~ | 73 | — |
| `replayability.warns-that-it-takes-over-your-life` | − | 74 | 60 of 73 reviews (82%) |

**The grind is filed as a complaint and written as a condition.** At 1,131 hours (up, 200 found it
helpful): very grindy, especially when new, and new content needs a lot of resources. At 60 hours
(down): *"my progress felt so slow"*. At 1,917 hours (down): *"I shouldn't consider your game a damn
second job."* The thumbs-down grind reviews are not new players who bounced: **the 41 of them have a
median of 194 hours, and 11 show over 1,000.**

**Money is read as a way to skip the wait, not to win.** The 73 *paying only shortens the grind* lines
run from boosters that raise the amount gained but not the drop odds, so rare stays rare (up), to
the moment *"you do not want to spend another week farming the same one type of enemy, boss, or
mission"* and pay (down) — the same fact, a different verdict. **The monetisation fight in this game is about time.**

**Time gates are the sharpest edge of it.** `gated-behind-real-world-time` is 49 — crafting timers of
12 hours to 3 days — and 3 bullets take the other side (`time-gating-does-not-get-in-the-way`), among them 234735996 at
704 hours: *"Crafting times help me pace rather than blowing through everything in a few months."*

**The loop is praised as a habit in words that are also a warning.** 221 `keeps-pulling-you-back`, 74
`warns-that-it-takes-over-your-life`, 44 `interest-comes-and-goes-in-waves`, 9
`keeps-playing-while-hating-it`. *"You never quit warframe, just take longer breaks"* appears, in some
form, in three reviews. **The last three modes appear in no other game in the queue.**

| **Fair** | `price.fair` 198, `everything-earnable` 133, `a-fair-business-model` 37, `cosmetic-only` 35, `players-buy-in-to-support-the-studio` 30, `currency-earnable-by-playing` 22, `the-player-market-works` 46 | **501** |
| **Neutral** | `paying-only-shortens-the-grind` 73, `advises-what-not-to-spend-on` 22, `monetisation-practice.unknown` 16 | **111** |
| **Unfair** | `what-you-can-own-is-capped-unless-you-pay` 21, `pay-affects-play` 16, `you-end-up-paying-sooner-or-later` 15, `the-shop-charges-far-too-much` 12, `feels-like-a-cash-grab` 10, others | **103** in `monetisation-practice` |

**The player market is what makes the premium currency earnable, and the reviews say so.** One
reviewer says the studio's platinum prices are very high but player-set prices are rock bottom; another
that *"you can even earn the platinum used to buy things in the market through trading in game items
with players."* The
premium currency can be traded between players, so a free player can buy what the shop sells by
selling what they farm. **46 bullets praise the market; 6 say it works badly; 1 names scammers.**

**The complaints are about slots and prices, not power.** The biggest unfair mode is *what you can own
is capped unless you pay* (inventory slots for frames and weapons, 21) — and the neutral *advises what
not to spend on* (22) includes the same advice 8 times — *"Spend your free plat on slots"*. `pay-affects-play`
is 16 in 3,233 reviews.

**`players-buy-in-to-support-the-studio` is 30** — reviewers who buy something they do not need *to pay
for what they get out of the game*. 0.93 per 100 reviews, the highest rate in the queue (The Anacrusis
0.82, Arcrunner 0.56).

**Section 4 is the one part of this page that describes a free-to-play model, and Dominion is not
free-to-play.** It is recorded here because it is most of what the reviews say.

## 5. What the sample says about the design itself

**It is a co-op game that works alone.** `much-better-with-friends` 114, `works-solo` 42 against
`punishing-solo` 11 (9 of them before 2019) and `needs-a-group` 9. The co-op praise is about
company more than mechanics: all `co-op-design` modes together are 21 bullets, and 3 of those complain
that story quests must be played alone.

**The community is praised far more than it is blamed.** `welcoming-community` 122,
`welcoming-to-newcomers` 13, `a-place-to-make-friends` and `strangers-became-friends` in single
figures, against `veterans-will-not-help-newcomers` 3, `a-strong-player-kills-everything-first` 3 and
the chat-filter complaints (`the-chat-filter-bans-for-harmless-words` 6). **Welcoming is the game's ninth most common tag.**

**The new-player problem is thirteen years old and named in every period.** 267 bullets on learning the
game. The reviews' own fix is to teach: *"ask for help and guidance as often as you can early on"*, *"Set
your matchmaking to solo for the first few missions"*, *"Don't feel dumb looking up plot summaries of
missions after you beat them."* **In the
reviews, the teaching is done by other players.**

**Movement is praised as a skill** (`movement.rewards-mastery` 69) and combat as weight
(`combat.impactful` 113, `makes-you-feel-superhumanly-strong` 34). Two bullets say the levels do not suit the movement.

**For Dominion, with Rico's stop reason in mind:** the lines that carry across are the ones not tied to
free-to-play — **a grind is tolerated when everything can be earned and the reward is visible; a
tutorial that lags the systems is a complaint for years; a co-op game praised for its company also has
to work alone; movement that rewards practice is named by name.** The run-based, extraction and arena
questions Dominion needs answered are not in this sample, because the game has none of those.

## 7. Confidence and limits

- **The sample is 1.07% of the population.** Whole-sample shares carry ±2.16%. Each period (480 to 720
  reviews) carries roughly ±3.7 to ±4.5; any single month is direction only.
- **Reviews shrank from 67 words to 28 on average.** Per-100 rates across periods mostly measure that.
  Only changes much larger than the fall in length (repetitive, looks-great) are read as real above.
- **581 reviews (18.0%) were edited after creation and are counted on the created date.** The flattening
  rule is open with Rico.
- **Two reading sessions.** Batches 1–14 (700 reviews, 2013-03 to 2016-02) were read before the stop on
  2026-09-24; batches 15–64 after. The filing habits of the two may differ; `best-in-its-category`
  (section 2) is the one place the tables suggest it.
- **The launch is barely covered.** 5 reviews from 2013-03 (of 460). Nothing here describes the open
  beta's first weeks.
- **No patch notes were pulled.** Update names and dates come from the reviews.
- **Hours are the hours shown on the review**, which may be total hours at the time of the pull rather
  than at the time of writing; 6 bullets say the Steam hours are not the reviewer's real time
  (`says-the-recorded-playtime-is-wrong`).
