# Warframe — English

**A sample: 3,235 of 302,482 English reviews read, ±2.16% on any whole-sample share.** The grid
(2026-09-14) summed 302,482 English reviews across 163 months; Steam's own English count on 2026-09-25
was 303,040. Figures are as of 2026-09-25.

**A free-to-play third-person co-op looter from Digital Extremes, on Steam since 2013-03** — thirteen
years of live service, the longest run in the queue. You play missions alone or with a squad, as what
reviewers call a "space ninja", with fast parkour movement. You farm parts to build new characters
(frames) and weapons; building takes real-world time (crafting timers of 12 hours to 3 days). Money buys
a premium currency (platinum) that can also be traded between players, so a free player can earn it by
selling what they farm. It is an open-ended loop of missions, not runs that end. The pages do not give
the squad size or the engine. 684,199 reviews in all languages on 2026-09-25, 87% positive, *Very
Positive*. **Rico stopped this game on 2026-09-24 because it is not the roguelike Dominion is aiming at**
(no run with a start and an end, no extraction, free-to-play), and resumed it the same week as research;
it was later finished. It is close to Dominion on camera, genre and setting (a third-person sci-fi co-op
PvE shooter) and far on format. Read section 5 with that in mind.

## 1. In plain words

**The grind is the game, and the players know it.** The top complaint by far is that it is too grindy —
**264 bullets, 8.2 per 100 reviews**, flat across all thirteen years (6.8 to 9.6). **223 of the 264 sit
under a thumbs up (84%).** The grind is named as the cost of a game people keep recommending. A reviewer
at 1,131 hours (200 found it helpful) calls it very grindy, especially for new players, and says *"Money
is purely for cosmetics. (Or to reduce the grind out of some stuff)"*. The thumbs-down at 6,720 hours
(456 found it helpful) names *"an extremely heavy grind and a general disrespect to the player's time"*
— and still lists great visuals.

**Free-to-play is read as fair, not as a trap.** A fair price is said **198** times (the third most
common thing said), everything can be earned by playing **133**, a fair business model 37, the shop
sells only looks 35, players buy things to support the studio 30, the premium currency can be earned by
playing 22, and the player market works 46. Against them: what you can own is capped unless you pay 21,
paying changes how strong you are 16, you end up paying sooner or later 15, the shop charges far too much
12, it feels like a cash grab 10. **Praise of how it makes money outnumbers complaint 134 to 103**, and
the biggest single remark about money is a neutral one: **paying only shortens the grind, 73** — money
buys time, not power.

**The pull is praised and feared in the same breath.** "It keeps pulling you back" is **221**; a warning
that it takes over your life is **74 bullets in 73 reviews, and 60 of the 73 are thumbs up.**
*"Relationships are temporary. The Void's embrace lasts forever."* (131 found it helpful). *"You never
quit warframe, just take longer breaks"* — interest that comes and goes in waves is **44**. Nine more say
they keep playing while hating it. **Those three were said of no other game when it was read** (section
6).

**The first hours are the weak point, and it never goes away.** 267 bullets are about learning the game:
*overwhelming at first* 72, *requires outside research* 43, *poorly explained* 42, *slow start* 36,
against *rewarding once learned* 34 and *easy to start* 15. **A reviewer at 1,389 hours jokes they are
almost done with the tutorial.**

**Most complaints come from people who still play.** **983 of the 1,646 complaints (59.7%) come from
reviews that recommend the game.** The thumbs-down reviews are not newcomers: they show a median of 62
hours, and 66 of the 325 show 1,000 hours or more.

**The thumb barely moves in thirteen years.** 85.4% in the worst year (2017) and 92.9% in the best (2024
and 2026), while the average review shrank from 67 words to 28.

**What it does not have is a technical problem.** Engineering is **3.9%** of everything said; repeated
crashes are 4 bullets in 3,233 reviews, and "runs on modest hardware" (29) outnumbers "needs a strong
PC" (12).

## 3.1 ⭐ The grind: the complaint that everyone makes and almost nobody leaves over

**Four modes describe the same loop.**

| Mode | | Bullets | Under a thumbs up |
|---|---|---|---|
| `unlock-pace.grindy` | − | 264 | 223 (84%) |
| `unlock-pace.grind-feels-earned` | + | 111 | — |
| `monetisation-practice.paying-only-shortens-the-grind` | ~ | 73 | — |
| `replayability.warns-that-it-takes-over-your-life` | − | 74 | 60 of 73 reviews (82%) |

**The grind is filed as a complaint and written as a condition.** At 1,131 hours (up, 200 found it
helpful): very grindy, especially when new, and new content needs a lot of resources. At 60 hours
(down): *"my progress felt so slow"*. At 1,917 hours (down): *"I shouldn't consider your game a damn
second job."* The thumbs-down grind reviews are not new players who bounced: **the 41 of them have a
median of 194 hours, and 11 show over 1,000.** (The page gives no review ids for these quotes.)

**Money is read as a way to skip the wait, not to win.** The 73 *paying only shortens the grind* lines
run from boosters that raise the amount gained but not the drop odds, so rare stays rare (up), to
the moment *"you do not want to spend another week farming the same one type of enemy, boss, or
mission"* and pay (down) — the same fact, a different verdict. **The monetisation fight in this game is
about time.**

**Time gates are the sharpest edge of it.** Waiting on real-world time is 49 — crafting timers of 12
hours to 3 days — and 3 bullets take the other side (the timers do not get in the way), among them
234735996 at 704 hours: *"Crafting times help me pace rather than blowing through everything in a few
months."*

**The loop is praised as a habit in words that are also a warning.** 221 keeps pulling you back, 74
warns that it takes over your life, 44 interest comes and goes in waves, 9 keeps playing while hating it.
*"You never quit warframe, just take longer breaks"* appears, in some form, in three reviews. **The last
three modes appeared in no other game in the queue when Warframe was read.** Risk of Rain 2's addiction
words (*crack*, *my wife left*) sat on the praise side; Warframe's reviewers write the same words as a
warning (*"Relationships are temporary. The Void's embrace lasts forever."*, 131 found it helpful). **A
game with no end to its loop gets reviewed for what it costs the player's life, and the review still
recommends it** (`cross-game.md` section 20).

**The corpus's "flat line".** The cross-game page names five shapes of complaint line — the
relationship (Helldivers 2), the price (Space Marine 2), the reviewer (Remnant II), the owner (Risk of
Rain 2) — and **the flat line (Warframe)**: a complaint built into the design and accepted by the people
who stay. A complaint can be the most common thing said about a game and still not be a reason to leave
it, when the reviewer also says everything can be earned (133), the grind pays off (111) and the price is
fair (198).

**Checked against a primary source:** nothing; no patch notes were pulled. Timer lengths and booster
rules are the reviewers' account.

## 3.2 ⭐ A free game its players call fair

| Side | Modes | Bullets |
|---|---|---|
| **Fair** | `price.fair` 198, `everything-earnable` 133, `a-fair-business-model` 37, `cosmetic-only` 35, `players-buy-in-to-support-the-studio` 30, `currency-earnable-by-playing` 22, `the-player-market-works` 46 | **501** |
| **Neutral** | `paying-only-shortens-the-grind` 73, `advises-what-not-to-spend-on` 22, `monetisation-practice.unknown` 16 | **111** |
| **Unfair** | `what-you-can-own-is-capped-unless-you-pay` 21, `pay-affects-play` 16, `you-end-up-paying-sooner-or-later` 15, `the-shop-charges-far-too-much` 12, `feels-like-a-cash-grab` 10, others | **103** in `monetisation-practice` |

**The player market is what makes the premium currency earnable, and the reviews say so.** One
reviewer says the studio's platinum prices are very high but player-set prices are rock bottom; another
that *"you can even earn the platinum used to buy things in the market through trading in game items
with players."* The premium currency can be traded between players, so a free player can buy what the
shop sells by selling what they farm. **46 bullets praise the market; 6 say it works badly; 1 names
scammers.**

**The complaints are about slots and prices, not power.** The biggest unfair mode is *what you can own
is capped unless you pay* (inventory slots for frames and weapons, 21) — and the neutral *advises what
not to spend on* (22) includes the same advice 8 times — *"Spend your free plat on slots"*. Paying
changes how strong you are in 16 of 3,233 reviews.

**Buying in to support the studio is 30** — reviewers who buy something they do not need *to pay for
what they get out of the game*. 0.93 per 100 reviews, the highest rate in the queue (The Anacrusis
0.82, Arcrunner 0.56).

**This is the one part of this page that describes a free-to-play model, and Dominion is not
free-to-play.** It is recorded here because it is most of what the reviews say.

**Checked against a primary source:** nothing; prices and shop rules are the reviewers' account.

## 4. What the sample says about the design itself

- **Co-op and online play.** **It is a co-op game that works alone.** Much better with friends 114, works
  solo 42 against too hard alone 11 (9 of them before 2019) and needs a group 9. The co-op praise is
  about company more than mechanics: all co-op design remarks together are 21 bullets, and 3 of those
  complain that story quests must be played alone. From the tag grep: rewards teamwork 7; fine with
  strangers 4, poor with strangers 4; **the host leaving ends everyone's run 4; peer-to-peer rather than
  dedicated servers 3**; lag and out-of-sync play 4; high latency 3; cannot connect 3; frequent
  disconnects 2; the reviewer's router blocks play 2; loot is shared 2; more players makes it trivial 1;
  cross-save works 9 against no cross-save 8; no crossplay at all 1. One mode has no players 4; the
  population is healthy 5. **Not raised as a pattern:** matchmaking, server trouble, cheating. **Bots,
  friendly fire, joining in progress, scaling to player count: nothing said** beyond the single bullets
  above.
- **Combat and feel.** Combat hits hard 113, feeling hugely powerful 34; each frame plays its own way 53;
  builds deep and varied 118 (7.8 per 100 in 2013–15). From the tag grep: weightless 1, projectiles too
  slow to hit 1. Overall the game is described through its progression more than its combat.
- **Movement and controls.** **Movement is praised as a skill** (69, 17 naming parkour, bullet jumping or
  the space ninja). Two bullets say the levels do not suit the movement. From the tag grep: movement
  responsive 14, unreliable 4; controls responsive and clear 14; the default layout is awkward 5; take
  hours to get used to 3; no choice of camera view 2; single bullets on a controller that does not work,
  rebinding that broke the controls, and the Steam overlay shortcut clashing.
- **Enemies, bosses and difficulty.** The sample says little here. Too hard alone 11 (mostly before 2019);
  a much stronger player kills everything first 3; new players need carrying 12. From the tag grep: too
  easy 8; good enemy variety 7, lacking 2; one-hit kills 4; harder settings only change the numbers 3;
  poor enemy behaviour 3; bosses are a chore 2; the end game demands the best-known build 2. The end game:
  nothing left to chase 27; the end game is only more unlocks 5; you grind for gear you will never use 12.
  One grind quote names farming *"the same one type of enemy, boss, or mission"*.
- **Progression, loot and randomness.** **Unlocking is a third of all game-design talk**: 754 bullets
  (320 praise, 421 complaint). The grind loop from four sides (section 3.1). Everything earnable 133;
  premium currency earnable by playing 22. Real-world timers 49 against 3 who say they help pacing. The
  part you need may never drop 39; boosters raise how much you get, not the drop odds. **The new-player
  problem is thirteen years old and named in every period**: 267 bullets on learning the game. The
  reviews' own fix is to teach: *"ask for help and guidance as often as you can early on"*, *"Set your
  matchmaking to solo for the first few missions"*, *"Don't feel dumb looking up plot summaries of
  missions after you beat them."* **In the reviews, the teaching is done by other players.** Returning
  players get no catch-up 5 (tag grep). Inventory caps: 21; *"Spend your free plat on slots"* 8 times.
- **Runs, content and replay.** **There are no runs** with a start and an end; the game is an open loop
  of missions. Keeps pulling you back 221; interest comes in waves 44; keeps playing while hating it 9;
  takes over your life 74. Plenty of content 142 (peak 6.0 per 100 in 2019–21); too much of everything
  11; repetitive 56, falling for real after 2019 as more to do than fighting (23) grew; a mode not worth
  playing 33.
- **Money and price.** Section 3.2: fair 501, neutral 111, unfair 103; praise outnumbers complaint 134 to
  103; paying only shortens the grind 73; buying to support the studio 30 (0.93 per 100, the highest in
  the corpus). Dominion is not free-to-play, so this is mostly context.
- **Tech.** Engineering is 3.9% of everything said (253 bullets). Crashes repeatedly: 4 in 3,233 reviews.
  Buggy 33; will not start at all 13; needs a strong PC 12 against runs on modest hardware 29; locked out
  of my own account 11. From the tag grep: well optimised 17; bugs break play 8; rare and minor 7; each
  update brings new bugs 8; known bugs never fixed 5; banned with no reason given 4; huge install size 3.
  6 reviewers say the hours Steam shows are not their real time played. The engine is not named.
- **The studio and the community.** **The community is praised far more than it is blamed.** Welcoming
  community 122, welcoming to newcomers 13, making friends and strangers who became friends in single
  figures, against veterans will not help newcomers 3, a strong player kills everything first 3 and the
  chat filter banning for harmless words 6. **Welcoming is the game's ninth most common tag.** Updates keep
  coming 117; a patch made it better 51; the developers listen 43; against a patch made it worse 27 and a
  patch weakened what players liked 14. Heavy-handed support 9. Live-service problems seen only here, with
  counts from the script and the tag grep: returning players get no catch-up 5, the early game was left
  behind 2, the interface keeps changing 2, the lore was rewritten 1. The story is a real draw: worth
  following 119, against thin 22.
- **What players asked for.** The page gives no list of requests; these are the wishes behind the
  complaints, with their counts. Less grind, or a faster way through it (264); better teaching in the game
  (267 bullets on learning it); no or shorter crafting timers (49); more variety (repetitive 56; modes
  worth playing 33); something to chase at the end (27; more than unlocks 5); more slots without paying
  (21); story quests playable in co-op (3); gentler moderation and support (chat filter 6, heavy-handed
  support 9); patches that do not weaken favourites (14); cross-save (8) and another platform (2).

## 5. For Dominion — our reading

**These are suggestions drawn from what players said, not something the reviews said.** Dominion is a
third-person, four-player co-op sci-fi PvE extraction and arena shooter built on runs, hosted on one
player's machine (listen server), in Unreal Engine 5.

**With Rico's stop reason in mind:** the lines that carry across are the ones not tied to free-to-play —
**a grind is tolerated when everything can be earned and the reward is visible; a tutorial that lags the
systems is a complaint for years; a co-op game praised for its company also has to work alone; movement
that rewards practice is named by name.** The run-based, extraction and arena questions Dominion needs
answered are not in this sample, because the game has none of those.

1. **A long grind is accepted when everything can be earned and the pay-off is visible.** Evidence: too
   grindy 264, but 223 of them thumbs up (84%); everything can be earned 133; the grind is worth it 111;
   the thumb flat at 87.9% to 91.4% for thirteen years. Dominion could: make all between-run unlocks
   earnable in play, and show clear progress bars toward each one. Strength: **strong**.
2. **Respect the player's time, even for fans.** Evidence: a thumbs-down at 6,720 hours with 456 helpful
   votes names *"a general disrespect to the player's time"*; *"a damn second job"* (1,917 hours); the 41
   thumbs-down grind reviews have a median of 194 hours. Dominion could: cap how long any single unlock
   takes, and avoid farming the same mission over and over for one part. Strength: **strong**.
3. **Teach the game inside the game; a tutorial that lags the systems is a complaint for years.**
   Evidence: 267 remarks on learning it; overwhelming 72, outside research 43, poorly explained 42, slow
   start 36; peak in 2016-18; players do the teaching instead. Dominion could: explain each system when it
   first appears, in plain words, and test with new players at every big update. Strength: **strong**.
4. **Avoid real-world timers.** Evidence: 49 complaints (12 hours to 3 days) against 3 who like them.
   Dominion could: tie unlocks to play, not to clock time. Strength: **medium**.
5. **Random drops need a limit on bad luck.** Evidence: the part you need may never drop 39; boosters do
   not change the odds; gear you will never use 12. Dominion could: add bad-luck protection or a way to
   craft or buy the missing part. Strength: **medium**.
6. **Give the end game something to chase besides more unlocks.** Evidence: nothing left to chase 27; the
   end game is only more unlocks 5. Dominion could: plan harder run types, high-stakes extraction or
   rotating challenges for players who have unlocked most things. Strength: **medium**.
7. **Movement that rewards practice gets praised by name.** Evidence: 69 times, 17 naming parkour or
   bullet jumping; 2 say the levels do not suit the movement. This is a third-person game, like Dominion.
   Dominion could: give its movement a skill ceiling (chained slides, jumps, dodges), and build arenas
   that reward it. Strength: **medium to strong**.
8. **Combat weight and feeling strong are core praise.** Evidence: combat hits hard 113; feeling
   overpowered 34; each frame plays its own way 53. Dominion could: give each class a clearly different
   way to play and strong hit feedback. Strength: **medium**.
9. **A co-op game praised for its company still has to work alone.** Evidence: much better with friends
   114; works solo 42 against too hard alone 11 and nobody to play with 9; story quests solo-only drew 3
   complaints. Dominion could: let every mode be played solo or in a group, and never lock story to one.
   Strength: **medium**.
10. **A welcoming community can be designed for.** Evidence: welcoming community 122 (ninth most common);
    teaching done by other players; against: new players need carrying 12, a stronger player kills
    everything first 3, veterans will not help 3. Dominion could: reward veterans for helping newcomers
    (bonus loot for carrying a new player), and stop very strong players from emptying a newcomer's run.
    Strength: **medium**.
11. **If anything is sold, sell time or looks, never power.** Evidence: paying only shortens the grind
    73; looks only 35; paying affects play just 16; buying to support the studio 30, the highest in the
    corpus. Dominion is not free-to-play, but any cosmetic store or DLC should follow this. Strength:
    **medium** (different price model).
12. **Do not cap what players can own behind payment.** Evidence: capped unless you pay 21; the most
    repeated spending tip is *"Spend your free plat on slots"* (8 times). Dominion could: give a generous
    stash and loadout space from the start. Strength: **weak to medium**.
13. **The pull is praised, but it can be read as harm.** Evidence: keeps pulling you back 221; takes over
    your life 74 (60 of 73 still thumbs up); keeps playing while hating it 9; interest comes in waves 44.
    Dominion could: make runs with natural stopping points, and welcome back players who take a break.
    Strength: **medium**.
14. **Bring returning players back up to speed.** Evidence: the page names "returning players with no
    catch-up" and "the early game left behind" as live-service problems seen only here (5 and 2 bullets on
    this rebuild's script run). Dominion could: plan catch-up for players who return after a season.
    Strength: **weak**.
15. **Variety beyond fighting cuts "repetitive".** Evidence: repetitive fell from 3.3 to 0.4-1.0 per 100
    after 2019, while "more to do than fighting" (23) grew. Dominion could: mix objectives inside runs
    (carry, defend, hack, escort) beside the fights. Strength: **medium**.
16. **Every mode must be worth playing.** Evidence: a mode is not worth playing 33 (peak 2.2 per 100 in
    2016-18). Dominion could: ship fewer modes, each one tested for fun. Strength: **medium**.
17. **Too many systems works against the game.** Evidence: too much of everything 11; overwhelming at
    first 72. Dominion could: keep the number of currencies and systems small. Strength: **weak**.
18. **Steady updates help; patches that weaken favourites hurt.** Evidence: updates keep coming 117;
    developers listen 43; a patch made it better 51 against made it worse 27 and weakened favourites 14.
    Dominion could: when a weapon or build must be weakened, explain why and offer something back.
    Strength: **medium**.
19. **A good-looking game ages.** Evidence: "it looks great" fell from 5.9 to 0.9 per 100, six-fold, far
    more than the fall in review length. Dominion could: plan visual refreshes for a long life, and lean on
    art direction more than raw detail. Strength: **weak**.
20. **Story can be a draw even in a looter.** Evidence: worth following 119 against thin 22. Dominion
    could: tell its story between runs, not by forcing solo story missions. Strength: **weak to medium**
    (a looter with a long-told story; Dominion's story needs are not tested here).
21. **Solid tech is noticed by its absence of complaints.** Evidence: crashes 4 in 3,233; runs on modest
    hardware 29 against needs a strong PC 12; but will not start 13 and locked out of account 11. Dominion
    could: keep minimum specs modest and make sign-in and account recovery simple. Strength: **weak to
    medium**.
22. **Moderation that is too strict costs goodwill.** Evidence: the chat filter bans for harmless words 6;
    heavy-handed support 9. Dominion could: if it has chat, filter rather than ban, and give a human
    appeal. Strength: **weak**.
23. **Trading between players can make rare things reachable.** Evidence: the player market works 46,
    works badly 6, scammers 1. Dominion could: consider letting teammates trade or hand over drops inside a
    run. Strength: **weak** (a free-to-play market is not Dominion's model).

## 7. Confidence and limits

- **The sample is 1.07% of the population.** Whole-sample shares carry ±2.16%. Each period (480 to 720
  reviews) carries roughly ±3.7 to ±4.5; any single month is direction only (weights up to ×591).
- **Reviews shrank from 67 words to 28 on average.** Per-100 rates across periods mostly measure that.
  Only changes much larger than the fall in length (repetitive, looks great) are read as real above.
- **581 reviews (18.0%) were edited after creation and are counted on the created date.** The flattening
  rule is open with Rico.
- **Two reading sessions.** Batches 1–14 (700 reviews, 2013-03 to 2016-02) were read before the stop on
  2026-09-24; batches 15–64 after. The filing habits of the two may differ; "best of its kind" (section
  2) is the one place the tables suggest it.
- **The launch is barely covered.** 5 reviews from 2013-03 (of 460). Nothing here describes the open
  beta's first weeks.
- **No patch notes were pulled.** Update names and dates come from the reviews; so do prices, timer
  lengths, booster rules and bans.
- **Hours are the hours shown on the review**, which may be total hours at the time of the pull rather
  than at the time of writing; 6 bullets say the Steam hours are not the reviewer's real time.
- **1,382 bullets (21.2%) said nothing specific.**
- **About 381,000 reviews in other languages** were not pulled; 41 English-tagged reviews carry the
  other-language tag.
- **The game has no runs, no extraction and no arenas**, so it cannot answer the questions Dominion most
  needs answered. It is free-to-play, unlike Dominion.
- **Slurs, jabs and personal details:** none are repeated on this page; the relationship jokes quoted in
  section 2 are kept as jokes, with no names.
