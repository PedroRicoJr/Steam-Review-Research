# Gunfire Reborn — English

**A sample: 1,884 of 43,869 English reviews read, ±2.51% on any whole-sample share.** The pull
(2026-09-27) found 43,869 English reviews across 77 months; Steam's own English count on 2026-09-28
was 43,871. Figures are as of 2026-09-28.

**A co-op (1–4 players) PvE roguelite shooter in first person, on Steam in Early Access from 2020-05 and
released 2021-11-17** (Steam's release date). You play one of a cast of animal heroes; each run is a
string of floors, and your build grows inside the run. It is a paid game with paid DLC characters and
free seasons that stay playable after they end, and it also came out on console. The pages do not name
the engine or the studio. 103,744 reviews in all languages on 2026-09-28, 93% positive, *Very
Positive*. **It is A18 on `planning/action-roguelike-list.md`, picked by the active loop as the next
closest to Dominion (round 501).** It shares Dominion's four-player PvE runs, builds that grow inside a
run, and a live-service season model. It differs in the camera (first person) and has no extraction and
no PvP. Three reviewers say the studio moved play sessions off players' own machines and onto its own
servers, the opposite way from Dominion's listen server.

## 1. In plain words

**It is a game people play with friends.** "Much better with friends" was said **181 times**, more than
any other specific thing. Ten reviews say it is how they play with family. Yet it also stands alone: 56
times players say it works fine solo, against **14** who say it is too hard alone.

**Players explain it by naming other games.** That happened **147 times**, the third most common thing
said. **114 of 1,878 reviews name Borderlands (4 of them thumbs down)** and **57 name Risk of Rain
(1 down)**. The usual line is "Borderlands as a roguelike". Roboquest is named in 9 reviews, all thumbs
up, usually as the closest rival.

**What they praise is the build that grows during a run.** Deep and varied builds were praised **110**
times, the pull to play again **106**, a fair price **100**, the cast of animal heroes **79**, and the
steady flow of updates **73**.

**The top complaint is the English.** **36 reviews** say the English text reads badly, the most common
complaint, and one more finds text left in Chinese. Three others find the odd wording charming.

**The thumb slides, and the complaints double.** The share of thumbs up falls from **97.9% in Early
Access to 88.8% in 2025–26**, and complaints rise from **20.9 to 46.3 per 100 reviews** (section 3).
The rise comes from bad translation, slow movement and too few players to match with — not from the
core loop.

**Most complaints come from people who still like it.** **387 of the 621 complaints (62.3%) come from
reviews that recommend the game.** The 114 thumbs-down reviews show a median of 13 hours played, against
26 for all reviews.

**Recent down votes are about things outside the game.** The most-helpful thumbs-down review in the
sample (91 people found it helpful) is about a clause added to the user agreement. Five reviews from
2026-05 to 2026-09 are thumbs down over AI-made art, and six complain that the console version was left
without updates.

## 3.1 ⭐ The late slide: translation, movement and an empty lobby

**Complaints per 100 reviews more than double, from 20.9 in Early Access to 46.3 in 2025–26.** Three
clusters carry most of the rise. Counts are reviews, by the month each review is counted in:

| Cluster | Tags | EA (569) | 1.0–2022 (411) | 2023–24 (479) | 2025–26 (419) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Translation | `reads-badly`, `translation-quality.partial` | 7 | 2 | 8 | **20** |
| Movement | `movement.sluggish`, `no-modern-moves` | 3 | 2 | 3 | **14** |
| Too few players | `slow-to-find-games`, `cannot-find-games`, `dead-game`, `the-numbers-are-falling` | 2 | 3 | 8 | **10** |

- **Translation.** New content keeps arriving with its own English, and the later reviews say the new
  text is the worst: *"the translation for some of the free update stuff is pretty shockingly subpar at
  times, though the most recent season has been much improved"* (220774330). One says *"every single item
  in the game [is] at least 3 lines long"*, and that player-made translation packs mostly cover only the
  base game (230540835). **Bad English is 1.9 per 100 here, ten times the next game** of 300 or more
  reviews in the corpus (Escape from Duckov 0.2, Terminull Brigade 0.1, Back 4 Blood 0.1; four small
  groups under 120 reviews each have one review on it, 0.9 to 1.3 per 100; `cross-game.md` section 22).
- **Movement.** Slow movement is 15 and missing modern moves 9 — together **the most repeated complaint
  about how the game feels**, and 14 of the 22 reviews on movement are from 2025–26. *"You can jump and
  dash, that is it, not even a sprint"* (230540835); *"you move like a snail with a pathetically short
  dash move"*, with no invincibility on the dash (204410221). 230540835 adds a design reading: the slow
  movement is what keeps fairly simple enemies dangerous.
- **Too few players.** **23 reviews** in all. *"your game is dying cause of low player base"*, with a plea
  for deep discounts to bring people in (216633728); *"matchmaking worse than tarkov, not worth playing if
  no friends"* (203253379).

**What a long life shows** (`cross-game.md` section 22): a game read only in its first year would miss
all three late clusters. **Checked against a primary source:** nothing; no patch notes were pulled, so
the update names and the dates of the new season text come from the reviews.

## 4. What the sample says about the design itself

- **Co-op and online play.** Friends carry it: 181 times "much better with friends", 10 reviews play with
  family, 8 bullets praise how it rewards teamwork. It also works alone (56) more than it punishes solo
  players (14). Drop and rejoin: 8 reviews; join in progress: 3; lag and out-of-sync play: 12; worse lag
  after the move to studio servers: 3; too few players to match with: 23 reviews. Loot per player is
  praised (4). Bots: 8 bullets in 6 reviews (section 3.2). Scaling to the number of players: praised 2,
  outpaces a full group 3, one strong player can carry the rest 4, more players makes it trivial 1. A
  dead player has to watch until the next checkpoint: 2. No ping shown before joining: 1. Crossplay skips
  the platform the reviewer bought it on: 1. **Friendly fire: nothing said** (0 bullets in the tag grep).
- **Combat and feel.** Combat hits hard (69) and **breaking the game is the point**: feeling hugely
  powerful is 48 and rises from 1.1 per 100 in Early Access to 4.8 in 2025–26. Every option plays
  differently (24); one option dominates (10); some options are never worth taking (9); something needs a
  nerf (7); one playstyle has too few tools (3). Each hero plays its own way (48). Weak-spot hits that do
  not count as critical hits: 2. Melee hits that do not land: 1.
- **Movement and controls.** Slow movement 15 and missing modern moves 9, the most repeated complaint
  about feel, 14 of the 22 in 2025–26 (section 3). Responsive movement is praised 5 times. Controls are
  almost silent: a controller that does not work 1, a missing binding 1, a wish for aim assist 1. The
  camera is first person, so nothing is said about a third-person camera.
- **Enemies, bosses and difficulty.** Satisfyingly hard 48; difficulty levels well spaced 24. The cost of
  feeling powerful: the power outgrows the challenge and some find it too easy (7), while the top
  difficulty brings one-shot deaths (10, rising to 1.2 per 100 in 2025–26). Enemy variety praised 14,
  lacking 8. The bosses are a highlight for 14; solo bosses were *"two hit deaths"* for one player until
  bots came (192796690). Single bullets on enemies that cannot be countered, bullet sponges, poorly

- **Progression, loot and randomness.** **A guaranteed upgrade every floor keeps runs building.** *"at

- **Runs, content and replay.** It keeps pulling you back (106); "I want more because it is good" (42); a

- **Money and price.** A fair price 100 (5.3 per 100); DLC is fair 24, only worth buying on sale 8.

## 5. For Dominion — our reading

**These are suggestions drawn from what players said, not something the reviews said.** Dominion is a
third-person, four-player co-op sci-fi PvE extraction and arena shooter built on runs, hosted on one
player's machine (listen server), in Unreal Engine 5.

1. **Any upgrade text players read in the middle of a run must be checked at every update, not once.**
   Evidence: bad English is the top complaint, 36 reviews (1.9 per 100, ten times any other game read at
   size), and it grew from 7 reviews in Early Access to 20 in 2025–26 as each season added new text. One
   reviewer adds that item text runs *"at least 3 lines long"* (230540835). The cross-game page draws this
   same lesson. Dominion could: keep upgrade text short (one line where possible), use icons and numbers,
   and make a text check part of every patch, in every language it ships. Strength: **medium** (clear and
   rising, but tied to this game's translation).
2. **Build for friends first, and make solo work too.** Evidence: "much better with friends" 181 times
   (fourth highest in the corpus); works alone 56 times against too hard alone 14. Dominion could: make the
   four-player session easy to start with friends, and tune and test a real solo path (with scaling or
   bots). Strength: **strong**.
3. **Let a player who drops get back into the run.** Evidence: 8 reviews; the second most helpful
   thumbs-down review (71 helpful) is about not being able to reconnect (168616519); one review turned to a
   thumbs up when reconnecting was added (171154399). Dominion could: on a listen server, let a dropped
   client rejoin the same run with their build kept, and decide what happens when the host drops (keep the
   run alive, save it, or hand it over). Strength: **medium**.
4. **Allow joining a run in progress.** Evidence: 3 reviews, one naming Deep Rock Galactic (179561786, 13
   helpful). Dominion could: let friends drop into a running session, at least between rooms or arenas.
   Strength: **weak**.
5. **Hosting changes are felt.** Evidence: 3 reviews say lag got worse after the move to studio servers;
   lag and out-of-sync play 12 reviews. Dominion could: since it hosts on a player's machine, test play on
   weak home connections and show the host's connection quality before a run. Strength: **weak**.
6. **Movement needs a sprint and a real dodge, or players will feel slow.** Evidence: slow movement 15 and
   missing modern moves 9; 14 of the 22 reviews on movement are from 2025–26; *"not even a sprint"*
   (230540835); a dash with no safety window (204410221). The counterpoint, from one reviewer: slow
   movement is what keeps simple enemies dangerous. Dominion could: give a sprint and a dodge with a short
   safety window, and keep enemies threatening by how they attack, not by keeping the player slow.
   Strength: **medium**.
7. **A guaranteed upgrade at fixed points keeps a run building.** Evidence: builds deep and varied 110;
   satisfying progress 55; an upgrade at the end of every floor called the best pacing in the genre
   (235815154). Dominion could: give a sure upgrade after each arena or area, on top of random drops.
   Strength: **strong**.
8. **Let players feel overpowered, but guard the top difficulty against one-shot deaths and boredom.**
   Evidence: feeling overpowered 48 times, rising from 1.1 to 4.8 per 100; one-hit deaths 10 reviews,
   rising to 1.2 per 100 late; too easy 7; power outgrowing the challenge also named. Dominion could: let
   builds get strong, raise challenge with enemy count and behaviour rather than one-shot damage, and flag
   one-shot attacks clearly. Strength: **medium**.
9. **Do not make the first runs feel weak because of between-run upgrades.** Evidence: slow start 22
   reviews (highest in the corpus) and grindy 18. Dominion could: make the first runs fun on their own,
   with meta upgrades adding options more than raw power. Strength: **medium**.
10. **Randomness is liked until the needed piece never comes.** Evidence: keeps runs fresh 47 times; the
    thing you need never shows up 15 reviews; luck decides the outcome 12 reviews. Dominion could: add
    bad-luck protection or a way to steer rolls (rerolls, a shop, a choice of three). Strength: **medium**.
11. **Bots can save solo play, but only if their damage keeps up.** Evidence: said 8 times in 6 reviews;
    "an absolute godsend" (235865471); won back a player who found solo bosses too deadly (192796690); but
    bots deal about an eighth of your damage while enemy health scales (234049844). Dominion could: if it
    adds bots, scale enemies to the humans, not the bots. Strength: **weak**.
12. **Loot each player can keep avoids fights inside the team.** Evidence: 4 reviews praise that nobody
    fights over drops (223278780). Dominion could: give each player their own drops, or share them in a
    clear way. Strength: **weak**.
13. **A small player base kills matchmaking with strangers.** Evidence: 23 reviews on too few players,
    rising from 2 to 10 per period. Dominion could: lean on friend invites and solo play, and not build
    features that need a full public lobby to work. Strength: **medium**.
14. **Seasons that never expire are praised.** Evidence: 6 reviews; *"No FOMO"* (220125902, 545 hours).
    Dominion could: if it runs seasons, keep old ones playable and their rewards earnable later.
    Strength: **weak** (few reviews, but no pushback at all).
15. **Paid heroes that are stronger cost trust.** Evidence: DLC fair 24 times, but 10 reviews say paid
    heroes are stronger or too much is paid; 1 complains that full completion needs paid DLC. Dominion
    could: keep paid content side-grades, not upgrades, and keep achievements reachable in the base game.
    Strength: **weak to medium**.
16. **Trust can be lost outside the game.** Evidence: a user-agreement clause (1 review, 91 helpful, the
    top thumbs-down review); AI-made art (5 thumbs-down reviews, 2026-05 to 2026-09); a console version
    left behind (6 reviews). Dominion could: keep the user agreement plain, say how art is made, and keep
    every platform it ships on updated together. Strength: **medium** (small counts, but high in helpful
    votes and all recent).
17. **A steady stream of updates is the strongest praise for a live game.** Evidence: 73 times, the
    highest rate in the corpus; a patch made it better 29; developers listen 15. Dominion could: plan a
    regular update rhythm before launch. Strength: **strong**.
18. **Watch the complaints over years, not just at launch.** Evidence: complaints went from 20.9 to 46.3
    per 100 and the thumb from 97.9% to 88.8% over six years, while praise held. Dominion could: track
    review complaints by period after launch, so a slow slide shows up early. Strength: **medium**.
19. **A one-line pitch that names other games helps players explain it.** Evidence: explained by naming
    other games 147 times; 114 name Borderlands, 57 Risk of Rain; "Borderlands as a roguelike". Dominion
    could: pick its own "X meets Y" line and use it in marketing. Strength: **medium**.
20. **A memorable cast helps.** Evidence: appealing heroes 79 times; each plays its own way 48. Dominion
    could: make each playable character look and play clearly different. Strength: **medium**.
21. **Weak-spot hits must count.** Evidence: 2 reviews say head or weak-spot hits do not register as
    critical. Dominion could: test weak-spot hit boxes against the art. Strength: **weak**.
22. **Story is not why people play this kind of game.** Evidence: story is mentioned 9 times in 3,350
    remarks. Dominion could: keep story light inside runs and spend effort on the loop. Strength: **weak**
    (absence of talk, not a complaint).
23. **Mod support is asked for.** Evidence: 10 reviews. Dominion could: decide early whether to allow
    mods. Strength: **weak**.

## 7. Confidence and limits

- **The sample is 4.3% of the population.** Whole-sample shares carry ±2.51%; each of the four periods
  carries roughly ±4 to ±5.
- **The first month is the heaviest weight** (2020-05, ×45), because Steam returned only 10 of the 20
  reviews wanted. Weighted shares lean on it. Every month is read at 10 to 115 reviews; a single month is
  direction only.
- **154 reviews (8.2%) were edited after creation and are counted on the created date.** The flattening
  rule is open with Rico.
- **No patch notes were pulled.** Update names, the server move, the reconnect fix, the bots patch, the
  user-agreement clause, the AI art, the console claims and the claim about how the studio treats its
  staff all come from the reviews and were not checked.
- **Hours are the hours shown on the review.** The thumbs-down median was first given as 13; the
  summary-file grep in section 8 gives 12. The page keeps both rather than guess which count was used
  first.
- **The later batches were read in this session, the early ones in earlier sessions.** The tags used for
  movement and population existed throughout, but a reader drift towards naming them more often late on
  cannot be ruled out from the data alone.
- **Reviews are very short** (60% are 15 words or fewer); 773 bullets (23.1%) said nothing specific.
- **24 reviews were written in another language**; they were filed on the language tag, not tagged by
  content. A grep for that tag today finds 12 reviews carrying it; the page does not record how the 24
  were counted.
- **About 60,000 reviews in other languages exist on Steam**; none were pulled.
- **Slurs, jabs and personal details:** none are repeated on this page.
