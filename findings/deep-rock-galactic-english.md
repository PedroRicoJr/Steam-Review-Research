# Deep Rock Galactic — English

**A sample: 2,130 of 215,564 English reviews read (2,133 sampled, 3 of them not reviews of the game),
1.0% of the group; the margin of error is not counted on this page.** The finished read of one group:
one game, one language, every sampled review tagged. When the grid was pulled, and how its month-sum
compares with Steam's own English count on that day, is not recorded on this page.

**The Steam store page calls it *"a 1-4 player co-op FPS"* (a first-person shooter; checked
2026-10-03, as recorded in `DOMINION-TAKEAWAYS.md`).** It is a co-op shooter about fighting waves of
insects in caves, with classes that each have their own job and maps built by the computer so each
mission is different. It came out in early access in February 2018 — the patch notes name the
anniversary events *"Seven Years in Orbit"* (Feb 2025) and *"Eight Years in Orbit"* (Mar 2026), which
count from **February 2018**, the first month in this corpus. It is a paid game; new content after
launch has been free (seasons, events), with cosmetic DLC on top (the Skullcrusher DLC, 2025-02, is
named in the patch notes). Season 05 "Drilling Deeper" shipped 2024-06-13 and Season 06 "Relics of
Hoxxes" in 2026-01. A roguelite spin-off, Rogue Core, came later. Developer, publisher, engine, the
1.0 date and who hosts are not given on this page. All languages: five groups were pulled (english,
latam, spanish, russian, schinese); Steam's own thumbs-up figure in the cross-game table is 97.1%, and
the rating is *Overwhelmingly Positive* per `findings/drg-rogue-core.md`. **How close to Dominion
(from `DOMINION-TAKEAWAYS.md`): close in spirit, not in camera.** It shares a sci-fi setting, a small
squad of friends against alien hordes, PvE only, classes that need each other, generated maps, and
four players. It differs in being first-person, in using missions rather than runs that build power,
and in having no player-versus-player risk. It is the corpus's model of a loved co-op game.

## 1. In plain words

**Almost nobody dislikes this game, and the reviews are mostly not about the game.** 97.1% of the
sampled reviews are thumbs up: 61 people out of 2,133 gave it a thumbs down. Of 4,511 things players
said, 3,744 are good and 515 are bad — good outnumbers bad **7 to 1**.

**The single most common thing a player writes is the community's catchphrase** — 610 times, 14% of
everything said. It is more common than every remark about the game's design. The people — friends,
kind strangers, a shared culture — are 28.8% of everything said, the second-largest group after the
game's design at 30.3%. **For a co-op shooter, the people are half the product.**

**The top praise is about who you play with.** After the catchphrase, players most often say it is
much better with friends (215), they call themselves by the game's own name for its players (141), and
strangers are kind (123). The first thing about the game itself — it keeps pulling you back (121) —
comes fifth. Silly actions with no effect on play, like dancing and drinking in the bar, are praised
111 times, more than the enemies or the levels.

**The top complaint is the same thing seen from the other side: you need people, and without them
the game thins out.** The top four complaints are: it gets repetitive (29), it is hard alone (27), you
need a group (22), and there is too little of it (20). Three of those four are about having no group.
The game's best feature and its main complaint are one property read from two sides.

**The 61 players who left agree with each other, and none of them names a defect.** No crash, no
broken servers, no price trick and no studio misconduct reaches the top complaints. Every one of them
is a trade-off the game chose: built around a group, around repeating missions, and around a long
unlock road.

**It changed over time: complaints fell for five years and then rose again.** Complaints per 100
reviews went from 55.5 in 2018 to 10.3 in 2023 and back up to 27.1 in 2026, while thumbs down stayed
under 5%. Reviews also got shorter: by 2023 three in four reviews said one thing, and a quarter of
everything written was the catchphrase. Review numbers on Steam peaked in 2023 and fell every year
after, while the studio kept shipping updates.

**The one surprise: the store's calendar moves reviews more than the studio does.** November — the
Steam autumn sale and the Steam Awards — is the biggest month in seven years out of eight. Only one
full new season ever beat it. The only shipped feature that visibly moved what players wrote was
letting them go back and earn old seasons' rewards.

## 3. ⭐ The complaints are what the praise costs

**Put the two lists side by side and four pairs sit on the same axis, pointing opposite ways.**

| The complaint | Count | The praise it is the cost of | Count |
|---|---|---|---|
| `content-variety.repetitive` | 29 | `procedurally-varied` + `varied-runs` | **115** |
| `solo-viability.punishing-solo` | 27 | `solo-viability.works-solo` | **79** |
| `playing-with-friends.needs-a-group` | 22 | `much-better-with-friends` | **215** |
| `progression.unlock-pace.grindy` | 15 | `satisfying-progression` | **40** |

**Each pair is one design decision, seen from both ends.** The game is built to be played with a
group, on procedurally built maps, with a long unlock road. **The people who like that say so 449
times. The people who do not say so 93 times.** The 61 negative reviews are worth naming, because they
agree with each other: **three of the top four complaints are the same complaint — you need people,
and without them the game thins out.** That is the exact opposite of the top positive mode,
`much-better-with-friends` (215).

The cross-game read finds the same pairs in every game: `content-variety.repetitive` is the tightest
band in the whole corpus — 1.8, 1.4 and 1.4 per 100 in Back 4 Blood, this game and Helldivers 2
(`cross-game.md` section 2). **A beloved game, a hated game and a contested game complain about it at
the same rate.**

### What the patch notes do not explain

- **The November peaks.** Neither November in the window shipped content — 2024-11 was a dev

- **The decline after 2023.** Reviews fall from 51,740 to 7,586 across three years while the studio

the storefront calendar out-moves all of it in seven years out of eight.** That is a finding about how

## 5. For Dominion — our reading

**These are suggestions drawn from what players said, not something the reviews said.** Dominion is a
third-person, four-player co-op sci-fi PvE extraction and arena shooter built on runs, hosted on one
player's machine (listen server), in Unreal Engine 5.

1. **Make the game great with friends, and still fine alone and with strangers.** Evidence: better
   with friends 215, fine alone 79, good with strangers 41 to 4; and on the other side hard alone 27,
   needs a group 22 — three of the top four complaints are about having no group. Dominion could: tune
   solo and duo runs so they work (scale enemies by player count), make matchmade strangers useful to
   each other, and give a solo player a reason to queue with others. Strength: **strong**.
2. **Give players a shared ritual — a salute, a phrase, a gesture — and let them own it.** Evidence:
   the catchphrase 610 times (28.6 per 100), identity 141; Back 4 Blood, which failed, has 0.0. But
   Rogue Core, with the same phrase, gets only 7.5 per 100: a ritual cannot be pasted in. Dominion
   could: put a salute or call-out button and a voice line in from day one, tie it to a moment that
   happens every run (the drop-in, the extraction), and see if players pick it up rather than forcing
   it. Strength: **medium** (strong count, but the cause is unclear; one failure against two survivors).
3. **Build cheap toys with no game effect into the hub and the run.** Evidence: dancing, the bar,
   petting the loot bug, the barrel — 111 times, more than enemies (75) and levels (20). Dominion could:
   give the pre-run hub a drink, a dance, a kickable object, a pettable creature, a lobby mini-game —
   things friends do while waiting for the host. Strength: **strong**.
4. **Give each class a job the others cannot do.** Evidence: every class needed 98; teamwork required
   36; one class weaker 6; a class missing 6. Dominion could: design classes around a team need
   (lighting, mobility, defence, resupply) rather than damage flavours, and check none falls behind.
   Strength: **strong**.
5. **Fight repetition with generated maps, but expect it to stay the top complaint.** Evidence:
   repetitive 29 against varied 115; the same rate (about 1.4 per 100) in every game. Dominion could:
   vary objectives, modifiers and map pieces between runs, and plan a content pipeline for players who
   run out (too little 20, runs out fast 9, nothing left to chase 6). Strength: **strong**.
6. **Make every run fun with nothing to earn; do not lean only on unlocks.** Evidence: Rico's note on
   reviewer `85232716` (no count) and the pull-you-back praise 121; grind complaint 15, nothing left to
   chase 6. Dominion could: judge each run by whether it is fun at max level with all unlocks done.
   Strength: **weak** on numbers, but Rico agrees with it.
7. **Let missed time-limited rewards come back.** Evidence: the old-season feature is the only shipped
   feature in the corpus that moved a count (4 of 9 thanks in the eight months after it shipped).
   Dominion could: never lock seasonal or event rewards away for good; let players switch to old reward
   tracks. Strength: **medium** (small count, clear link).
8. **Steady, free updates buy trust that lasts years.** Evidence: steady updates 69, studio earned
   trust 63, listens and acts 39, free post-launch content 1.5 per 100; price and sales are only 2.7% of
   complaints. Dominion could: plan a regular free update rhythm and sell only cosmetics on top, and say
   so. Strength: **strong**.
9. **A clear difficulty ladder the player picks for themselves.** Evidence: well-graded difficulty 42;
   hard in a good way 1.2 per 100. Dominion could: offer named difficulty steps per run, each clearly
   harder, chosen at the start. Strength: **medium**.
10. **Let players say what they want from a run before it starts.** Evidence: the 1,790-hour reviewer
    (`178648003`) and Rico's note: some want fun, some rewards, some clears. Six complain of trolls.
    Dominion could: start with Discord channels or a role picker (Rico's free version); later a lobby
    flag such as "casual / full sweep / fast clear". Strength: **weak** (one review, Rico's own view).
11. **A shared joke in the tone helps the culture form.** Evidence: the satire lands 68; it is 3.2 per
    100 here and 3.3 in Helldivers 2, 0.0 in Back 4 Blood. Dominion could: give the corporation or the
    war a voice players can laugh at and quote. Strength: **medium**.
12. **Make strangers good for each other.** Evidence: strangers good 41 to 4; welcoming community 123;
    no loot stealing (0.0 per 100 here vs 5.8 in Rogue Core with shared pickups). Dominion could: give
    each player their own loot or a fair split, reward reviving and helping, and avoid systems where one
    player's gain is another's loss. Strength: **medium**.
13. **Read the review text, not only the score, and know Steam's calendar.** Evidence: November sales
    beat every update but one season in eight years; the catchphrase turned reviews into a greeting;
    complaints per 100 doubled in 2025–26 while thumbs-down stayed under 5%. Rogue Core's text moved a
    month before its score. Dominion could: track complaint rates per month, not just the percentage;
    time big updates away from or into the autumn sale on purpose. Strength: **medium**.
14. **Widen the cast.** Evidence: 9 ask for a roster that includes people it leaves out; 6 miss a
    class. Dominion could: let players pick body type, voice and look for their soldier. Strength:
    **weak**.
15. **Think about phobias when designing bugs or swarms.** Evidence: 2 of 10 accessibility remarks are
    about phobia. Dominion could: consider an option that changes the look of the creepiest enemies.
    Strength: **weak**.

## 7. Confidence and limits

1. **A 1.0% sample**, 20 reviews a month at the floor; the margin of error is not counted on this page,
   and single months are rough. Per-year margins are not counted either.
2. **Why review volume collapsed after 2023.** The sample says nothing about players who stopped
   playing, because they stopped writing too.
3. **Anything before 2024-03 about patches.** Steam capped the news feed at 100 items.
4. **What non-English players think.** Four other languages for this game are pulled and unread —
   `latam`, `spanish`, `russian`, `schinese` — 112,943 reviews on Steam: Russian (65,935), Simplified
   Chinese (36,562), LatAm Spanish (2,138), European Spanish (8,308). Four reviews in the English group
   are written in another language.
5. **Whether the 61 negative reviewers are representative.** 61 is a small number and they cluster on
   one theme; that is a pattern, not a population estimate.
6. **Accessibility.** The old page counted 9 bullets and said the tree had no phobia branch, and that
   two observations that needed one were filed elsewhere (gap 4 in `tag-tree-open-gaps.md`). **Round
   111 built the branch on Rico's ruling; the count is now 10, two of them phobia.**
7. **Edited reviews.** 286 (13.4%) carry a later edit and are counted on the day they were created.
8. **Not checked against a primary source:** anything a reviewer says about prices, events or the
   studio. The patch dates in section 3 are from the studio's own patch notes in Steam's news feed.
9. **Small mismatches between counts** (from `DOMINION-TAKEAWAYS.md`): thumbs up is 2,072 of 2,133 on
   the first count and 2,069 of 2,130 with the excluded files left out (both 97.1%); complaints per 100
   are 24.1 in the early cross-game table and 23.7 in the later recount; accessibility is 10 remarks in
   one place and 9 in another; the bullet total is 4,511 at round 111, 4,514 in the early cross-game
   table and 4,519 in today's script run.
10. **Slurs and personal details:** none is quoted on this page.
