# The Anacrusis — English findings

**611 reviews read of 611 pulled, from an English population of 1,290.** 2,411 bullets, 3.95 per
review, 8% unknown, **0 unfitted, 0 excluded**. Rounds 184–196.

| Measure | Value |
|---|---|
| Sample % positive | **57.45%** |
| Rule 6 weighted % positive | **58.49%** |
| Steam's all-language score | 46.6% (Mixed) |
| Margin of error (finite population, 1,290) | **±2.85%** |
| Months read | 55 of 56 |
| Months at 100% census | **49 of 55** |
| Multi-dated reviews | 125 (20.5%) |

**The sample is nearly a census.** 49 of 55 months were read completely; the shortfall sits in six
months, and two of them carry it — **2022-06 read at 18.6% (64 of 345) and 2023-01 at 26.4%
(79 of 299)**. Those are the early access launch month and the free weekend. Both are months where
the game reached people who had not chosen it, so **the under-read months are the ones most likely to
be more negative than the rest**, and the weighted figure moves *up* rather than down because the
months read in full skew later and harsher.

⚠️ **The 57.45% English figure is not comparable to Steam's 46.6%.** That number is all-language and
lifetime; this is English only. **Do not put them side by side in a summary.**

## 1. The finding: the believers left, and the complaint did not

`production.scope-mismatch.the-potential-is-still-there` is the third-largest mode in the corpus at
**74 bullets**. It is also the clearest time series in it.

| Year | `the-potential-is-still-there` | `community.population.dead-game` |
|---|---|---|
| 2022 | **35** | 8 |
| 2023 | **33** | 14 |
| 2024 | **4** | 15 |
| 2025 | 1 | 5 |
| 2026 | 1 | 5 |

**Sixty-eight of the seventy-four "it could still be great" bullets are from 2022 and 2023.** After
the December 2023 1.0 release the mode all but stops appearing. Nothing replaced it: the 2024–2026
reviews do not argue that the game got worse, they stop arguing that it will get better.

**Meanwhile the complaint that outlasted everything got proportionally louder:**

| Year | weightlessness bullets | share of that year's bullets |
|---|---|---|
| 2022 | 37 of 767 | 4.8% |
| 2023 | 51 of 869 | 5.9% |
| 2024 | 33 of 502 | 6.6% |
| 2025 | 14 of 162 | **8.6%** |

The weightlessness cluster is `combat.weightless` **81** + `effects-and-gore.impacts-look-weak` **32**
+ `sound-effects.weak-or-thin` **24** = **137 of 2,411 (5.7%)**, against `combat.impactful` **21**.
**Six and a half to one, and the ratio widened every year.**

`154491765` states the mechanism that the time series shows: *"They have received complaints about
this since it arrived on Early Access and have done absolutely nothing to rectify it."*

**This is the deep principle for a game-maker.** The corpus is not a game that got worse. It is a
game whose single largest problem was named in the first month, never fixed, and eventually
exhausted the people who were arguing on its behalf. **Optimism has a half-life, and the thing that
kills it is not decline — it is the same complaint surviving a year of patches.**

## 2. The most-repeated complaint is the thing you do most, and one reviewer says it is misdiagnosed

`combat.weightless` at 81 is the corpus' largest single mode. The 30-helpful `131738483` puts it in
proportion: *"this game is 99% shooting and that aspect feels worse in every way than L4D, so I can't
recommend it over that."*

**Only one reviewer in 611 offers a fix list rather than a verdict.** `165340291`:

> add viewbob, do a whole pass on animations, remake Commons from scratch, add bullet penetration on
> pellets / consider replacing hitscan, either implement physics or just death animations (enemies
> just sort of slowly lump on ground, no momentum on ragdolls)

…and then makes the argument the rest of the corpus does not:

> The bad sound design is red herring… Now imagine you've fixed the audio – an audio engineer
> specialist has worked on it and it's amazing now, but that's the only thing that has been fixed.
> **The game would still have a lot more important problems at hand.**

**24 bullets blame the sound and 32 blame the visual impact; he says both are downstream of the same
missing layer** — no hit reaction, no death animation variety, no momentum. That is one claim from
one reviewer and it is the only structural explanation the corpus contains.

## 3. The corpus argues about a different game in one bullet out of nine

**263 of 2,411 bullets (10.9%) name another game.** By comparison, **Redfall's whole run measured
3.8%.**

| Mode | Bullets |
|---|---|
| `.beaten-by-a-competitor` | 77 |
| `.explained-by-naming-other-games` | 71 |
| `.derivative-of-an-older-game` | 63 |
| `.beats-its-rivals` | 34 |
| `.best-in-its-category` | 9 |
| `.unlike-anything-else` | 8 |
| `.beaten-by-games-it-does-not-name` | 1 |

**The split is not one-sided.** 141 bullets say it loses or copies; 51 say it wins or stands alone;
71 use Left 4 Dead purely to say what the game *is*, with no verdict. **A third of the comparison
traffic is description, not judgement** — which is why `.explained-by-naming-other-games` had to
exist as a separate neutral mode.

**`marketing` is 14.4% of this corpus, the highest of any game read so far.** The game is discussed
by reference more than on its own terms, and 10.9 of those 14.4 points are the comparison modes.

## 4. The population is the second story, and the studio made it worse

`community.population.dead-game` **47** · `only-the-studio-chat-fills-a-lobby` **8** ·
`nobody-ever-heard-of-it` **7** · `dead-in-my-region` · `the-numbers-are-falling` = **70 bullets**.

The reviews carry their own measurements, which is rare:

> *"the monthly average players is 3.4 per day and even on a Saturday night it only peaks at 7"* (`157013622`)
> *"the player count displayed a pitiful '1.' Yes, you guessed it, that lone soul was none other than myself."* (`160509575`)
> *"This game had maybe 11 players before the f2p weekend"* (`130514365`)

**And the studio's own communication splits almost evenly, in opposite time periods.**

| | Bullets |
|---|---|
| `listens-and-acts` + `open-about-what-it-is-doing` | **34** |
| `punishes-criticism` + `disputes-the-player-count` + `adversarial` + `ignores-feedback` + others | **28** |

The praise clusters in 2022–2023. **Almost every negative bullet is 2024 or later**, and three
reviewers describe the same sequence independently: the charts show almost nobody, the studio says
the charts are wrong, the studio removes the people who post them. `139919128` quotes his ban notice
in full, issued for linking the game's own SteamDB page, with no reason given. `141244492` records
the end of it: *"the devs have now finally admitted that there is a low player count."*

⚠️ **The Discord is both the praise and the dependency.** Eight bullets say it is the only reliable
way to fill a lobby — including one from a reviewer offering it as friendly advice. **A player who
cannot get along in that server cannot play the game at all**, which makes the social climate a
gameplay requirement here rather than a nicety (gap 120).

## 5. The characters complaint may be a delivery problem, not a writing problem

`narrative.characters-writing.flat-or-annoying` carries **32 bullets**. `166892773` (21 hours) argues
the whole corpus has it backwards:

> I believe some, if not all, voice lines have a specific **"weight" that depicts how often they
> appear** and most of those voice lines characterise the characters best… **you're not gonna get
> this all from just one hour** of playing the game, i'm still learning more about these characters
> after nearly 24 hours

**The corpus can test this claim, and it survives the test:**

| Bullet | n | Median hours | Under 2h |
|---|---|---|---|
| `characters-writing.flat-or-annoying` | 31 | **1** | **52%** |
| `characters-writing.funny-or-memorable` | 9 | **6** | 22% |

**The reviewers who say the cast has no personality played a median of one hour. The reviewers who
say the cast is good played six.** That does not prove the line-weighting mechanism, and it is
exactly the pattern the mechanism predicts.

⚠️ **This matters more than the complaint count.** If the writing exists and the delivery hides it,
the fix is a line-selection change, not a script. **If a studio read only the bullet count it would
rewrite the wrong thing.** Recorded as gap 114; the mode was not built because one reviewer is one
reviewer.

**Context that weakens the corpus generally:** the median playtime across all 611 reviews is **2
hours**, 46.2% are under two hours, and **163 reviews (26.7%) show zero hours — 113 of them
negative.** Steam's counter only sees the Steam copy, and this game shipped into Game Pass, so an
unknown share of those zeroes are people who played it elsewhere.

## 6. Wayfinding is the third complaint, and the game's answer to it is also a complaint

`confusing-layout` **22** + `cannot-spot-what-you-need` + `ui-ux.hides-information` +
`nothing-in-the-place-says-what-happened-here` = **45 bullets**.

The 19-helpful `159128455` names the missing pieces precisely:

> the ship of the Anacrusis is a pristine space-faring vessel overrun with a hostile alien force, and
> yet, there are **no indications of other survivors than yourselves, no gore-splatter, no signage,
> no unpossessed corpses, no hi-vis paint, no HUD element**

**That list is the finding.** The same absent set dressing does two jobs at once: it fails to tell
the story and it fails to show the way out. `156652417` reaches the same place from the narrative
side. **The mode built for it went under `level-design`, not `narrative`, because the fix is props in
the level.**

**And the game's own answer is itself in the corpus.** `130889109`, who complained he could not tell
where to go, then complained about the fix: *"the game will just path you to where you need to go…
this felt more like an annoyance and like the game was treating me like an idiot."*

## 7. What the corpus is positive about

Not everything here is a complaint. **58 bullets praise the art direction** (`looks-well-directed`),
23 the environment art, 30 the workshop, 25 the special enemies, 23 playing with friends.

The clearest positive signal is that **the workshop and the community are the two things nobody
argues about.** `197189537` (110 hours) is the extreme case: *"honestly helped me out with my career
in making custom maps & level design in general! This was one of my first ways of going into Unreal
Engine."*

**And the game's single least-contested feature is the one that covers for the missing players.**
`ai-teammates.takes-over-when-you-step-away` has three sightings and all three are praise — including
one from a reviewer who does not recommend the game: *"It has one good innovation that when you go
afk in co-op session AI takes control of the character."*

## 8. The 2025 reviews are largely not about the game

`review.the-controversy-drove-me-away` reached **4**, and two of them are the highest helpful counts
in their batch — **41 and 45 helpful** — both about the studio lead's public political positions.
Neither describes play.

**A findings pass has to say that plainly rather than fold it into the score.** The corpus' most
visible 2025 reviews are a boycott, and the year's 37.7% positive rate cannot be read as a verdict on
the game without saying so.

`narrative.world-and-setting.politics-put-me-off` carries **3** more, about the game's cosmetic
banners. ⚠️ **The approving half has no tag** — `181915055`'s *"I love the pride banners"* sits
homeless. **Three modes in the tree record a player put off by a game's politics and none records the
opposite. That is a completeness fault in the tree, and it means the corpus reports this argument
one-sidedly** (gap 115).

## 10. What this corpus cannot tell you

1. **Whether the line-weighting claim in §5 is true.** One reviewer, one mechanism, and a playtime
   correlation that is consistent with it. It needs the game, not the reviews.
2. **Whether the character-select complaint is a bug, a patch, or a misreading.** `153599609` and
   `194024444` say the game assigns a character even in single player; `159107507` says *"you get to
   pick your character from the menu."* Fourteen months apart. **Check the patch notes, not the
   corpus.**
3. **How many owners never installed it.** `176679912` reports fewer than 10% hold the achievement
   for opening the game, which counts something different from every `dead-game` bullet — but it is
   one reviewer reading a public statistic (gap 116).
4. **The non-English population.** 1,290 of 1,886 all-language reviews are English. **596 reviews in
   other languages are unread**, and this game's Spanish-language reviews were visibly present in the
   English group, which suggests the language tagging is imperfect.
