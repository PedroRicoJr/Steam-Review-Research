# The Anacrusis — English

**A sample, nearly a census: 611 of 1,290 English reviews read, ±2.85% on any whole-sample share**
(finite population, 1,290). **49 of the 55 months read were read in full.** 611 reviews read of 611
pulled, from an English population of 1,290. The pull date and the planned quota (802) are in `raw/the-anacrusis/english/MANIFEST.md`.
Rounds 184–196.

**A co-op horde shooter for one to four players, built on purpose as a rival to Left 4 Dead** (Steam
store page: *"a 1-8 player, sci-fi first-person shooter"* with *"4 player co-op"*, checked 2026-10-03).
Made by Stray Bombay, a small independent studio whose lead wrote Left 4 Dead; Steam appid 1120480. Set
on a huge space ship overrun by aliens, played as episodes: reviewers name **5 episodes and 4–6 hours**
of content. An AI "director" moves the difficulty by how well you are doing; bots take over a player's
character when they step away; custom maps through a map workshop. Paid, and also shipped on Game
Pass. **Early access from January 2022, full release (1.0) December 2023.** The engine is not named on
these pages, though one reviewer says the map tools taught him Unreal Engine. All languages: **1,886
reviews, 46.6% positive (Mixed)**; 1,290 of them English. **It is row 3 of `GAMES-TODO.md`, picked as
the closest structural sibling to Back 4 Blood in the corpus**: it holds the pedigree fixed and changes
the budget — Back 4 Blood had Warner Bros; this had a small independent team. Both were measured
against the same fourteen-year-old game and both lost. It is close to Dominion: a four-player co-op PvE
shooter, sci-fi, on a space ship, made by a small team, paid, with bots and an adaptive director. It
differs in camera (first person, not third), in shape (fixed episodes in a horde game, not runs, arenas
or extraction), and it has no loot or build system the old pages talked about.

## 1. In plain words

**The thing players do most — shoot — feels like it hits nothing, and that never got fixed.** "The
shots feel weightless" is the biggest single complaint (81 times). With hits that look weak (32) and
thin sound (24) it makes 137 complaints against 21 that say shooting hits hard. Its share of each
year's comments grew every year: 4.8%, 5.9%, 6.6%, 8.6%.

**At first, players defended the game, and then they stopped.** "It could still be great" was said 74
times, 68 of them in 2022 and 2023. After the full release in December 2023 it was said 4 times, then
once, then once. Later reviews do not say the game got worse; they stop saying it will get better.

**The lobbies emptied, and the studio argued about it.** "Nobody plays this" was said 46 times (47
when first counted). In 2022 and 2023 players praised the studio for listening (34 times together);
from 2024 on they complained it fought critics and denied the low player count (28 times together).
Some say they were banned for posting the numbers.

**Players explain it by naming other games.** About one comment in nine names another game, nearly
three times Redfall's rate. Most often it is Left 4 Dead: 77 say a named game does it better, 63 say
it copies an older game, 34 say it beats its rivals, and 71 just use Left 4 Dead to say what it is.

**Who likes it played longer.** Thumbs-up reviews show a median of 3 hours, thumbs-down 1 hour. The
people who say the cast is flat played a median of 1 hour; the people who like the cast played 6.

**The look and the map-making tools were liked the whole time.** The art direction is praised 58
times; the custom map workshop 29 times (30 when first counted), and nobody argues about it.

**The surprise: the year with the worst score is mostly not about the game.** 2025 reads 37.7%
thumbs up, and its two most-helpful reviews are about the studio lead's politics, not about play.

## 3.1 ⭐ The finding: the believers left, and the complaint did not

`production.scope-mismatch.the-potential-is-still-there` is the third-largest mode in the corpus at
**74 bullets**. It is also the clearest time series in it.

| Year | `the-potential-is-still-there` | `community.population.dead-game` |
|---|---|---|
| 2022 | **35** | 8 |
| 2023 | **33** | 14 |
| 2024 | **4** | 15 |
| 2025 | 1 | 5 |
| 2026 | 1 | 5 |

**Sixty-eight of the seventy-four "it could still be great" bullets are from 2022 and 2023.** After
the December 2023 1.0 release the mode all but stops appearing. Nothing replaced it: the 2024–2026
reviews do not argue that the game got worse, they stop arguing that it will get better.

**Meanwhile the complaint that outlasted everything got proportionally louder:**

| Year | weightlessness bullets | share of that year's bullets |
|---|---|---|
| 2022 | 37 of 767 | 4.8% |
| 2023 | 51 of 869 | 5.9% |
| 2024 | 33 of 502 | 6.6% |
| 2025 | 14 of 162 | **8.6%** |

The weightlessness cluster is `combat.weightless` **81** + `effects-and-gore.impacts-look-weak` **32**
+ `sound-effects.weak-or-thin` **24** = **137 of 2,411 (5.7%)**, against `combat.impactful` **21**.
**Six and a half to one, and the ratio widened every year.**

`154491765` states the mechanism that the time series shows: *"They have received complaints about
this since it arrived on Early Access and have done absolutely nothing to rectify it."*

**This is the deep principle for a game-maker.** The corpus is not a game that got worse. It is a
game whose single largest problem was named in the first month, never fixed, and eventually
exhausted the people who were arguing on its behalf. **Optimism has a half-life, and the thing that
kills it is not decline — it is the same complaint surviving a year of patches.** The cross-game page
adds: **no other game read shows optimism collapsing as a separate event from sentiment falling** —
because no other game was defended this hard for this long. **A game does not die from a decline. It
dies when the people arguing on its behalf run out of argument**, and **the one complaint you do not
answer is the one that eventually converts your defenders into ex-players** — silently, by attrition,
not by a bad patch.

## 3.2 ⭐ The most-repeated complaint is the thing you do most, and one reviewer says it is misdiagnosed

`combat.weightless` at 81 is the corpus' largest single mode (3.4% of bullets). The 30-helpful
`131738483` puts it in proportion: *"this game is 99% shooting and that aspect feels worse in every way
than L4D, so I can't recommend it over that."* Every other strength — 58 bullets praising the art
direction, 30 the workshop, 25 the special enemies — sits on top of a core action that 137 bullets say
lands on nothing.

**Only one reviewer in 611 offers a fix list rather than a verdict.** `165340291`:

> add viewbob, do a whole pass on animations, remake Commons from scratch, add bullet penetration on
> pellets / consider replacing hitscan, either implement physics or just death animations (enemies
> just sort of slowly lump on ground, no momentum on ragdolls)

…and then makes the argument the rest of the corpus does not:

> The bad sound design is red herring… Now imagine you've fixed the audio – an audio engineer
> specialist has worked on it and it's amazing now, but that's the only thing that has been fixed.
> **The game would still have a lot more important problems at hand.**

**24 bullets blame the sound and 32 blame the visual impact; he says both are downstream of the same
missing layer** — no hit reaction, no death animation variety, no momentum. That is one claim from
one reviewer and it is the only structural explanation the corpus contains. **Fix the audio alone and
the game still feels the same.**

## 3.4 ⭐ The population is the second story, and the studio made it worse

`community.population.dead-game` **47** · `only-the-studio-chat-fills-a-lobby` **8** ·
`nobody-ever-heard-of-it` **7** · `dead-in-my-region` · `the-numbers-are-falling` = **70 bullets**
(2026-09-03 page). The script on 2026-10-03 counts dead game 46, only the studio chat fills a lobby 9,
nobody ever heard of it 10, dead in my region 3, the numbers are falling 1, most owners never played
it 1.

The reviews carry their own measurements, which is rare (what the reviewers say, not checked):

> *"the monthly average players is 3.4 per day and even on a Saturday night it only peaks at 7"* (`157013622`)
> *"the player count displayed a pitiful '1.' Yes, you guessed it, that lone soul was none other than myself."* (`160509575`)
> *"This game had maybe 11 players before the f2p weekend"* (`130514365`)

**And the studio's own communication splits almost evenly, in opposite time periods.**

| | Bullets |
|---|---|
| `listens-and-acts` + `open-about-what-it-is-doing` | **34** (16 + 18) |
| `punishes-criticism` + `disputes-the-player-count` + `adversarial` + `ignores-feedback` + others | **28** (8 + 5 + 4 + 3, then working on the wrong thing first 6 and misreads what players want 2) |

The praise clusters in 2022–2023. **Almost every negative bullet is 2024 or later**, and three
reviewers describe the same sequence independently: the charts show almost nobody, the studio says
the charts are wrong, the studio removes the people who post them. `139919128` quotes his ban notice
in full, issued for linking the game's own SteamDB page, with no reason given (the reviewer says).
`141244492` records the end of it: *"the devs have now finally admitted that there is a low player
count."*

⚠️ **The Discord is both the praise and the dependency.** Eight bullets say it is the only reliable
way to fill a lobby — including one from a reviewer offering it as friendly advice, and one quoting the
studio's own instruction to arrange games there. The cost, months later: *"Their Discord server members
are not very mature, and people get easily pissed when you have different opinions."* (`219373901`)
**A player who cannot get along in that server cannot play the game at all**, which makes the social
climate a gameplay requirement here rather than a nicety (gap 120). **When the chat server is the
matchmaker, its social climate is a system requirement.**

## 3.5 ⭐ The characters complaint may be a delivery problem, not a writing problem

`narrative.characters-writing.flat-or-annoying` carries **32 bullets** (31 by the script on
2026-10-03). `166892773` (21 hours) argues the whole corpus has it backwards:

> I believe some, if not all, voice lines have a specific **"weight" that depicts how often they
> appear** and most of those voice lines characterise the characters best… **you're not gonna get
> this all from just one hour** of playing the game, i'm still learning more about these characters
> after nearly 24 hours

**The corpus can test this claim, and it survives the test:**

| Bullet | n | Median hours | Under 2h |
|---|---|---|---|
| `characters-writing.flat-or-annoying` | 31 | **1** | **52%** |
| `characters-writing.funny-or-memorable` | 9 | **6** | 22% |

**The reviewers who say the cast has no personality played a median of one hour. The reviewers who
say the cast is good played six.** That does not prove the line-weighting mechanism, and it is
exactly the pattern the mechanism predicts.

⚠️ **This matters more than the complaint count.** If the writing exists and the delivery hides it,
the fix is a line-selection change, not a script — cheaper than a rewrite by an order of magnitude.
**If a studio read only the bullet count it would rewrite the wrong thing. Count the complaints, then
check who is making them.** Recorded as gap 114; on 2026-09-03 the mode was not built because one
reviewer is one reviewer. The script on 2026-10-03 finds
`narrative.characters-writing.the-best-lines-rarely-play` on 1 bullet, used in no other game.

**Context that weakens the corpus generally:** the median playtime across all 611 reviews is **2
hours**, 46.2% are under two hours, and **163 reviews (26.7%) show zero hours — 113 of them
negative.** Steam's counter only sees the Steam copy, and this game shipped into Game Pass, so an
unknown share of those zeroes are people who played it elsewhere.

## 3.6 ⭐ Wayfinding is the third complaint, and the game's answer to it is also a complaint

`confusing-layout` **22** + `cannot-spot-what-you-need` + `ui-ux.hides-information` +
`nothing-in-the-place-says-what-happened-here` = **45 bullets** (2026-09-03 page; the script counts 22,
7, 12 and 4).

The 19-helpful `159128455` names the missing pieces precisely:

> the ship of the Anacrusis is a pristine space-faring vessel overrun with a hostile alien force, and
> yet, there are **no indications of other survivors than yourselves, no gore-splatter, no signage,
> no unpossessed corpses, no hi-vis paint, no HUD element**

**That list is the finding.** The same absent set dressing does two jobs at once: it fails to tell
the story and it fails to show the way out. `156652417` reaches the same place from the narrative
side. **The mode built for it went under `level-design`, not `narrative`, because the fix is props in
the level, not script.**

**And the game's own answer is itself in the corpus.** `130889109`, who complained he could not tell
where to go, then complained about the fix: *"the game will just path you to where you need to go…
this felt more like an annoyance and like the game was treating me like an idiot."* **Signposting is
level design; a nav marker is an apology for its absence.**

## 3.7 ⭐ What the corpus is positive about

Not everything here is a complaint. **58 bullets praise the art direction** (`looks-well-directed`),

cannot make a good horde shooter.**

## 5. For Dominion — our reading

**These are suggestions drawn from what players said, not something the reviews said.** Dominion is a
third-person, four-player co-op sci-fi PvE extraction and arena shooter built on runs, hosted on one
player's machine (listen server), in Unreal Engine 5.

1. **The action players do most must feel great first, before anything else.**
   Evidence: weightless shooting 81 times, the top complaint; with weak hits (32) and weak sound (24),
   137 against 21 for "hits hard". Its share grew every year (4.8% to 8.6%). *"this game is 99%
   shooting"* (131738483).
   Dominion could: make gun feel a gate for any public build. Give every hit a reaction (flinch,
   stagger, part damage), several death animations, and ragdolls that keep the bullet's momentum.
   Then tune sound and effects on top of that, not instead of it.
   Strength: **strong**.
2. **Sound and visual impact may be symptoms, not the cause.**
   Evidence: one reviewer (165340291) argues sound (24) and visual (32) complaints both come from no
   hit reaction, no death variety, no momentum; fixing audio alone would not help.
   Dominion could: when "feels weak" comes in, check enemy hit reactions and death motion first, then
   audio.
   Strength: **weak** (one reviewer), but it is the only cause given.
3. **An unanswered top complaint slowly turns defenders into ex-players.**
   Evidence: "the potential is still there" fell from 35 and 33 a year to 4, 1, 1 after full release,
   while the weightless complaint grew. *"They have received complaints about this since it arrived on
   Early Access and have done absolutely nothing"* (154491765).
   Dominion could: if early access or playtests name one top complaint, fix it or say clearly why not,
   before full release. Track hope ("it could be great") as a signal; when it drops, time is short.
   Strength: **strong**.
4. **A four-player co-op game needs players or a way to play without them.**
   Evidence: 70 times about too few players; 47 "dead game"; reviewers quoting player counts of 1 to
   11.
   Dominion could: make solo with bots and two- or three-player runs fully good, so an empty
   matchmaking pool does not end the game; scale enemies to party size.
   Strength: **strong**.
5. **Let bots take over a player who steps away.**
   Evidence: 3 mentions, all praise, even in a thumbs-down review; the least argued-about feature.
   Dominion could: when a player goes idle or drops on a listen server, let a bot run their character
   and hand it back when they return.
   Strength: **weak to medium** (few mentions, but none against).
6. **Do not make a chat server the matchmaker.**
   Evidence: 8 say the studio Discord is the only way to fill a lobby; one says its members are
   unpleasant; a player who cannot fit in there cannot play.
   Dominion could: build in-game public lobbies and quick join, so outside chat is a bonus, not a
   requirement.
   Strength: **medium**.
7. **Never argue with players about the player count; never ban for posting it.**
   Evidence: 28 negative studio-talk mentions, almost all 2024 on; three reviewers tell the same story
   of denial and bans; the studio later admitted the low count (141244492).
   Dominion could: be honest about numbers; answer criticism, do not remove it.
   Strength: **medium**.
8. **Levels should show the way with props and story, not with a nav arrow.**
   Evidence: 45 times about wayfinding; *"no signage, no unpossessed corpses, no hi-vis paint, no HUD
   element"* (159128455); the auto-path felt like *"treating me like an idiot"* (130889109).
   Dominion could: use lighting, signs, damage, bodies and paint to lead players through each arena
   and extraction route; keep any marker as a light help, not the main guide.
   Strength: **medium**.
9. **Small-team excuses do not save a core flaw.**
   Evidence: 23 reviews grade against the team's size, split both ways; one says great small-team
   games exist.
   Dominion could: expect players to judge the shooting by the standard of the best in the genre, not
   by team size.
   Strength: **medium**.
10. **Content length matters for a paid co-op game.**
    Evidence: 42 say too little; 4-6 hours, 5 episodes named.
    Dominion could: lean on runs that play out differently each time, so a small number of arenas
    gives many hours.
    Strength: **medium**.
11. **Count a complaint, then check who is making it.**
    Evidence: 32 say the cast is flat; those reviewers played a median of 1 hour, the fans played 6.
    One reviewer says key lines are set to play rarely.
    Dominion could: let the best character lines play early and often enough to be heard in a first
    hour; when reading feedback, look at hours played before rewriting.
    Strength: **weak to medium**.
12. **A custom map workshop builds goodwill.**
    Evidence: 30 praise, uncontested; one reviewer says it started his Unreal level-design career.
    Dominion could: later, consider letting players make arenas (one reviewer says the map tools taught
    him Unreal Engine). A long-term nice to have, not a launch need.
    Strength: **medium**.
13. **Art direction is the safest praise, but it does not hold players.**
    Evidence: art direction 58, environment art 23 - yet the game still emptied.
    Dominion could: keep a strong look, but spend first on feel.
    Strength: **medium**.
14. **Adaptive difficulty splits players.**
    Evidence: the AI director was judged both ways (the takeaways entry gave no count; the script
    counts 7 bullets, section 4).
    Dominion could: if difficulty moves under players, let them see or choose it.
    Strength: **weak**.
15. **Studio politics can swamp reviews.**
    Evidence: 4 reviews in 2025 with the top helpful votes (41, 45), about the studio lead, none about
    play; 3 about the game's banners.
    Dominion could: know that the studio's public voice is part of the product page.
    Strength: **weak**.
16. **Being compared to a giant is unavoidable; make it a short label, not a contest.**
    Evidence: 263 of 2,411 things said (10.9%) name another game; 77 say a named game is better; 63
    say it copies; 71 just say "Left 4 Dead in space".
    Dominion could: know which games it will be measured against and beat them on at least one clear
    thing.
    Strength: **medium**.

## 7. Confidence and limits

1. **Sample share and margin.** 611 of 1,290 (47.4%), ±2.85%. 49 of 55 months at 100% census; the
   shortfall sits in six months, and two carry it — **2022-06 read at 18.6% (64 of 345) and 2023-01 at
   26.4% (79 of 299)**. Per-period margins were not computed on this page.
2. **The under-read months lean negative.** Both are months where the game reached people who had not
   chosen it, so the weighted figure (58.49%) sits *above* the sample (57.45%).
3. **The weighted stats file is stale.** `raw/the-anacrusis/english/_the-anacrusis-english-group-stats.md`
   is built from 7 monthly stat files and 150 reviews, not the full read. The 58.49% weighted figure is
   the 2026-09-03 page's.
4. **The English figure is not Steam's.** 57.45% is English only; Steam's 46.6% is all-language and
   lifetime.
5. **Edited reviews.** The 2026-09-03 page counted 125 multi-dated reviews (20.5%); the script's edit
   marker finds 66 (10.8%). All are counted on the created date. Some reviewers kept their review as a
   running list of what the studio fixed.
6. **Playtime is a weak guide.** Median 2 hours; 46.2% under 2 hours; 163 (26.7%) at zero hours, 113
   of them negative. Game Pass play is not counted by Steam.
7. **2025 is a political row**, not a verdict on the game (section 3.8).
8. **What this corpus cannot tell you:**
   1. **Whether the line-weighting claim in section 3.5 is true.** One reviewer, one mechanism, and a
      playtime correlation that is consistent with it. It needs the game, not the reviews.
   2. **Whether the character-select complaint is a bug, a patch, or a misreading.** `153599609` and
      `194024444` say the game assigns a character even in single player; `159107507` says *"you get
      to pick your character from the menu."* Fourteen months apart. **Check the patch notes, not the
      corpus.**
   3. **How many owners never installed it.** `176679912` reports fewer than 10% hold the achievement
      for opening the game, which counts something different from every `dead-game` bullet — but it
      is one reviewer reading a public statistic (gap 116).
   4. **The non-English population.** 1,290 of 1,886 all-language reviews are English. **596 reviews
      in other languages are unread**, and this game's Spanish-language reviews were visibly present
      in the English group, which suggests the language tagging is imperfect.
9. **Unchecked claims.** Player counts, the bans, the studio's statements and the character-pick
   question are what reviewers say; none was checked against a primary source. Release dates are from
   the 2026-09-03 pages.
10. **Slurs and personal details.** None are quoted on this page; the 2025 political reviews are
    described, not quoted.
