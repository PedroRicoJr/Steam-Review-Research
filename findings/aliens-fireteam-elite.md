# Aliens: Fireteam Elite — what this game teaches

**Read: 1,501 of 1,501 English reviews. 4,400 observations. 0 unfitted.**
**Margin of error ⚠️ ±3.56%.** ⚠️ **2021-08 holds 25.4% of the English population and was read at
1.62%** — see `aliens-fireteam-elite-english.md` §0 before using any number here.

---

## Why this game was in the queue

**It is the closest structural match to Dominion in the whole corpus, and it is the only one that
worked.**

| | Aliens: Fireteam Elite | Dominion |
|---|---|---|
| View | Third person | Third person |
| Team size | 3 | 3–4 |
| Structure | Class-based, ability-driven PvE | Class-based, ability-driven PvE |
| Business model | Paid, no free tier | Paid |
| Networking | Peer-to-peer, host migration | **P2P listen-server at launch** |
| Outcome | **79.7%, Mostly Positive** | — |

**Every other game in the queue is a failure to learn from.** This one is a success — and the useful
thing about it is that it is a *modest* success, not a phenomenon. **Deep Rock Galactic at 97.1% and
Helldivers 2 at 83.3% are outliers whose lessons may not transfer. This one sold well, reviewed
well, ran five years, and stopped.** That is the realistic good outcome.

---

## The one thing to take from it

🔑 **The game got the hard part right and lost its audience on the cheap part.**

**193 reviews say the adaptation is faithful. 187 say there is not enough of it.** Those are the two
largest modes in the game and they sit within six of each other. The atmosphere, the guns, the world
and the feel — the expensive, risky, creative work — all landed. **What ended the game was mission
count, repetition, and a matchmaking pool that emptied.**

⚠️ **For Dominion this inverts the usual worry.** The instinct on a solo project is to fear that the
core feel will not be good enough. **This corpus says a small studio can nail the feel and still be
finished by content volume and population.** Twelve missions across four areas, at a median of 13
hours played, is what "not enough" looked like for a paid third-person co-op PvE game in 2021.

## Four specific transfers to Dominion

### 1. A three-player co-op game with no chat has to win its matchmaking, and this one did not

**The single most avoidable failure in this corpus.** The game shipped with **no text chat and no
voice chat**. `community.social-features.cannot-communicate` takes **51 bullets**, and one review's
entire text is *"why the hell is there no text or voice chat??"*

**That would be survivable on its own. It compounds with two other faults:**

| Fault | Bullets | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| Nobody to match with | 73 `dead-game` + 41 `cannot-find-games` | You cannot get a team |
| Cannot speak to them | 51 `cannot-communicate` | The team you get cannot coordinate |
| The bots do not cover | 60 `useless-in-combat` | The fallback is not a fallback |

🔴 **The chain has no exit.** `228598427` walks all three in one review: no lobby, or a lobby of
over-levelled strangers who leave or steamroll you, or bots that shoot you in the back. His
conclusion is that the AI option should be **taken out of the game**, because its existence promises
a solo route that is not there.

**For Dominion:** the launch is peer-to-peer listen-server sessions with 3–4 players and a design
target of 200 on a dedicated server. **Communication is not a polish item; in this genre it is part
of whether the session works at all.** Build the text and voice path with the session code, not
after it.

⚠️ **And notice which one is cheapest.** Chat is the cheapest of the three to build and it is the
one this game skipped.

---

### 2. Bots are a promise, and a bad bot is worse than no bot

**60 bullets say the AI teammates are useless in combat. 28 say they enable solo play.** More than
two complaints for every endorsement — and the surrounding modes are sharper than the headline:

- `.cannot-configure-your-bots` — they default to Gunner and the player cannot change their class
- `.actively-harms-you` — they shoot you in the back and stand in your line of fire
- `.the-game-itself-warns-you-off-its-bots` — **the game tells you not to rely on them above a
  certain difficulty**

🔑 **That last mode is the finding.** The studio knew, said so in the interface, and shipped the
option anyway. **A bot that the game itself disclaims is an admission printed inside the product.**

**For Dominion:** if the session can run short-handed, decide early whether AI fill is a supported
way to play or a stopgap, and **say which in the design, not in a warning dialog.** Across the whole
corpus `.useless-in-combat` takes **128** bullets and `.no-bots-provided` takes **2**. **Players
complain about bad bots sixty times more often than about missing ones.**

⚠️ **The counter-evidence is real and worth holding.** `234027899` put in 130 hours and clears
Intense difficulty with one friend and a bot. **Bots that genuinely work are a retention feature.
Bots that half work are a liability.**

---

### 3. Sale-dependence is a slow way to lose a co-op game

**137 reviews — 9.1% of the entire group — tell the reader to wait for a discount.** That is the
game's second largest negative.

⚠️ **This is not "the game is bad".** `publishing.price.fair` takes 35. **The recurring verdict is
that the game is worth about ten dollars and not about forty.**

🔴 **One review shows what that costs a game whose product is other people.** `229190054` is a
thumbs **down** whose whole complaint is pricing history: *"Enjoyable game when it reached my price
point, too bad it took so long to be discounted. As the playerbase is dead now."*

🔑 **He waited for the price, and the wait cost him the thing he was buying.** In a single-player
game a sale-dependent reputation costs revenue per unit. **In a co-op game it also costs the
population, because everyone waiting for the same discount arrives after everyone else has left.**

**For Dominion:** a co-op game's price strategy is a population strategy. **The people who buy at
80% off three years in are buying an empty lobby, and they review it accordingly.**

---

### 4. A licence buys an audience and narrows it in the same move

**76 reviews carry `only-worth-it-if-you-already-love-the-source` — "this is for fans, everyone else
stay away".** That is the game's fourth largest negative, and it is written *by people recommending
the game*.

**The adaptation modes this game forced into the tree show how specific the audience's demands
are:**

| Complaint | Example from the corpus |
|---|---|
| The thing is missing | The pulse rifle without its grenade launcher |
| The thing is there and fenced off | The Queen you cannot fight; the power loader behind glass |
| The thing is present and wrong | The pulse rifle's 99-round magazine, locked behind a grind |
| The monster stopped being frightening | *"the aliens look as scary as a dog"* |
| It is faithful to the wrong instalment | *"this is not an Alien game, this is an Aliens game"* |

🔑 **The last one was built on the final review of the group and it is the sharpest.** A series is
not one source. **Fans do not ask whether you were faithful; they ask which part you were faithful
to.**

**For Dominion, which has no licence, the transfer is inverted:** every one of those complaints is a
cost this project does not pay. **An original setting cannot disappoint anyone's memory.** What it
loses is the 193 bullets of goodwill the licence bought — **Dominion has to earn attention that this
game was handed.**

## The counterexample worth holding

⚠️ **The single largest mode in the game is `review.positive.unknown` at 334 — 22.3% of all
reviews say nothing at all.** Add the negative equivalent and **358 reviews (23.9%) are
content-free.**

🔑 **Nearly a quarter of a successful game's reviews carry no information about the game.** They are
"nice", "gg", "Yez", "8/10", a film quote, a joke. **A game-maker reading its own reviews for
signal should expect to throw away one in four before starting.**

**And the informative ones are short.** This game averages **2.93 bullets per review** — fourth
lowest of the eight games. **Redfall, the disaster, averages 3.76 and The Anacrusis 3.96.** People
write at length when they are angry or when the game is complicated; a game that works produces
short reviews.

---

## What this game is not evidence for

1. 🔴 **How it launched.** The launch month holds 25.4% of the English population, was read at
   **1.62%**, and contributes **66.6% of the group's uncertainty.** Nothing here describes the first
   weeks with confidence.
2. **Whether the co-op design is good.** The corpus says the *population* failed. It cannot separate
   "the matchmaking is bad" from "not enough people bought it".
3. **Dedicated-server behaviour.** This game is peer-to-peer and the complaints reflect that —
   players freezing and dropping *from the host*. **Dominion's 200-player dedicated target has no
   analogue anywhere in this corpus.**
4. **Anything after 2026-09.** The last review read is dated 2026-09-01.
5. **Non-English players.** 9,505 non-English reviews are unread.

## Tree contribution

**Rounds 212–242, thirty-one rounds, 1,501 reviews.** **69 modes built — the tree went from 874 to
943 — and 28 gaps closed. 0 unfitted observations.**

**That is the third largest contribution in the corpus**, behind the original English run (172)

and the whole `marketing.reputation.i-want-a-sequel-to-this-one` shape exist because this is the
first licensed adaptation read.**
