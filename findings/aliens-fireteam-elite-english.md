**Cold Iron Studios / Focus Entertainment · appid 1549970 · released 2021-08-23 · paid.** A
third-person, class-based, ability-driven co-op PvE shooter for three players, based on the Aliens
films. No Early Access. Paid, with no free tier, plus paid add-ons and a season pass. Online play is peer
to peer: one player hosts, with host migration. The campaign is twelve missions across four areas. Bots

ability-driven PvE, paid, hosted by one player. It differs in team size (three, not four), in being
missions rather than runs, in having no extraction or arena mode, and in being a licensed game. Row 5 of

## 1. In plain words

**This is the study's one ordinary success.** It was good, it sold, it ran five years and it stopped.
Steam has it at 79.7% thumbs up; the sample reads 81.35%.

**The most-praised thing and the most-blamed thing are both true.** 193 reviews say it is faithful to
the films. 187 say there is not enough of it. Many reviews say both at once.

**Praise is about how it feels; complaints are about how much there is.** Players praise the world, the
friends, the builds and the guns. They complain about too few missions, repeats, and nobody left to
play with.

**The team play broke three ways at once.** There was nobody to match with (73 say the game is dead, 41
say matchmaking finds no game). There was no way to talk to the people you got (51, and the game had no
text or voice chat). And the computer teammates did not cover the gap (60 say they are useless).

**137 reviews (9.1%) say wait for a sale.** The usual verdict is that the game is worth about ten
dollars, not forty. One reviewer waited for the price and found the players had already gone.

**It held steady for five years and then dipped.** Each year from 2021 to 2025 sits between 80.5% and
85.4% thumbs up; 2026 is 75.3%. The 2026 complaints are about empty lobbies, not about the game.

**The surprise: a thumbs up does not mean nothing is wrong.** The 1,221 thumbs-up reviews carry 1,187
complaints between them — almost one each.

## 3. ⭐ The thumb and the text disagree, and the text is the useful one

**This game is 79.7% positive on Steam and its praise-to-complaint ratio is 0.88 : 1.** Back 4 Blood
is **69.2%** positive and its ratio is **0.86 : 1** — ten points apart on the thumb, within two
hundredths on the text (cross-game section 13).

🔴 **The 1,221 thumbs-up reviews in this game carry 1,187 complaints between them** - almost one
each - alongside 1,759 pieces of praise. **The 280 thumbs-down reviews carry 918 complaints and 98
pieces of praise.**

🔑 **A thumbs up is not a report that nothing is wrong. The thumb records whether they would buy it
again; the text records what they would change.**

## 3. ⭐ The central tension: the most praised thing and the most criticised thing are both true

**Nearly one review in eight says the adaptation is right. Nearly one in eight says there is not
enough of it.** These are not competing camps — reviews carry both. `132762903` is a thumbs **down**
whose first three words are *"faithful recreation of aliens"*, and whose complaint is the
twelve-mission campaign.

🔑 **Getting the licence right and having enough game are two separate findings, and this corpus
proves a game can do the first and fail the second.** The fiction brought people in; the content
budget decided how long they stayed. (Table in section 2.)

## 3. ⭐ The co-op promise is the whole product, and three separate faults break it

**156 reviews say the game is much better with friends. 37 say they have nobody to play with.**
The design assumes three people; the corpus shows three independent ways that assumption fails, and
they compound.

**1. There is nobody to match with.** `community.population.dead-game` takes **73** and
`engineering.matchmaking.cannot-find-games` takes **41**.

**2. When you get people, you cannot speak to them.** `community.social-features.cannot-communicate`
takes **51**. **The game shipped with no text chat and no voice chat.** `222497258`'s entire review
is *"why the hell is there no text or voice chat??"*

**3. When you play alone, the substitute does not work.**
`game-design.ai-teammates.useless-in-combat` takes **60**, and the surrounding modes are worse:
`.cannot-configure-your-bots` (they default to Gunner and cannot be changed),
`.actively-harms-you` (they shoot you in the back), and
`game-design.ai-teammates.the-game-itself-warns-you-off-its-bots` — **the game tells you not to
rely on them above a certain difficulty.** (Tag-count grep, 2026-10-03: cannot configure 7, actively
harms you 16, the game warns you off 3.)

⚠️ **28 reviews still say the bots enable solo play.** `234027899` played 130 hours and clears
Intense difficulty with one friend and a bot. **The bots are not uniformly bad. The count is 60
against 28** — more than two complaints for every endorsement.

🔑 **A three-player co-op game with no chat has to win its matchmaking, and this one did not.**
`228598427`, a careful thumbs-down written explicitly for newcomers, walks the whole chain: no
lobby, or a lobby of over-levelled strangers who leave or steamroll, or bots that are worse than
nothing. His conclusion is that the AI option should be **removed**, because its existence promises
a solo game that is not there.

## 3. ⭐ The price is doing more work than the game

**137 reviews — 9.1% of the whole group — say "buy it on sale".** That is the **fifth largest mode
in the game and its second largest negative**, behind only content amount. At 9.1 per 100 it is second
in the corpus after Redfall's 17.0 (cross-game section 22).

⚠️ **This is not the same as saying the game is bad.** `publishing.price.fair` takes 35 and
`.too-high-for-what-it-is` takes 30. **The recurring verdict is that the game is worth about ten
dollars and not about forty.** `224397410`: *"This is a good horde shooter, for the $10 I paid.
Heck, this is a good $40 horde shooter."* — a minority position, and he states the comparison
because he expects the reader to disagree.

🔴 **One review shows the cost of that reputation.** `229190054` is a thumbs **down** whose entire
complaint is pricing history: *"Enjoyable game when it reached my price point, too bad it took so
long to be discounted. As the playerbase is dead now."* **He waited for the price and the wait cost
him the thing he was buying — other players.** A sale-dependent reputation in a co-op game
eventually eats the co-op.

## 3. ⭐ What the reviewers actually did with the licence

`narrative` took 448 bullets (10.2%), and the adaptation modes are unusually rich because **this is
the only licensed adaptation in the corpus.** The run built nine modes here that no other game
needed:

- `.faithful-to-the-source-it-adapts` (193) and `.does-not-feel-like-the-source-it-adapts` (28)
- `.only-worth-it-if-you-already-love-the-source` (76) — **the fourth largest negative in the game**
- `.an-iconic-thing-from-the-source-is-missing` — the pulse rifle without its grenade launcher
- `.an-iconic-thing-is-there-and-you-never-use-it` — the Queen you cannot fight, the power loader
  behind glass
- `.the-monster-is-no-longer-frightening` — *"the aliens look as scary as a dog"*
- `.carries-over-the-part-of-the-source-i-dislike`
- `.none-of-the-sources-characters-are-here`
- `.the-additions-do-not-belong-in-the-source`
- `.faithful-to-one-part-of-the-series` — built on the **last review of the group**

The pulse rifle's 99-round magazine, locked behind a grind, is the "present and wrong" case (the game's
master page).

🔑 **The last review read taught the tree that a series is not one source.** `234223542`: *"You'll
note this is not an Alien game. This is an Aliens game. The developers have really picked up on
what made James Cameron's vision of the Alien universe unique."* **Which instalment a licensed game
adapts is a fact its buyers care about, and until that review the tree could only say faithful or
not.**

⚠️ **76 reviews tell non-fans to stay away.** That is the licence working as a filter, not as a
draw. **A licence buys an audience and narrows it at the same time.**

## 5. For Dominion — our reading

**These are suggestions drawn from what players said, not something the reviews said.** Dominion is a
third-person, four-player co-op sci-fi PvE extraction and arena shooter built on runs, hosted on one
player's machine (listen server), in Unreal Engine 5.

These include every lesson the game's own "for Dominion" page (`findings/aliens-fireteam-elite.md`)
gives, marked (page).

1. **You can nail the feel and still lose on content and population.** (page) Evidence: faithful 193,
   too little content 187; the 2026 drop is about empty lobbies, not design. Dominion could: plan content
   volume and repeat-play (runs, arenas, extraction maps, modifiers) as hard as the feel; for a small
   team, systems that make many runs from few parts matter more than hand-built missions. Strength:
   strong.
2. **Build chat with the session, not after it.** (page) Evidence: 51 say there is no way to talk; no
   text or voice chat at launch; chat is the cheapest of the three co-op faults to fix and the one this
   game skipped. Dominion could: ship text chat, voice chat and a ping/marker system on day one, with
   mute and report. Strength: strong.
3. **A three- or four-player game must win its matchmaking.** (page) Evidence: dead game 73, can't find
   a game 41, nobody to play with 37; 2026 complaints are about this. Dominion could: make joining easy
   (quick join, open lobbies, friend invites, joining a run in progress), keep one shared pool rather
   than many split queues, and add crossplay if possible. Strength: strong.
4. **A bad bot is worse than no bot; decide early what bots are for.** (page) Evidence: useless 60
   against 28 who say bots make solo work; bots can't be set, shoot you, and the game warns you off them;
   across the corpus bad bots 128, missing bots 2; 228598427 wants them removed. Dominion could: either
   make bots a real supported way to play (choose their class, they revive, they never hurt you, they hold
   up on high difficulty) or scale the run to the number of players and leave bots out. Say which in the
   design, not in a warning box. Strength: strong.
5. **A co-op game's price plan is a population plan.** (page) Evidence: 137 say wait for a sale (9.1%);
   the verdict is "worth ten, not forty"; 229190054 waited and found the game empty. Dominion could:
   price so launch buyers feel it is fair, and plan early discounts, bundles or friend passes to keep
   lobbies full, rather than a long wait for a deep sale. Strength: strong.
6. **Expect a quarter of reviews to say nothing.** (page) Evidence: 358 (23.9%) carry no detail; good
   games get short reviews (2.93 points each). Dominion could: plan feedback reading knowing about one in
   four reviews is noise. Strength: strong.
7. **A licence buys an audience and narrows it; an original setting pays neither cost.** (page)
   Evidence: 193 faithful, 76 "only for fans", plus the missing-iconic-thing complaints. Dominion could:
   earn the attention a licence hands out - a clear look, a strong first hour and a reason to tell
   friends - since it has no film to lean on. Strength: medium.
8. **Fans ask "which part were you faithful to".** (page) Evidence: "not an Alien game, an Aliens
   game" (234223542). Dominion could: since it draws on its own source books for lore, pick which part of
   them it follows and say so. Strength: weak (one review), but sharp.
9. **Do not fence off the iconic thing.** Evidence: the Queen you cannot fight, the power loader behind
   glass, the grenade launcher missing, the 99-round magazine locked behind a grind. Dominion could: if
   the world has a famous threat or tool, let players fight it or use it, and do not lock the signature
   version of a gun behind a long grind. Strength: weak (no counts), clear pattern.
10. **Thumbs up does not mean nothing is wrong.** Evidence: thumbs-up reviews carry 1,187 complaints
    (about one each). Dominion could: read the complaints in positive reviews as the to-do list. Strength:
    strong.
11. **Peer-to-peer hosting needs good drop handling.** Evidence: players freeze and drop from the host;
    frequent disconnects is a main 2026 complaint; the game has host migration. Dominion could, as a
    listen-server game: plan host migration or a quick rejoin, save run progress so a dropped player can
    come back, and test bad connections. Strength: medium.
12. **Strangers far above your level ruin the first games.** Evidence: 228598427 - over-levelled
    strangers who leave or steamroll. Dominion could: match by level or difficulty, and let new players
    find a team at their level. Strength: weak to medium.
13. **Deep builds and distinct classes are what players praise.** Evidence: builds 112, guns 81, classes
    38. Dominion could: keep classes clearly different and builds deep. Strength: strong.
14. **Monsters must stay scary.** Evidence: "as scary as a dog". Dominion could: keep the main enemy's
    threat clear with sound, size and behaviour, and do not let it become just a crowd. Strength: weak.
15. **When updates stop, the game goes quiet, not angry.** Evidence: live service talk only 1.7%.
    Dominion could: expect that ending a game's support will not draw a storm, but the population will
    fade. Strength: medium.
16. **Paid add-ons split players.** Evidence: 14 not worth it, 11 fair, 58 points in all. Dominion could:
    if it sells add-ons, keep maps and modes free so the player pool stays whole. Strength: weak.
17. **Friendly fire splits people.** Evidence: present both as a cost and as fun. Dominion could: make it
    a lobby option or a difficulty rule. Strength: weak.
18. **The page's network note:** the game's page wrote its lessons for a Dominion with a 200-player
    dedicated-server target, and says nothing in the corpus speaks to that. Dominion starts as a
    four-player listen server, so this game's peer-to-peer complaints are the right comparison now; the
    200-player lessons are kept for the possible larger version later (Rico, 2026-10-03).

### The game page's own words, moved here as well

These are the "For Dominion" passages from `findings/aliens-fireteam-elite.md`, kept as written there.

- **The one thing to take from it.** 🔑 **The game got the hard part right and lost its audience on the
  cheap part.** ⚠️ **For Dominion this inverts the usual worry.** The instinct on a solo project is to
  fear that the core feel will not be good enough. **This corpus says a small studio can nail the feel
  and still be finished by content volume and population.** Twelve missions across four areas, at a
  median of 13 hours played, is what "not enough" looked like for a paid third-person co-op PvE game in
  2021.
- **Chat.** **For Dominion:** the launch is peer-to-peer listen-server sessions with 3–4 players and a
  design target of 200 on a dedicated server. *(Rico, 2026-10-03: Dominion starts at four players on a
  listen server; a larger version, possibly 200 players, may come later. See `Rico notes.md`.)*
  **Communication is not a polish item; in this genre it is part of whether the session works at all.**
  Build the text and voice path with the session code, not after it. ⚠️ **And notice which one is
  cheapest.** Chat is the cheapest of the three to build and it is the one this game skipped.
- **Bots.** **For Dominion:** if the session can run short-handed, decide early whether AI fill is a
  supported way to play or a stopgap, and **say which in the design, not in a warning dialog.** Across
  the whole corpus `.useless-in-combat` takes **128** bullets and `.no-bots-provided` takes **2**.
  **Players complain about bad bots sixty times more often than about missing ones.** **Bots that
  genuinely work are a retention feature. Bots that half work are a liability.** **A bot that the game
  itself disclaims is an admission printed inside the product.**
- **Price.** **For Dominion:** a co-op game's price strategy is a population strategy. **The people who
  buy at 80% off three years in are buying an empty lobby, and they review it accordingly.** In a
  single-player game a sale-dependent reputation costs revenue per unit. **In a co-op game it also costs
  the population, because everyone waiting for the same discount arrives after everyone else has left.**
- **Licence.** **For Dominion, which has no licence, the transfer is inverted:** every one of those
  complaints is a cost this project does not pay. **An original setting cannot disappoint anyone's
  memory.** What it loses is the 193 bullets of goodwill the licence bought — **Dominion has to earn
  attention that this game was handed.**
- **Reading reviews.** **A game-maker reading its own reviews for signal should expect to throw away
  one in four before starting.**
- **Not evidence for.** **Dedicated-server behaviour.** This game is peer-to-peer and the complaints
  reflect that — players freezing and dropping *from the host*. **Dominion's 200-player dedicated
  target has no analogue anywhere in this corpus.**

## 7. Confidence and limits

### 0. The sampling limit, stated first (section 0 of the page before this rebuild, which `cross-game.md` cites)

🔴 **This group reads at ±3.56%, not the ±2.5% the plan solved for.** Rule 12 binds: the margin is
computed from the reviews actually in each month's bucket, never from the quota asked for.

⚠️ **One month causes almost all of it. 2021-08 holds 4,450 reviews — 25.4% of the whole English
population — and 72 of them were read. That is 1.62% coverage.**

**Here is the part that matters, and it is not obvious from the coverage number alone:**

| Month | Read | On Steam | Coverage | Share of the group's uncertainty |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **2021-08** | **72** | **4,450** | **1.62%** | 🔴 **66.6%** |
| 2021-09 | 190 | 2,917 | 6.51% | 10.3% |
| 2021-10 | 33 | 512 | 6.45% | 1.8% |
| 2021-11 | 32 | 496 | 6.45% | 1.8% |
| 2023-04 | 28 | 434 | 6.45% | 1.5% |
| *the other 57 months* | *1,146* | *8,742* | *13.1%* | *18.0%* |

🔑 **One month out of sixty-two carries two thirds of everything this document could be wrong
about.** Had 2021-08 returned the 290 reviews it asked for, the group would read at **±2.49%** —
on target. Every other month hit its number.

**The cause, measured in Phase A:** `month_windows` splits a calendar month into four even windows
starting on the 1st, and does not know when the game came out. The game released on **2021-08-23**,
so three of the four windows for its launch month cover days before it existed, all 4,450 reviews
sit in the fourth, and one window returns about 72 before Steam stops advancing its cursor.

**Rico declined the fix on 2026-09-04** (*"i never said apply the fix. I meant there's no issue, so
execute the retreival now on loop"*). The sampler is unchanged and the month was not re-pulled.
**The margin is not the problem. Hiding it would be.**

### What this corpus cannot tell you

1. 🔴 **The launch month.** 1.62% coverage, 25.4% of the population, 66.6% of the uncertainty. **Any
   claim about how the game landed in its first weeks rests on 72 reviews.**
2. **Non-English players.** 9,505 non-English reviews are unread. Steam's all-language figure is
   79.7%; this group reads 81.35% raw and 83.42% weighted. **Do not compare those numbers directly.**
3. **Why people stopped.** The corpus holds the reviews of people who wrote one. **It cannot see the
   larger group who played twelve missions and never came back without saying so.**
4. **Whether the DLC changed anything.** Paid add-ons and monetisation take **58 bullets across 15
   modes**, and they point both ways — `.dlc-not-worth-it` (14) against `.dlc-is-fair` (11).
   **No review in the group reads as a systematic assessment of the season pass.**
5. **Sales.** Nothing here measures revenue. *"Sold well"* in section 3 comes from the review count
   against its rating, not from a figure Valve publishes.
6. **Whether the co-op design is good.** The corpus says the *population* failed. It cannot separate
   "the matchmaking is bad" from "not enough people bought it".
7. **Dedicated-server behaviour.** This game is peer-to-peer; nothing in this corpus has a
   dedicated-server analogue.
8. **Whether a licence pays.** It bought 193 bullets of goodwill and cost 76 reviews telling non-fans
   to stay away; nothing here nets those against each other.
