# Banzai Escape — English

**A census: all 80 English reviews read. No margin of error.** Every English review there was at the
pull was read, so there is no sample and no margin. Steam's own English count is 51 up and 30 down as
of 2026-09-10 — one review arrived after the pull. Ours is 51 up and 29 down. The tables in section 2
were re-run on 2026-10-03.

**A one-person on-rails cover shooter from 2016, ported from Android, sold at about $6.** Not a
roguelike and not a third-person shooter: a Time Crisis with a keyboard. Missions you replay to earn
money; money buys guns and ammunition. The engine is not recorded on this page (two reviewers call it
a Unity shooter). Steam, all languages: 168 reviews, 115 up, 68.5% — English is 48%. **It is the oldest
game in the block by four years, and its 80 reviews are spread across ten years — 26 of them in 2016
and never more than eight in any year since.** It was read as the fifth of the ten-game block. It is
not close to Dominion: it shares shooting, cover and boss fights, but it is single-player, on rails,
made for phones first, and has no runs, co-op or extraction. Its lessons are about economy, fairness
and controls.

## 1. In plain words

**One rule decides how players feel: you have to buy your ammunition.** 17 times a reviewer said so —
twice as often as anything else — and 11 of those 17 voted thumbs down. Run out of ammo in a mission
and you fail, so you replay old levels to earn the money.

**The complaint never went away.** It was said in every year from 2016 to 2021, and again in 2024 and
2026. A reviewer says the maker promised in 2018 to fix it in a sequel; this game kept it.

**The other half of the complaints come from it being a phone game moved to PC.** 9 times the
controls did not respond, 8 times a death felt unfair, 5 times a needed control was missing, and 4
times a reviewer said outright it is a phone port.

**The people who like it say it is small, funny and hard.** 5 want a sequel, 5 say it is hard in a good
way, 5 say the writing is funny, and 4 like the characters.

**Fans forgive it for being short.** All 5 reviewers who said it is too short still recommend it.

**The voices split players.** 8 times a reviewer said the voice acting is bad and 3 times that it is
good, about the same voices.

**The surprise: this game asks almost nothing of the study.** It drew the fewest comments per review
of any game read, and needed nothing new to describe it.

## 3. The big stories

### 3.1 ⭐ One design decision is the whole complaint: you buy your ammunition

`game-design.progression.unlock-pace.gated-behind-farming` — **17 bullets, the largest mode by a
factor of two, 11 of them thumbs down.** Money buys guns and magazines; running out mid-mission
fails it; the fix is to replay earlier levels for cash.

> *"Playing the same mission over, and over, and over again to earn money to buy ammo to use a good
> gun in order to complete the mission is not a good gameplay mechanic."* (`25319033`)
> *"Also you have to buy ammo for your weapons? This is the first shooter I've seen do this, and I can
> see why."* (`55038865`)
> *"Do you like grinding the same level again, and again, and again, and again to eventually have
> enough money to buy enough ammo to fight a boss for 5min straight?"* (`226313875`, 2026)

🔑 **This is a ten-year-old complaint that never stopped.** It appears in every year from 2016 to 2021, and again in
2024 and 2026. The developer told a 2018 reviewer it would be fixed in the sequel (`47975155`); this
game was never changed. **A single economy rule is 9% of every bullet and 16% of every complaint about the game.**

Not checked against a primary source: the developer's 2018 reply is as the reviewer gives it.

### 3.2 ⭐ The other half of the complaints is the port

| Mode | Bullets | What it is |
|---|---|---|
| `game-feel.controls.unresponsive` | 9 | the laser room, steered by mouse drag — *"the character moves everywhere except where you want"* |
| `fairness.losses-feel-arbitrary` | 8 | hit behind cover, no warning markers, instant-kill melee |
| `controls.missing-expected-bindings` | 5 | no weapon switch, so a boss may have to be fought with the pistol |
| `platform-support.built-for-another-platform` | 4 | *"a mobile port... the HUD and all the point and click stuff instead of a proper keyboard binding"* |

**Four modes, one cause.** Four reviewers say outright that it is a phone game; the laser-room
complaint is the touch-drag control on a mouse; the missing weapon switch is a touch-UI decision. The
tree files these under four subjects because they are four different experiences, and **the reader
has to add them up to see that a port is the story.** Two more reviewers bought it thinking it was a
third-person shooter and got a phone-style rail shooter (`sold-as-a-different-kind-of-game`, 2).

## 5. For Dominion — our reading

**These are suggestions drawn from what players said, not something the reviews said.** Dominion is a
third-person, four-player co-op sci-fi PvE extraction and arena shooter built on runs, hosted on one
player's machine (listen server), in Unreal Engine 5.

1. **Never make players pay for the basics they need to finish content.**
   Evidence: 17 times it was said, 11 thumbs down, every year for ten years; 16% of all complaints.
   Dominion could: keep ammo found or refilled in the run; if ammo is a resource, never let running
   out mean grinding old content to come back; if extraction rewards buy gear, keep a free basic kit.
   Strength: medium (one small game, but the largest and longest-lasting complaint in it).
2. **Cover must protect and attacks must warn.**
   Evidence: 8 times it was said that deaths felt unfair: hit behind cover, no warning markers,
   instant-kill melee.
   Dominion could: make cover rules exact and readable; give every big enemy attack a wind-up and a
   visual or sound warning; avoid instant kills without a tell.
   Strength: medium.
3. **Players expect to switch weapons whenever they want.**
   Evidence: 5 times it was said; a boss fought with only a pistol.
   Dominion could: always allow switching and rebinding.
   Strength: weak to medium.
4. **Build controls for the platform players are on.**
   Evidence: 4 say it is a phone port, 9 say controls do not respond, all from one cause.
   Dominion could: design for keyboard and mouse and for controller from the start, with full
   rebinding.
   Strength: medium.
5. **A small, funny, hard game earns a loyal few.**
   Evidence: 5 want a sequel; funny writing 5, likeable cast 4, satisfyingly hard 5.
   Dominion could: give characters a voice and humour; it buys goodwill that bugs and roughness do not
   erase.
   Strength: weak.
6. **When a fix is promised for "the sequel", players remember.**
   Evidence: the 2018 reply (`47975155`); the complaint continued to 2026.
   Strength: weak.
