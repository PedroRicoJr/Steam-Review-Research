# Banzai Escape — English

**A census: all 80 English reviews read. No margin of error.** Steam's own English count is 51 up
and 30 down as of 2026-09-10 — one review arrived after the pull. Ours is 51 up and 29 down.

**A one-person on-rails cover shooter from 2016, ported from Android, sold at about $6.** Not a
roguelike and not a third-person shooter: a Time Crisis with a keyboard. It is the oldest game in the
block by four years, and its 80 reviews are spread across ten years — 26 of them in 2016 and never
more than eight in any year since.

## 1. ⭐ One design decision is the whole complaint: you buy your ammunition

`game-design.progression.unlock-pace.gated-behind-farming` — **17 bullets, the largest mode by a
factor of two, 11 of them thumbs down.** Money buys guns and magazines; running out mid-mission
fails it; the fix is to replay earlier levels for cash.

> *"Playing the same mission over, and over, and over again to earn money to buy ammo to use a good
> gun in order to complete the mission is not a good gameplay mechanic."* (`25319033`)
> *"Also you have to buy ammo for your weapons? This is the first shooter I've seen do this, and I can
> see why."* (`55038865`)
> *"Do you like grinding the same level again, and again, and again, and again to eventually have
> enough money to buy enough ammo to fight a boss for 5min straight?"* (`226313875`, 2026)

🔑 **This is a ten-year-old complaint that never stopped.** It appears in every year from 2016 to 2021, and again in
2024 and 2026. The developer told a 2018 reviewer it would be fixed in the sequel (`47975155`); this
game was never changed. **A single economy rule is 9% of every bullet and 16% of every complaint about the game.**

## 2. The other half of the complaints is the port

| Mode | Bullets | What it is |
|---|---|---|
| `game-feel.controls.unresponsive` | 9 | the laser room, steered by mouse drag — *"the character moves everywhere except where you want"* |
| `fairness.losses-feel-arbitrary` | 8 | hit behind cover, no warning markers, instant-kill melee |
| `controls.missing-expected-bindings` | 5 | no weapon switch, so a boss may have to be fought with the pistol |
| `platform-support.built-for-another-platform` | 4 | *"a mobile port... the HUD and all the point and click stuff instead of a proper keyboard binding"* |

**Four modes, one cause.** Four reviewers say outright that it is a phone game; the laser-room
complaint is the touch-drag control on a mouse; the missing weapon switch is a touch-UI decision. The
tree files these under four subjects because they are four different experiences, and **the reader
has to add them up to see that a port is the story.**

**Every one of the five people who say it is too short recommends it.** Five want a sequel — more,
in share, than any game in the corpus. Three reviewers in 2016 and 2017 name the developer and say the
game was *"made with passion"*; one grades it as a first game and says so.

> *"It's not the best game ever made or a GOTY candidate, but most definitely exceeded expectations.
> I'll be waiting for Episode 2!"* (`35074164`)

**A recommending reviewer writes 0.86 complaints — the lowest in the block.** The people who like
this game write short, and the people who do not write about the ammo.

## 4. What the tree learned: nothing, and that is a measurement

**66 distinct modes, the fewest of any game read. Zero unique. Zero built.** A 2016 rail shooter with
2.25 bullets per review exercised 7% of the tree and needed none of it changed.

⚠️ **Four subjects carry one cause (§2), and that is as designed — Rico's ruling, 2026-09-10.** A mode
records what the player felt, not what caused it. Working out that four complaints share one root is
the developer's job, done in house; a findings page may point it out, as §2 does, and the tags do not.

**Voice acting is 8 bullets, 5 thumbs down, and both kinds:** *"6th grade school theater level of
bad"* (`77141318`) and *"pretty good for a low cost game. Well done."* (`25319033`). The subject's
`.badly-acted` and `.well-acted` both fired on the same performances. **The property is not disputed;
the standard is.**
