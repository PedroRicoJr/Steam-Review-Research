# 末世少女 Zombie Girl — English

**A census: all 89 English reviews read. No margin of error, no month-coverage caveat.** Every English
review on the game was read, so there is no sample and no margin. Steam's own English count is 65 up
and 24 down; so is ours. Figures are as of 2026-09-10; the tables in section 2 were re-run on
2026-10-03.

**A single-player third-person zombie shooter made by one person in Unreal Engine 5, launched 2023-10,
sold at about $7, and tagged on the store with *Sexual Content*, *Nudity* and *NSFW*.** Floors are
generated by the game; about two hours long by most accounts; all voice audio is Chinese or Japanese.
Steam, all languages: 979 reviews, 499 up, 51.0% — **English is 9% of the audience.** **It is the first
of the ten small third-person games Rico cleared as one block on 2026-09-10.** It shares the
third-person camera, the shooting and Unreal Engine 5 with Dominion; it is single-player, has no
co-op, no runs in Dominion's sense, and is not sci-fi.

## 1. In plain words

**What decides how players feel is whether the store page told the truth.** 19 of the 89 reviews —
one in five — argue about the adult store tags. 12 times a reviewer said the tags promised adult
content the game does not have, and 9 of those 12 voted thumbs down. 8 times a reviewer said the tags
never promised it, and all 8 voted thumbs up. Take those 19 reviews out and the other 70 are 77.1%
thumbs up.

**The top praise is the price.** 14 times a reviewer said the price is fair, and all 14 recommend
the game.

**The top complaint is that there is too little game.** 14 times it was said, and 12 of those 14
still recommend it. Most say about two hours; one reviewer finished the story in thirteen minutes.

**People who recommend the game write most of the complaints.** 86 of the 136 complaints (63.2%)
come from thumbs-up reviews. They read like a wish list to the maker: a map, a dodge, more outfits,
more levels.

**Players grade it kindly because one person made it.** 8 reviews say so outright, 7 of them thumbs
up.

**It went from "quick updates" to "the maker has gone".** In 2023, 3 reviews praised steady updates
and 3 said the maker listens. In 2024, 2 were waiting on a promised skill tree. In 2026, 2 said
updates had stopped.

**The surprise: English speakers like it far more than everyone else.** 73.0% of English reviews are
thumbs up against 51.0% across all languages, and English is only 9% of the reviews. This page cannot
say why.

## 3. The big stories

### 3.1 ⭐ The thumb on this game is a vote on whether the store tags lied

**Nineteen of 89 reviews — one in five — argue about the adult tags, and the two sides split the thumb
almost perfectly.** (Twenty bullets; one review makes the claim twice.)

| Claim | Mode | Bullets | Thumbs up | Thumbs down |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| The tags promise adult content the game does not have | `marketing.positioning.sold-as-a-different-kind-of-game` | 12 | 3 | **9** |
| The tags never promised it; buyers did not read | `marketing.expectation-management.the-mismatch-was-the-buyers-fault` | 8 | **8** | 0 |

> *"It promises nudity, sexual content, NSFW and fails to deliver."* (`148135531`, 147 helpful — the
> most-upvoted review of the game)
> *"Game literally does not say that is has sex or nudity in it, if you read the description."*
> (`148267026`)
> *"people are judging the game because the user tags says nsfw... the 'user tags'"* (`168049101`)

🔑 **The disagreement is not about the game. It is about who wrote the tags.** Three reviewers say
outright that the tags are Steam's user tags, not the studio's. One (`163896162`) argues the game
cannot be pornographic because it is sold in Germany. **The tree can record both positions and
cannot settle which is true** — the store page as it stood in 2023 is not in the corpus.

**This is the game's entire argument.** Strip the nineteen tag reviews out and the remaining 70 are
77.1% positive with almost nothing to fight about. Beside it, 11 bullets say the main character is
designed to titillate (a neutral tag), and 4 reviewers say their thumb will flip if one thing is
fixed — one of them if more adult content is added. `findings/cross-game.md` section 15 sets this
beside FULL METAL SCHOOLGIRL as the block's two review pools that are arguments about fan service.

## 5. For Dominion — our reading

**These are suggestions drawn from what players said, not something the reviews said.** Dominion is a
third-person, four-player co-op sci-fi PvE extraction and arena shooter built on runs, hosted on one
player's machine (listen server), in Unreal Engine 5.

1. **The store page must match what is in the game.**
   Evidence: 12 times players said the store tags promised something the game does not have, 9 of
   them thumbs down; the most-helpful review (147 helpful) is this claim. 8 thumbs-up reviews defended
   it. 19 of 89 reviews (one in five) are this argument.
   Dominion could: check the store tags (including the ones players add) before launch and after; if
   a user tag is wrong, say plainly on the store page what the game is and is not.
   Strength: medium (one game, but clear and one-sided on the thumb).
2. **A short game is forgiven when the price matches.**
   Evidence: 14 say too little content, 12 of them still recommend; 14 say the price is fair, all
   thumbs up.
   Dominion could: set the launch price against the hours a team will really get out of it.
   Strength: medium.
3. **Promising a feature starts a clock.**
   Evidence: 2 reviews in 2024 waiting on a promised skill tree; by 2026, 2 say the developer left.
   Dominion could: only announce features with a date it can keep; if plans change, say so.
   Strength: weak.
4. **Show players where to go.**
   Evidence: 7 times it was said, 4 from people who still recommend it.
   Dominion could: always show the next goal and the extraction point on screen and on the map.
   Strength: weak to medium.
5. **Enemies that soak bullets read as lazy difficulty.**
   Evidence: 4 times it was said.
   Dominion could: make harder enemies harder by what they do, not only by more health; show hits
   landing.
   Strength: weak.
6. **Voice audio in the players' language matters.**
   Evidence: 2 times it was said that there are no English voices.
   Dominion could: plan which languages get voices, and make sure key callouts also show as text.
   Strength: weak.
