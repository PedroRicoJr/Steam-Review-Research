# Deep Rock Galactic: Rogue Core — English

**A sample: 814 of 7,990 English reviews read, ±3.26% on any whole-sample share** — margin of error
at 95% confidence, computed from the actual count, not the plan (Rule 12). **The plan asked for 1,298
and Steam returned 814**: its cursor stops advancing at about 120 reviews per weekly window, and the
launch month needed 966. **The sampler was fixed in round 154 and the fix applies from Redfall onward;
this group was not re-pulled.** Every figure is a photograph taken 2026-09-02, when the read finished.

**A spin-off of Deep Rock Galactic, by the same studio, in the same world, played by the same fans. A
co-op shooter built on runs: a team goes in, picks up upgrades from a pool the whole team shares, and a
timer inside the run decides when the run ends. Paid game (one reviewer complains about small paid
extras "in a paid game"). In Early Access since 2026-05-20; every review read was written inside Early
Access, in its first five months.** The camera is not stated on the pages or in the Steam store's short
description; the pages do not say who hosts a game, how many play at once, or the engine (the store API
was not queried for this rebuild). All languages: **15,210 reviews, 60.1% positive, Mixed.** **Why it was
picked: it is the controlled experiment of this study** — same studio, same universe, same players as
Deep Rock Galactic (97.1% against 70.3%), with every variable except the game held constant. It is row 1
of `GAMES-TODO.md`. **Close to Dominion on the core loop** — a co-op team, sci-fi setting, runs with
upgrades picked up along the way, and a clock that pushes the team out — but not known from the pages to
be third person, to be hosted on one player's machine, or to have an extraction or arena mode.

## 1. In plain words

**The same studio, the same world and the same fans gave the parent game 97.1% thumbs up and this game
70.3%.** That is the sample: 814 English reviews read of 7,990. Steam's own figure across all languages is lower,
60.1%. Only the game changed, so the gap is about the game.

**Players are not saying it is broken; they are saying they disagree with how it is built.** Half of
everything said (1,321 of 2,643 points) is about how the game is designed. Only 3.2% (85 points) is
about crashes, lag or speed.

**The most-praised thing is a defence of the game.** 83 times, a reviewer argues with the other reviews
instead of describing the game — about one review in ten. Next come the parent game's catchphrase and
salute (61 times) and "worth buying even unfinished" (38 times).

**The thing named most is the clock inside each run, and most people who name it still recommend the
game.** It comes up 112 times; 69 of those are on thumbs-up reviews. That share held steady between
60.7% and 62.7% the whole way through, even while the score fell.

**The plain complaints are about upgrades, luck and sharing.** 79 say the upgrades barely change
anything, 53 say luck decides whether you win, and 47 say teammates grab the upgrades you wanted. When
the studio loosened the sharing in June, two players who liked the fight over upgrades gave a thumbs
down over the change.

**The score rose, then fell.** June was the best month (74.3% thumbs up) and September the worst
(50.0%), after a July hotfix. What players wrote about patches turned sour in July, a month before the
votes followed.

**The surprise: a complaint that is loud in single reviews is tiny in the counts.** Complaints that the
cast or the world carry politics are 9 of 2,643 points. The run clock alone is 112.

## 3. ⭐ The headline number is an average of two opposite trends

**Split by the month the review was written, this game has a best month and a worst month, and they
are three months apart.**

| Review month | Thumbs up | Thumbs down | % up | `patch-quality` good / bad |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2026-05 launch | 343 | 139 | 71.2% | 24 / 5 |
| 2026-06 Update 1 | 162 | 56 | **74.3%** | 29 / 5 |
| 2026-07 hotfix 01.02 | 40 | 23 | 63.5% | 4 / 4 |
| 2026-08 | 21 | 18 | 53.8% | 2 / 2 |
| 2026-09 (partial) | 6 | 6 | **50.0%** | 2 / 2 |

**A 24-point fall from June to September.** The aggregate 70.3% is dominated by the launch month,
which is 59% of the sample.

**The order is checkable and the text moved first.** `live-ops.patch-quality` was 5.8 : 1 positive in
June and 1 : 1 in July (4.8 : 1 at launch, cross-game section 9). **The thumb rate turned in July and
kept falling.** What people wrote changed a month before how they voted. The same lead showed up at
corpus level: the bullet ratio at 300 reviews predicted the thumb rate at 500. **The text is the leading
indicator; the score is the lagging one.**

> *"Patch RC: EA: 01.02 broke the game. No one bothered to actually playtest their patch… Their first
> big patch was a great one, but they decided to absolutely decimate the game with a 'hotfix'."*
> (`229967213`)

**Checked against a primary source:** nothing. What hotfix 01.02 did is the reviewers' claim; the
patch notes were not read for this page.

## 3. ⭐ The most-named thing in the game is a disagreement, not a complaint

`game-design.session-flexibility.a-clock-decides-when-you-leave` — **112 bullets, the largest mode in
the game**, ahead of every observation about combat, art, price or progression.

| Who raised the run timer | Bullets |
|---|---|
| Reviewers who gave a thumbs **up** | **69** |
| Reviewers who gave a thumbs **down** | 43 |

**61.6% of the people who raise the timer recommend the game.** Across ten batches the split stayed
inside **60.7 – 62.7%** — the most stable measurement in this study — **and it did not move when the
thumb rate collapsed.**

> *"I also like the timer, because it actually put pressure on me"* (`226877922`)
> *"The timer makes this game impossible especially if solo"* (`227471291`)

**The mode is named for the complaint and carries a 3:2 positive majority.** A study that had tagged
the timer as a complaint would have reported the opposite of what these reviews say.

**For a designer this is the useful shape:** the feature everyone argues about is not the feature
driving the score. Per 100 reviews read the timer is **13.8** here and **0.0** in Back 4 Blood, Deep
Rock Galactic and Helldivers 2 (cross-game section 9). ⚠️ A later study of Risk of Rain 2 moved 3 of
these timer reviews to a new praise, "the clock is the thrill", after this page was first written; the
re-run of 2026-10-03 counts 109.

## 3. ⭐ The shared upgrade pool, and the constituency the loudest complaint hides

**62 bullets complain about it** — `teammates-can-take-your-things` 47 and
`the-design-sets-players-against-each-other` 15 — the most-complained-about system in the game.
Update 1 loosened it. **Two reviewers then filed thumbs down over the fix.**

> *"the devs bowed to people who play with randoms and took out all the teamwork and strategizing so
> now you just play by yourself with other people"* (`228588560`)
> *"they turned a fun team game into a solo game you can play with friends"* (`228588082`)

**`co-op-design`'s complaint share fell from 68% to 54% across the run**, entirely because of when
the reviews were written, not because the tagging changed. Per 100 reviews read the pool complaint is
5.8 here, 0.2 in Back 4 Blood and zero in Deep Rock Galactic and Helldivers 2 (cross-game section 9). The
re-run of 2026-10-03 also counts **23** bullets saying that competing for pickups *is* the fun — 4.6 per
100 at launch, 0.5 in June, 0.0 after.

## 5. For Dominion — our reading

**These are suggestions drawn from what players said, not something the reviews said.** Dominion is a
third-person, four-player co-op sci-fi PvE extraction and arena shooter built on runs, hosted on one
player's machine (listen server), in Unreal Engine 5.

1. **A run timer will be the most-argued feature, and that is fine if it is a real choice.**
   Evidence: the timer is 112 mentions, the top item; 69 of them are on thumbs-up reviews (61.6%), and
   the split never moved as the score fell. Dominion could: keep a clock as pressure, but test it alone
   and with four, since the solo complaint is the sharpest one (227471291); think about a softer clock
   for solo or smaller teams; and do not treat "people talk about the timer" as "people hate the
   timer". Strength: strong.
2. **Shared loot that teammates can grab is the biggest co-op fight - and some players love the
   fight.** Evidence: 62 complaints (47 + 15), the most of any system; loosening it in Update 1 drew two
   thumbs-down reviews from people who wanted the friction back. Dominion could: give each player their
   own pick of upgrades by default (protects games with strangers), and offer shared or contested picks
   as a friends-only or optional rule. Strength: strong on the complaint, weak (2 reviews) on the
   friction-lovers.
3. **Upgrades have to change how you play, not just add a few percent.** Evidence: 79 say upgrades
   barely change anything, 38 say whole kinds of choices are useless, against 32 praising deep builds.
   Dominion could: make each run upgrade change a behaviour (how a gun or ability works) and check that
   every option gets picked. Strength: strong.
4. **Luck must not decide the run.** Evidence: 53 say the draw decides the outcome; randomness
   complaints reach 6.6 reviews per 100, second highest in the corpus. Dominion could: let players
   re-roll, bank, or pick from several upgrade offers, and give a bad-luck floor. Strength: strong.
5. **Do not starve players of ammo, health or supplies.** Evidence: 43 say supplies run out too fast.
   Dominion could: tune resupply to the team size and to the timer, so scarcity is a choice, not a wall.
   Strength: medium.
6. **One rushed patch can undo the goodwill a good patch earned.** Evidence: patch talk 5.8 good to 1
   bad after Update 1, then 1 to 1 after hotfix 01.02; thumbs-up fell from 74.3% to 50.0%; "No one
   bothered to actually playtest their patch" (229967213). Dominion could: playtest every hotfix with a
   full team before release, and be ready to roll back. Strength: strong (clear time line), though the
   later months are small.
7. **Watch what players write, not only the score - the words turn first.** Evidence: patch talk went
   sour in July, the score turned a month later. Dominion could: read new reviews and community posts
   after every patch and act on the tone before the score moves. Strength: medium.
8. **A run-based follow-up to a loved game is judged against that game.** Evidence: 38 say another game
   does it better; 51 say the store framing promised something else; the parent's players are angry it
   was slowed for this one (227898874). Dominion could: describe runs, the timer and the shared rules
   plainly on the store page and in trailers, so no one buys the wrong game. Strength: medium.
9. **Early Access is fine if the price matches what is there.** Evidence: 38 say worth it unfinished,
   30 say wait, 44 say too little content for the price, 28 say not finished enough. Dominion could: if
   it goes into Early Access, say what is in and what is coming, and price for what is in. Strength:
   medium.
10. **A culture cannot be copied in; it has to grow.** Evidence: the parent's catchphrase shows up 28.6
    times per 100 there and 7.5 here. Dominion could: leave room for players to make their own rituals
    (emotes, a squad call-out, a shared line) instead of borrowing one. Strength: medium (one clean
    case).
11. **Players can like the studio and still dislike the game.** Evidence: highest patch praise of the
    first four games, worst scores among them. Dominion could: not read praise for fast fixes as proof
    the design is right. Strength: medium.
12. **Movement must feel quick.** Evidence: 11 reviews say moving feels slow (cross-game section 24).
    Dominion could: make sprint, dodge and climbing feel snappy, since a third-person shooter is judged
    on it. Strength: weak here (stronger across the corpus).
13. **Small, loud complaints are small.** Evidence: the cast and politics complaint is 9 of 2,643 points.
    Dominion could: triage by counts, not by how angry one forum post is. Strength: medium.

## 7. Confidence and limits

1. **English only.** 7,990 of 15,210 all-language reviews. Nothing here speaks for the other 7,220.
2. **±3.26%, not ±2.5%.** The launch month is covered at 8.0% and every other month at about 16%.
   **The under-covered month is 75% of the group**, so month-level figures for 2026-05 are the
   weakest numbers here. **Section 3's trend does not depend on them** — it depends on June through
   September, which are covered at about 16% each. Per-period margins: not counted on this page.
3. **The sample says 70.3% thumbs up; Steam says 60.1%.** The cross-game page puts the gap down to the
   launch month being under-read.
4. **The game is in early access and still changing.** Every figure is a photograph taken 2026-09-02.
   Section 3 exists because the photograph moved while it was being taken. The later months are small
   (63, 39 and 12 reviews), so the fall in score rests on few reviews per month.
5. **19.4% of these reviews were edited after posting** and the summariser flattens each one onto its
   creation date. A review written in May and rewritten in August is counted in May. (The 2026-10-03
   re-run counts 79, 9.7%, carrying an edit date; not reconciled.)
6. **Counts moved slightly between the page's date and the re-run:** bullets 2,643 → 2,654; the timer
   112 → 109 (3 moved to "the clock is the thrill" during the Risk of Rain 2 read); too little content
   44 → 42; needs more work 28 → 27. `demands-coordination` (32) was listed as a complaint on the old
   page and is filed as praise now; `sold-as-a-different-kind-of-game` (51) was marked mixed and is filed
   as a complaint now. The old figures are kept where the old page used them.
7. **Not checked against a primary source:** what Update 1 and hotfix 01.02 changed (reviewers' claims;
   patch notes not read), the parent game's update slowdown, and prices.
8. **Slurs and personal details:** none are quoted on this page; the cast-and-politics complaint is
   counted, not quoted.
9. **Reviews in other languages inside the English group:** not counted on this page.
