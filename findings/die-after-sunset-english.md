access from 2022-02 and released as 1.0 in 2023-08 at about €20.** Enemies get stronger as the sun

reviewer thanks the publisher PQube for a free copy. The engine is not recorded on this page. Steam,

— third person, a shooter, built on runs, enemies that grow stronger as the sun sets — but has no
co-op (players call that a broken promise) and no extraction.**

## 1. In plain words

**What decides how players feel is when they played.** In early access in 2022, 71% of 35 reviews were
thumbs up. In 2023, the year of full release, 40% of 10. From 2024 to 2026, 17% of 6. Players say full
release made the game worse, and then the studio left.

**The broken promise is co-op.** 5 times a reviewer said the game delivered less than was promised,
and 4 of those 5 are about co-op that never came.

**The top design complaint is movement.** 7 times a reviewer said moving is slow and clumsy — 5 of
them still recommend the game. 3 more said the dodge does not work reliably, and 4 that the guns have
no punch.

**Players say the "roguelite" part is thin.** 4 times a reviewer said upgrades barely change anything,
and 2 said it is not really a roguelite at all.

**What players like is the idea and the look.** 4 times a reviewer praised the cartoon look, and 4
times the idea of keeping the battlefield lit as the sun sets.

**Bugs grew after release.** 4 times a reviewer said a bug stopped play — a soft-lock, a first boss you
cannot reach or see — and 3 more that it is buggy.

**The surprise: the same reviewer changed sides.** One review that preferred it to Risk of Rain 2 in
2022 was edited two years later to say it is now a below-average buggy game.

## 4. What the sample says about the design itself

Counts in this section are from the tag-count grep named in section 8 unless a section above is cited.

- **Co-op and online play** — **None in the game.** Co-op was promised, players say, and never came: 4
  of the 5 broken-promise comments (section 3.2); one also asks for a horde mode. The studio's roadmap
  is not in the study, so "co-op was promised" is the reviewers' claim.
- **Combat and feel** — the guns have no punch: 4 (3 thumbs down); the shooting feels responsive: 1.
  The starting weapon is worthless and enemy shots hit at any range: part of the 2 unfair-loss
  bullets. One character is weak and slow next to the other: 2; each character plays its own way: 2.
  Paying coins to use a skill, and cooldowns not shown at the crosshair: 1. Nothing said about stuns.
- **Movement and controls** — slow and clumsy: 7 (5 from recommenders; vertical movement tiresome
  without upgrades). The dodge feels useless and delayed: 3 (all thumbs up); the double jump has to be
  bought (1) and sometimes fails. A game pad feels like aim repulsion: 1. Mouse delay with forced
  acceleration: 1. A field-of-view slider and hold-to-sprint wanted (narrow view a handicap, 1).
- **Enemies, bosses and difficulty** — hit from off screen with no direction indicator, and enemy
  shots decimate you at any range: 2. You cannot tell when you are being hit — no health warning, no
  telegraphs: 2. Enemies spawn on top of you: 1. Too few enemy types: 1. Difficulty only adds enemies:
  1. The bosses are too easy: 1; the same three bosses every time: 1; some events far harder than
  others: 1. Enemies strengthen as the sun sets — the core idea, praised 4 times as fresh. A 1.0 patch
  made the first boss invisible (`made-it-worse`, 1).
- **Progression, loot and randomness** — upgrades barely change anything (mostly elemental
  resistances): 4; not a roguelite: 2; few items and most have no effect: 1; grindy 1; shallow 1.
  Against: upgrades and missions invite different builds 2; lots of interesting powers 1; currency
  progression satisfying 1. The double jump bought with currency: 1.
- **Runs, content and replay** — little to do, three bosses, a first run beaten in an hour: 2. The
  levels look the same: 2; fun for ten to fifteen hours before the single map repeats (one
  `never-grew` bullet). Short runs fit a busy day: 1. Nothing tells you that beating the boss starts a
  new level: part of the 2 poorly-explained bullets. "The content didn't pick up much" over two years
  (`110055549`).
- **Money and price** — about €20 at full release. Too high in 2023 against better games: 1. A cash
  grab — two characters added and no fixes: 1. Refunded: 1. Good value in early access: 1. Not worth
  it yet; play the demo and wait: 2. One reviewer discloses a free copy from the publisher.
- **Tech** — bugs that stop play: 4 (a soft-lock after the pirate-base event, an unreachable first
  boss, quests that auto-fail and can freeze the game). Buggy: 3. Fell through the ground: 1; stuck on
  the scenery and wants an unstuck button: 1. Crashes many times: 1. Frame-rate lock and v-sync do
  nothing: 1. Ultrawide menus overlap: 1. Frame rate drops: 1. Against: now stable 1; undemanding 1;
  great on Steam Deck 1.
- **The studio and the community** — praised as devoted in early access; responds to bugs constantly
  (1); adds content every month (1). The developer commented on one review (`118300691`). Working on
  the wrong thing first — characters and maps before the core play: 1. Waiting for a campaign mode: 1.
  1.0 made it worse; then called abandoned (1).
- **What players asked for** — co-op (4 of 5 broken-promise comments); faster, cleaner movement and a
  reliable dodge (7 and 3); builds that matter (4); guns with punch (4); a hit warning and a direction
  indicator (2); a field-of-view slider (1); an unstuck button (1); a campaign mode (1); more content

## 5. For Dominion — our reading

**These are suggestions drawn from what players said, not something the reviews said.** Dominion is a
third-person, four-player co-op sci-fi PvE extraction and arena shooter built on runs, hosted on one
player's machine (listen server), in Unreal Engine 5.

1. **Full release must be better than early access, not worse.**
   Evidence: 71% thumbs up in early access (35 reviews), 40% in the 1.0 year (10), 17% after (6);
   *"the game at 1.0 is worse than it was in early access"*; a 1.0 patch broke the first boss.
   Dominion could: freeze features well before 1.0, test the first run and first boss above all, and
   do not ship 1.0 with new breaking changes.
   Strength: medium (small numbers, clear direction).
2. **If co-op is promised, it must ship, and early.**
   Evidence: 4 of 5 broken-promise comments are missing co-op.
   Dominion already is co-op; the lesson is to have four-player co-op and hosting working from the
   first public build, not added later.
   Strength: weak by count, high stakes for Dominion.
3. **Movement and dodge feel decide a roguelite shooter, again.**
   Evidence: slow movement 7 (5 from recommenders), unreliable dodge 3 (all recommenders), weightless
   guns 4. ArcRunner got the same complaint.
   Dominion could: make the dodge always fire when pressed, give clear hit feedback, and tune speed
   with playtests.
   Strength: medium (two games, same complaints).
4. **Builds must feel different, or players say it is not a roguelite.**
   Evidence: shallow builds 4, "not a roguelite" 2.
   Dominion could: make upgrades change how you play (new behaviour), not just small number bumps.
   Strength: weak to medium.
5. **The early-access-to-abandoned arc repeats across a studio's games.**
   Evidence: this game and ArcRunner show the same arc a year apart (the page says same studio).
   Dominion could: plan the after-launch years before launch; the block shows small studios are
   praised early and blamed later.
   Strength: medium (read with the block).
