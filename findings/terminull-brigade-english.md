## 1. In plain words

**One fault decided this game: a stutter that freezes play.** It is named 165 times, more than three
times the next real complaint. With its close neighbours (the picture speeding up and slowing down,
freezes, lag) it is 225 of 2,248 points made (10.0%).

**The fault was there the whole time.** It was named in the first month and in the last month read, and
two reviewers say it was already there in the test before launch, nine months earlier.

**Many people who name the fault still like the game.** 40 of the 165 who name the stutter recommend
it, and 18 say they will change their verdict the day it is fixed. People who named the stutter
recommend the game 25.0% of the time; people who named neither the stutter nor the free reward
recommend it 50.8% of the time.

**The best-liked things are the builds and the shooting.** Deep and varied builds are praised 27 times
(77% of those reviews are thumbs up); weighty shooting 18 times. The game is free, so 13 say it is easy
to try, and 13 like that the paid extras only change looks.

**An outside paid-install deal brought in 90 reviews, and 76 of them never played.** Taking every one of
them out moves the score only from 40.98% to 43.3%. The deal is not why the game scores 41%.

**It got worse with time.** Every review from February to September 2026 was read: 62 reviews, 32.3%
thumbs up, against 41.0% for the whole read. "The idea is good, the content will come" fell from 28
times in 2025 to 3 in 2026.

**The surprise: only the most loyal players saw the biggest change.** Six reviewers say the second
season swapped the run mode for gear farming. Four of them had played more than 70 hours; the typical
reviewer had played about five.

## 3. ⭐ This corpus holds three different populations and one score

Splitting all 737 reviews by what they named:

| Group | n | Share | % up |
|---|---|---|---|
| Named stutter, freezing or a frozen machine, **not** the reward | **188** | 25.5% | **25.0%** |
| Named the outside reward, **not** the stutter | 80 | 10.9% | **25.0%** |
| Named both | 10 | 1.4% | 20.0% |
| Named **neither** | 459 | 62.3% | **50.8%** |

**This split was measured after every batch from 400 onward and it never moved.** Across nine
batches the stutter group stayed between 24.7% and 27.3% of the pool, and its approval stayed between
25.0% and 29.0%.

🔴 **The 50.8% is not the game's score and must never be printed as one.** It is what is left after
removing two effects, and it exists to size those effects — not to describe the game.

**What the table does say:** the game's headline number is built from a large indifferent middle that
approves at about half, dragged down by two roughly equal-sized groups that approve at a quarter.

## 3. ⭐ The stutter is the corpus

`engineering.performance.stutter` is **165 bullets** — more than three times the next real complaint
(`production.content-variety.repetitive`, 50). Add its neighbours and the cluster is
`stutter` 165 + `unstable-framerate` 23 + `freezes-or-hangs` 17 + `netcode.lag-and-desync` 20 =
**225 of 2,248 (10.0%)**. It is the largest single complaint of any game in the study, 7.3% of
everything said about this game (cross-game section 12).

✅ **24% of the people reporting it recommend the game anyway.** 40 of 165. That is the single most
important number in this corpus, and it is easy to miss:

> *"Besides the stutter issuees its a reeally fun rogue like game"* · *"lotta stuttering but kinda fun
> nonetheless"* · *"Excellent free roguelike 3PS, once they fix those lag spikes!"* · *"The bloody
> Stuttering in this game. Otherwise good."*

**The complaint is not a proxy for disliking the game.** A studio counting only thumbs would read 165
stutter reports as 165 rejections. **Forty of them are recommendations with a condition attached.**
18 reviews say outright that the verdict flips the day it is fixed. `234031384` names the cost of not
answering him:

> *"tell me when you fix the stutters, or at least drop some english patch notes... so that i can
> better know when that deal braking problem for this otherwise good game is fixed"*

**He is willing to come back and cannot find out when to.**

### It was there in month one and it was there in the last month read

|---|---|---|---|
| 2025-07 | 50 | 7 | 14.0% |
| 2025-08 | 424 | 96 | 22.6% |
| 2025-09 | 45 | 21 | **46.7%** |
| 2025-12 | 97 | 12 | **12.4%** |

⚠️ **December is not a recovery.** 33 of December's 97 sampled reviews are reward arrivals with zero

### Nobody agrees what causes it, including the studio

Reviewers name at least four different causes: the servers, frame rate tied to network delay, the
netcode, and the studio's own statement that it is server-side. `202002088` reports the studio saying
so and adds the mechanic: the stutter freezes the whole world at once, *"so it does not put anyone at
a disadvantage."* **The tree files each claimed cause on its own mode and picks no winner** — the
corpus cannot settle a technical question.

`218520118` and `217246854` both say it has been there since the public test. **Nine months.**

**Checked against a primary source:** nothing. The causes and the studio's statement are reviewers'
accounts.

## 3. ⭐ The outside-reward campaign put 90 reviews in the corpus, and 76 of them never played

`marketing.discovery.installed-it-to-claim-an-outside-reward` is **90 bullets** — the second largest
mode in the group. The game ran an outside-reward campaign: a chat platform paying its users to install
and play fifteen minutes.

| | n | % up | Median hours | Zero-hour |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Reward reviews | 90 | **24.4%** | **0** | **76** |
| Everyone else | 647 | **43.3%** | 5 | 148 |

**Removing every reward review moves the group from 40.98% to 43.3%** — about **2.3 points** — and the
praise-to-complaint balance from 0.36 to 1 only to 0.37 to 1 (cross-game section 12).

🔴 **That is the honest size of the effect, and it is smaller than it looks.** The campaign is visible,
it is measurable, and **it is not why this game scores 41%.** A studio that blames its score on the
reward crowd is wrong by roughly nine tenths.

## 3. ⭐ The season replaced the game, and only the people who played most could see it

`live-ops.patch-quality.replaced-the-core-loop-with-a-different-one` was built in round 210 on four
reviews and finished the run with six:

| Review | Hours | Thumb |
|---|---|---|
| `220803108` | **402** | down |
| `224900562` | **147** | down |
| `219464772` | **102** | down |
| `219828133` | **73** | down |
| `216530497` | 13 | down |
| `213157491` | 4 | up |

**Four of the six played more than seventy hours. The median for the whole group is five.**

> *"In S2, they replaced the run-based format with a build-based format where you play too-short
> levels with a fixed build to farm gear. The whole gameplay loop that made the game fun for me is
> gone — it's not the same game any more."* — `219464772`, 102 hours

> *"The first season you could equip the gear you've earned and play the now 'classic' mode with it...
> Now the gear you earn can only be used in the repetitive modes."* — `220803108`, 402 hours

🔴 **Nobody at zero hours raises it.** You have to have been there before to notice that the game was
replaced. **The people who can tell a studio this are the last people it loses.** The signal arrives
as six reviews against 165 about a stutter — so a bullet count buries it. Weigh it by playtime or you
will not see it at all.

✅ **A script separated this from its lookalike and the lookalike is more common.** The same search
returned Back 4 Blood measured against Left 4 Dead and Rogue Core measured against Deep Rock Galactic
— **both compare this game to a different game, not to its own past.**

## 3. ⭐ The spying fear, and the reviewers who checked

`publishing.data-and-privacy.suspected-of-spying` carries 21 bullets — administrator prompts, an
anti-cheat driver, accusations of spyware and currency mining. Three reviewers went and checked, and
they say the opposite:

> *"people claiming that it's trying at access the Runescape launcher don't know how to check what's
> going on. It asks for admin to access its own launcher, which is unfortunately names
> 'rs_launcher.exe'. If you actually look at the details you can see that it is their own launcher
> inside of their own directory."* — `214226524`

⚠️ **Both counts are worth having and they are not the same count.** 21 bullets record what players
fear. Three record what someone found when they looked. **A count of the fear belongs next to a count
of what was found, and never inside it.** A normal Windows elevation prompt with an unhelpfully named
executable produced twenty-one accusations. **Naming a file badly is a support cost.**

## 5. For Dominion — our reading

**These are suggestions drawn from what players said, not something the reviews said.** Dominion is a
third-person, four-player co-op sci-fi PvE extraction and arena shooter built on runs, hosted on one
player's machine (listen server), in Unreal Engine 5.

1. **A single unfixed performance fault can become the whole game.** Evidence: stutter 165 times, 225
   with its neighbours (10.0% of all points), from public test to the last month; stutter-namers
   recommend the game 25.0% of the time against 50.8% for those who named neither. Dominion could: treat
   hitches and freezes as a launch blocker; test a full four-player match with the host on a mid-range
   machine, since a listen server puts the host's frame rate on everyone; hunt hitches (shader
   compiling, loading, garbage collection, network spikes) before content. Strength: strong.
2. **Players who report a fault are often fans waiting to come back - tell them when it is fixed.**
   Evidence: 40 of 165 stutter reviews are thumbs up; 18 say the verdict flips on the fix; 234031384
   asks only to be told. Dominion could: keep a public known-issues list and write short, plain patch
   notes that say "fixed: X". Strength: strong.
3. **Know and say where a hitch comes from.** Evidence: reviewers name four causes and the studio a
   fifth; no one knows. Dominion could: build in its own timing and network logs so the team can say
   whether a hitch is the host, the client or the network, and tell players. Strength: medium.
4. **Do not replace the loop players came for; add beside it.** Evidence: 6 reviewers, 4 with over 70
   hours, 5 thumbs down, say Season 2 swapped runs for gear farming and locked earned gear out of the old
   mode. Dominion could: keep the run mode as the home mode, let earned gear or unlocks work in every
   mode, and weigh feedback by hours played after any big change. Strength: medium (few reviews, but the
   most committed players).
5. **Do not pay for installs.** Evidence: 90 reward reviews, 76 at zero hours, 24.4% up; the second wave
   was 9.1% up with 32 of 33 at zero hours. Dominion could: skip "install and play 15 minutes" deals;
   if it runs promotions, aim them at people who will play. Strength: strong.
6. **But do not blame the score on outsiders.** Evidence: removing every reward review moves the score
   only 2.3 points. Dominion could: always check how much a suspected cause really moves the number
   before acting on it. Strength: strong.
7. **A crossover is a door, not a fix.** Evidence: 14 crossover reviews, 8 up and 6 down; 3 say a
   crossover on top of an unfixed fault reads as choosing marketing over repair. Dominion could: run
   collaborations or events only after the main faults are fixed. Strength: weak to medium.
8. **Name every file and permission prompt plainly.** Evidence: 21 spyware fears from an admin prompt,
   an anti-cheat driver and a launcher file with a confusing name; 3 found it was harmless. Dominion
   could: name its files after the game, avoid admin prompts, explain any anti-cheat on the store page.
   Strength: medium.
9. **Builds and shooting are what players will praise if the game runs.** Evidence: deep builds 27
   (77% up), shooting weight 18 (66% up). Dominion could: keep run builds deep and hits weighty; they
   were the strongest praise even here. Strength: medium.
10. **Do not let the game read as a copy.** Evidence: 19 call it a copy of an older game (21% up), 15
    send people to a rival (13% up); when players name other games just to describe it, 76% are up.
    Dominion could: have one clear thing of its own (for example extraction plus arena runs in a
    listen-server team) and lead with it. Strength: medium.
11. **Matchmaking must find a game even when few are online.** Evidence: 21 say they could not find a
    game. Dominion could: let players host and start alone or with bots, and fill games as people join.
    Strength: medium.
12. **Shop praise and shop complaints can live side by side.** Evidence: 13 praise looks-only extras,
    yet shop complaints run 13.2 reviews per 100. Dominion could: if it sells looks, keep prices fair and
    never tie them to play. Strength: medium.
13. **Watch the late years, not only the lifetime score.** Evidence: 2026 reads 32.3% up against 41.0%
    lifetime; "the potential is still there" fell from 28 to 3. Dominion could: track recent reviews and
    hope-type comments as an early sign that players are giving up. Strength: medium.
14. **Screen motion can make players sick.** Evidence: 2 reviewers report motion sickness or headache.
    Dominion could: offer camera shake and blur toggles and a field-of-view slider. Strength: weak.
15. **Loot nobody wants is a chore.** Evidence: 2 reviewers complain about sorting worthless drops.
    Dominion could: auto-sell or auto-skip junk. Strength: weak.

## 7. Confidence and limits

### The sampling limit

| Month | Real | Read | Share | Weight |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **2025-07** | **578** | 50 | **8.7%** | **×11.6** |
| **2025-08** | **1,615** | 424 | 26.3% | ×3.8 |
| 2025-09 | 129 | 45 | 34.9% | ×2.9 |
| 2025-12 | 294 | 97 | 33.0% | ×3.0 |
| 2026-02 → 2026-09 | 62 | 62 | **100%** | ×1.0 |

**The two largest months are the two least-read, and they are the launch.** 2025-07 and 2025-08 hold
**2,193 of the 2,818 reviews — 78% of the whole population** — and together they were read at 21.6%.

✅ **The weighting barely moves the answer.** Sample 40.98%, Rule 6 weighted 40.63% — **0.35 points
apart.** The under-read months are not hiding a different game.

⚠️ **A single observation in 2025-07 counts for 11.6.** Any share below about 1% in the weighted table
can be produced by two or three reviews in that one month. **Use the raw counts for small modes and
the weighted shares only for large ones.**

### What this corpus cannot tell you

- **Why the stutter happens.** Reviewers name four causes and the studio names a fifth. **The corpus
  records the claims and settles none of them.**
- **How many people play.** Four reviewers across five weeks put the live count between 100 and 657;
  `202932618` gives 466 now, 657 peak, just under 6,000 at launch. **These are player-reported
  figures from an outside tracker, not the studio's.** ⚠️ Sentences about population must lean on the
  **matchmaking** count (`cannot-find-games`, 21 bullets), which is what players actually experienced,
  not the population count.
- **Whether the launch months are like the rest.** 2025-07 was read at 8.7% and carries a ×11.6
  weight. The weighting agrees with the sample to 0.35 points, which is evidence and not proof.
- **What the non-English reviewers said.** 2,855 of the 5,673 all-language reviews are not English and
  none were read.
- 🔴 **One review in the sample is about a different game.** `229578207` carries 1,470 hours, a garbled
  spelling of this game's name, and a body describing a souls-like with a stamina bar and a
  seventy-dollar price. **Only its two stutter sentences were summarised.** It was caught by reading
  it, not by any check in the pipeline — **so the rate at which this happens is unknown.**
- **Whether free-to-play itself scores badly.** The comparison group is paid games; nothing in this
  corpus separates the model from the game.
- **Per-period margins:** not counted on this page.

### Other limits

- **46 reviews (6.2%) have more than one date**, each counted on the created date.
- **The December reward figure was printed wrong twice** (5.3%, then 10.3%, then 9.1%); the gap between
  waves is the safe finding.
- **Counts moved slightly between the page's date and the re-run:** bullets 2,248 → 2,250; reward
  installs 90 → 89; spying fears 21 → 18; repetitive 50 → 48; "judged unfairly" 19 → 18; "the loop was
  replaced" 6 → 7. The old figures are kept where the old page used them.
- **Hours:** the page gives the whole group's median as five; the grep of 2026-10-03 gives four. Not
  reconciled.
- **Not checked against a primary source:** the stutter's cause, the studio's server-side statement,
  player counts, the reward campaign's terms, the season change, prices.
- **Some numbers come from other games' pages** (shop complaints 13.2 per 100 from The First
  Descendant's section; zero-hour share 30.4% from Aliens: Fireteam Elite's page).
- **Slurs and personal details:** none are quoted; two health reports (motion sickness, headache) are
  given only as a count with review ids.
- **Reviews in other languages inside the English group:** not counted on this page.
