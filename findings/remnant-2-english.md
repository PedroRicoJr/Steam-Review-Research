# Remnant II — English

**A sample: 1,382 of 38,120 English reviews read, ±3.27% on any whole-sample share.** Grid month-sum
38,120 against Steam's own English total of 38,117 on the day the row was added — the grid matches
Steam to within 3 reviews. Figures are as of 2026-09-13 unless marked as today's script run
(2026-10-03, section 8).

**A third-person co-op souls-like shooter from Gunfire Games, published by Gearbox (the reviews later
name Arc Games), released 2023-07-25 at full price.** The sequel to *Remnant: From the Ashes* (2019):
procedurally assembled worlds, three DLCs the reviews name (*The Awakened King*, *The Forgotten
Kingdom*, *The Dark Horizon*), dual archetypes, and secrets. Worlds are put together by the computer
from set pieces, so two players may see different areas, bosses and secrets. Co-op is hosted by one
player, who keeps the campaign; others join as guests (the guest complaints in section 4 show this).
The largest group size and the engine are not given on this page. 68,559 reviews in all languages,
82.3% positive, *Very Positive*. **The third game in the large-games queue and the game every Immortal:
Unchained review named.** How close to Dominion (from `DOMINION-TAKEAWAYS.md`): very close on the
outside — third-person, co-op, sci-fi/fantasy shooter, and one player hosts, so its online complaints
are the ones Dominion is most likely to meet. It differs in being a long campaign with checkpoints
rather than runs, having no extraction, and being hard in the "souls" way.

## 1. In plain words

**A sequel that people accept as a sequel, play with friends, and cannot finish without a wiki.**
83.6% of the sampled reviews are thumbs up.

**The two most common specific remarks tie at 139 each:** *this is a worthy follow-up to the first
game* and *much better with friends* (each 10.1 per 100 reviews). The first fades over the years (12.7
→ 4.7 per 100); the second grows (7.3 → 12.4). By 2026 one review in eight says *play it with
friends*.

**The top complaint is that the best things are hidden so well you need a guide** — 61 times, 4.4 per
100, and **46 of the 61 still recommend the game**. It is the one complaint that rises every year:
2.6 per 100 in the launch quarter, 10.0 in 2026. Add the item that never drops (24), the dice roll that
decides which areas you ever see (7), rewards that need a second player (3) and a fan-made tool to beat
the dice (2), and **the content the game hides is about seven complaints per 100 reviews — the biggest
group of complaints.**

**The second complaint is the first game.** 53 say this one falls short of it — 30 of them thumbs
down — against 139 who accept the sequel. **For every reviewer who says the first was better, 2.6 say
this one is better.** The same features come up on both sides: armour set bonuses (seven reviews) and
a cap on character upgrades that the first game did not have (nine). The most-helpful review in the
sample is three words — *"uncap trait points"*, 174 votes.

**Who complains changed, the game did not.** The thumbs up fell from 86.5% in the launch quarter to
79.6% in 2025, 82.4% in 2026; complaints per 100 rose from 67 to 91 while praise held at 104 to 126.
Launch players had machines that struggled; 2024 players missed the first game; 2025–26 players could
not find the hidden classes.

**The tech complaint is not crashes, it is a PC that cannot run it.** Needs a strong PC (17), only runs
right with upscaling on (14), lowering the settings does not help (7), only stable with the processor
slowed down (2), nine reviews that never got past the launcher (8 thumbs down, median 2 hours), and
twelve lost saves. These fade to almost nothing by 2026.

**The surprise: the only character players speak of with love is the dog.** Eleven remarks about the
world reacting to you are almost all one class's pet dog — *"you get a dog which you can pet"*.

## 5. For Dominion — our reading

**These are suggestions drawn from what players said, not something the reviews said.** Dominion is a
third-person, four-player co-op sci-fi PvE extraction and arena shooter built on runs, hosted on one
player's machine (listen server), in Unreal Engine 5.

1. **Hide secrets, but never hide the core: let players see in the game what is left to find.**
   Evidence: hidden behind a guide 61 (4.4 per 100, rising to 10.0 in 2026), 46 of them from fans; the
   one ask for an in-game list (`182187651`). Dominion could: keep secrets for cosmetics and lore; put
   every class, perk and major item on an in-game codex that shows "not yet found" and hints where;
   never gate a class on a hidden puzzle. Strength: **strong**.
2. **Do not let the dice decide whether a player ever sees content or gets a key item.** Evidence:
   never drops 24; roll decides content 7; a third-party tool to beat the reroll 2; a buddy grinding two
   weeks for a ring. Dominion could: use bad-luck protection and "pity" counters; let players choose or
   pin a map type or boss for a run; show drop sources. Strength: **medium**.
3. **Never make upgrading gear make the game harder.** Evidence: upgrading makes the game harder 9;
   *"you're better off not upgrading"*; levelling up changes nothing 3; a new weapon restarts the grind
   6. Dominion could: scale run difficulty by the chosen tier, not by the player's gear; make every
   upgrade felt in play. Strength: **medium**.
4. **Do not cap the thing that makes builds fun.** Evidence: *"uncap trait points"*, 174 votes, the
   most-helpful review; 9 trait-cap reviews; armour set bonuses missed 7. Dominion could: let players
   max a build's key stats over time; if there is a cap, let it be raised. Strength: **medium**.
5. **Build-making is the praise; protect it.** Evidence: deep builds 98; each class plays its own way
   12; two classes at once named as the thing the sequel added; firing range and loadout slots thanked
   after 4 asked. Dominion could: offer class combos or sub-classes, a firing range in the hub, and
   saved loadouts from day one. Strength: **strong**.
6. **The guest must never lose out to the host.** Evidence: a guest can decide your campaign 3; only
   the host keeps progress 2; the guest fights at a disadvantage 3; a guest cannot keep DLC loot; no
   ping shown before joining. Dominion could: give every player their own progress and loot from a run;
   test dodge and hit timing on the client, not only the host; show ping before joining; never let one
   player's choice lock another's progress. Strength: **medium** (small counts, but this is exactly
   Dominion's listen-server setup).
7. **Do not make a downed player watch for minutes.** Evidence: a dead player spectates until the next
   checkpoint 3 (sometimes a whole boss). Dominion could: allow revives, a timed respawn or a way to help
   while down. Strength: **weak**.
8. **Take control away from the player as little as possible, and make every attack readable.**
   Evidence: one-hit kills 11; stuns; attacks beyond their visible reach 3; baited dodges 2; boss hiding
   behind adds 3; slow healing 3; stamina 2; no parry 2; the final fight cannot be read 7, with music
   drowning the cues. Dominion could: telegraph big hits, match hitboxes to visuals, keep stuns short,
   mix boss music under attack sounds. Strength: **medium**.
9. **Test low-end PCs early and make settings matter.** Evidence: needs a strong PC 17; only right
   with upscaling 14; lowering settings does not help 7; slowed processor 2; will not start 9 (8 thumbs
   down, median 2 hours). Dominion could: set a minimum spec and test against it, make each setting
   visibly lighten the load, support upscalers but do not require them, catch launch failures.
   Strength: **medium** (many reviews; the pages do not name the engine, so whether Dominion would meet
   the same load is not shown here).
10. **Never lose a player's save.** Evidence: lost saves 12; the save bug a reviewer says went a year
    unfixed; no cloud save. Dominion could: use cloud saves and backups; never let a guest or cheater
    write the host's save. Strength: **medium**.
11. **Give players a way to talk.** Evidence: no voice, no text, only a ping — 11, the top community
    complaint. Dominion could: ship voice chat, text chat and a rich ping wheel. Strength: **medium**.
12. **Spend on the world, keep the story short.** Evidence: world worth exploring 92 and places with
    mood 20, against a thin story 47, the ending 14, flat characters 12 and the game talks too much;
    story worth following only 23. Dominion could: tell the story through places and short lines; do not
    make players stand still to listen. Strength: **medium**.
13. **A companion players can pet earns affection cheaply.** Evidence: the dog — 11, the only
    character named with love. Dominion could: give a class or the hub a creature or drone players can
    interact with. Strength: **weak**.
14. **Keep levels moving.** Evidence: one area drags the rest down 7; slow lifts 4; invisible walls 3;
    jumping sections 3; hard to use map 13 (no compass on the minimap). Dominion could: cut long lifts
    and doors from runs; give the minimap compass directions; avoid platforming. Strength: **medium**.
15. **Price for a sale world, and keep DLC fair.** Evidence: buy on sale 20, steady every year; price

## 7. Confidence and limits

1. **This is a 3.6% sample, not a census.** ±3.27% on a whole-sample share; wider on any month; 32 of
   the 39 months are the 20-review floor and a single review moves 2026-09 by ten points. Per-period
   margins are not counted on this page.
2. **The launch quarter is a third of the sample and half the game.** 465 of 1,382 reviews are
   2023-07 to 09. Anything said about 2025 or 2026 rests on 240 and 170 reviews. The five-period
   table is the honest resolution.
3. **The launch week is under-sampled.** 2023-07 is 70 of 7,906 (0.89%) against 3.5% elsewhere. The
   grid split July into four weekly windows of 70; the first three end before the 2023-07-25 launch
   and returned nothing, and that is the whole gap between the planned 1,602 and the 1,382 read. The
   launch week's thumb (82.9%) rests on 70 reviews.
4. **79 reviews are dated by creation, not by their last edit.** The edits run both ways here too
   (*"tried again. Awful game"* and *"changed this to a recommend"*); the direction of the error is
   unknown.
5. **No patch notes were pulled.** The reviews name the three DLCs, the prism system, the boss rush
   mode, the loadout slots and the region filter as later additions; this page cannot confirm any of
   them from the studio's side, and cannot say whether the wiki problem was ever addressed, or whether
   the save corruption was fixed. The tree records what the player felt, not the root cause.
6. **One language.** About 30,000 reviews in other languages are unpulled — and twelve of the 1,382
   "English" reviews were not in English.
7. **21% of bullets are `unknown`**, 396 of them a wordless thumbs up — between Space Marine 2's 19%
   and ARC Raiders' 31%. The direction audit found 0 disagreements and 0 unfitted observations across
   all 28 batches.
8. **Jabs.** One defence of the hidden secrets calls other players a name; it is cut from the quote.
9. **Counts moved after this page was first written.** Today's script run differs by a bullet or two
   in several rows (section 2 notes each); the cross-game recount gives 121.6 praise and 77.5
   complaints per 100 and 1.57 : 1, against this page's 1.60 : 1.
