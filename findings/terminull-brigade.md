### Why this game was in the queue

**It is the only failed third-person-shooter roguelike with enough reviews to sample.** The corpus
already held co-op horde shooters (Back 4 Blood, The Anacrusis, Deep Rock Galactic, Helldivers 2)
and one controlled spin-off (Rogue Core). This adds the shape Dominion is closest to — run-based,
third person, ability-driven, four players — in its failing state.

## 1. The short version

**One fault decided this game: a stutter that freezes play, named 165 times** — more than three times
the next real complaint, and one point in ten with its close neighbours.

**A quarter of the people who report it still recommend the game** (40 of 165), and 18 say their
verdict flips the day it is fixed.

**A paid install deal brought 90 reviews, 76 from people who never played** — but removing them all
moves the score only 2.3 points.

**The latest reviews are the unhappiest:** every 2026 review from February on was read, and only 32.3%
are thumbs up, against 41.0% overall.

**The best-liked things are deep builds (27) and weighty shooting (18).**

## 5. ⭐ The finding

**One unfixed technical fault can become the whole conversation, and a quarter of the people
reporting it are still trying to recommend you.**

`engineering.performance.stutter` is **165 bullets — more than three times the next real complaint.**
Add its neighbours and one performance cluster is **10% of every observation in the corpus**. It was
named in the first month, it was named in the last month read fourteen months later, and two
reviewers say it was there in the public test before launch.

✅ **40 of those 165 reviews are thumbs up.**

> *"Besides the stutter issuees its a reeally fun rogue like game"* · *"lotta stuttering but kinda
> fun nonetheless"* · *"The bloody Stuttering in this game. Otherwise good."*

**The lesson is not "performance matters."** It is that **a single unanswered fault converts your
supporters into a queue of conditional recommendations, and every one of them is a customer you
already won and are holding at the door.** 18 reviews say outright that the verdict flips the day it
is fixed. `234031384` names the cost of not answering him:

> *"tell me when you fix the stutters, or at least drop some english patch notes... so that i can
> better know when that deal braking problem for this otherwise good game is fixed"*

**He is willing to come back and cannot find out when to.**

### Three specific transfers to Dominion

#### 1. Buying installs does not buy players, and the second campaign is worth a quarter of the first

The game ran an outside-reward campaign — a chat platform paying its users to install and play
fifteen minutes. It put **90 reviews into this corpus**, and **76 of them show zero hours played.**

| Wave | n | % up | Zero-hour |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2025-08 | 41 | **36.6%** | 31 of 41 |
| 2025-12 | 33 | **9.1%** | **32 of 33** |

**Same reward, same game, four months apart, twenty-seven points apart.** By December almost nobody
who took the reward started the game.

⚠️ **And the campaign is not why the game scores 41%.** Removing every reward review moves the group
from 40.98% to 43.3% — **2.3 points.** A studio blaming its score on the reward crowd would be wrong
by about nine tenths.

**The transfer:** a paid install push writes reviews from people with no experience of the game, it
degrades sharply on repetition, and **it is loud enough to distract a studio from a fault that costs
four times as much.**

#### 2. Replacing the core loop is invisible to everyone except the people you lose last

Six reviewers say a season update swapped the shape of play — a run-based roguelite replaced by
gear farming. **Their playtimes: 4, 13, 73, 102, 147 and 402 hours.** The group median is **five**.

> *"they replaced the run-based format with a build-based format... The whole gameplay loop that made
> the game fun for me is gone — it's not the same game any more."* — `219464772`, 102 hours

🔴 **Nobody at zero hours raises it.** You have to have been there before to notice the game changed
underneath you. **The signal arrives only from the accounts a studio most wants to keep, and it
arrives as six reviews against 165 about a stutter — so a bullet count buries it.**

**The transfer for a live game:** when you change the loop, the complaint volume will not tell you.
**Weigh it by playtime or you will not see it at all.**

#### 3. A crossover brings people in and tells you nothing about what they found

`marketing.discovery.came-for-a-crossover-with-something-i-already-like` was built in round 209 after
a script found a crossover named in **25 reviews across five games**. In this game it carries **14
bullets: 8 thumbs up, 6 down.**

**The mode had to be neutral, and the corpus is what settled that.** The crossover is a door, not a
verdict.

⚠️ **It also has a cost the corpus records.** Three reviewers address the *licence holder* rather than
the studio and say lending the name was a mistake:

> *"the fact these devs decided to do a crossover without fixing the biggest thing that so many
> negatives are pointing it out is just bad"* — `217246854`

**A crossover on top of an unfixed fault reads as choosing marketing over repair**, and reviewers say
so in exactly those words.

### The counterexample worth holding

**The corpus contains its own correction, and the corpus is where a studio would find it.**

`publishing.data-and-privacy.suspected-of-spying` carries 21 bullets — administrator prompts, an
anti-cheat driver, accusations of spyware and currency mining. Three reviewers went and checked, and
they say the opposite:

> *"people claiming that it's trying at access the Runescape launcher don't know how to check what's
> going on. It asks for admin to access its own launcher, which is unfortunately names
> 'rs_launcher.exe'. If you actually look at the details you can see that it is their own launcher
> inside of their own directory."* — `214226524`

⚠️ **Both counts are worth having and they are not the same count.** 21 bullets record what players
fear. Three record what someone found when they looked. **A count of the fear belongs next to a count
of what was found, and never inside it** — and the studio's job is the fear, whatever the facts are.

**The transfer:** a normal Windows elevation prompt with an unhelpfully named executable produced
twenty-one accusations. **Naming a file badly is a support cost.**

## 6. What this page cannot tell you

- **It is not evidence that free-to-play games score badly.** The comparison group is six paid games.
  This one's zero-hour share, playtime distribution and population complaints are all shaped by
  costing nothing, and **nothing in this corpus separates the model from the game.**
- **It is not evidence about why the stutter happens.** Reviewers name at least four causes — the
  servers, frame rate tied to network delay, the netcode, and the studio's own server-side statement.
  **The tree files each claim on its own mode and picks no winner.**
- **It is not a clean read of the launch.** 78% of the English population sits in July and August
  2025, and those two months were read at 21.6%. ✅ The Rule 6 weighting agrees with the raw sample to
  **0.35 points**, which is evidence and not proof.
- 🔴 **It is not a clean sample in one respect that no check would catch.** `229578207` carries 1,470
  hours and a body describing **a different game entirely** — a souls-like with a stamina bar and a
  seventy-dollar price — with two stutter sentences bolted on. Only those two sentences were
  summarised. **It was caught by reading it, so the rate at which this happens is unknown.**
- **English only:** 2,855 other-language reviews are unread.
- **Player counts are reviewers' claims** from an outside tracker, not checked.
- **The ranked lists are a re-run of 2026-10-03;** a few counts differ from the English page's
  2026-09-03 figures (spying fears 18 against 21, judged unfairly 18 against 19).

### Tree contribution

The Terminull Brigade run spanned **rounds 197–211**, built **28 modes** across fourteen batch
sections, and closed **14 open gaps** — several of them opened during earlier games.

**The one worth naming is gap 115, which The Anacrusis run flagged as a fault in the tree itself:**
three modes recorded a player put off by a game's politics and **none recorded a player naming the
same content as a reason they liked it.** This run built
`narrative.world-and-setting.politics-drew-me-in`. **The tree can now hold both sides of that
argument.**

**Two subject-level questions this run raised and could not answer** — both are Rico's, both are
written up in `tag-tree-open-gaps.md`:

- **`accessibility` has no subject for a physical reaction to how the picture moves.** Two reviewers
  report motion sickness and headache from the stutter. Motor, vision, hearing, phobia, trauma,
  addiction, self-harm and mental-health-portrayal do not hold it.
- **There is no `game-design.loot` subject.** Two reviewers complain about sorting worthless drops.
  `build-and-customisation` covers what the player **chooses**, not what the game **hands** them.
  Both bullets sit on an interface mode, **which reads as two interface complaints and is not what
  either reviewer said.**

**And one method finding that is not about this game.** Every mode built after round 204 was checked
against the corpus with a script first, and **the script disagreed with the eye four times**:

| Round | The eye said | The script found |
|---|---|---|
| 205 | four sightings of a searching complaint | one — the other three were correctly tagged on their own faults |
| 208 | four copied reviews | five across four months, and the earliest is the original |
| 209 | "the beta was better" was a build | five hits, **two say the opposite** |
| 210 | "the loop was replaced" was a build | five hits, **two compare this game to a different game** |

🔑 **The word count is not the sighting count, and the gap between them is usually more than half.**
