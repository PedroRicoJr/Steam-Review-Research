## 1. In plain words

**What decides the game's public face is a fight about fan service.** 12 reviews say the game hides
what is under the skirts with a black shadow and call that censorship; 9 of them vote thumbs down, and
those 12 reviews carry 52% of every helpful vote on the game. 11 reviews say the buyers were wrong to
expect more, and all 11 vote thumbs up.

**The top complaint is the price.** 22 times a reviewer said $49.99 is too much for what it is, and 14
of those 22 still recommend it. 10 more said wait for a sale. 7 reviewers paid bundle prices and found
it worth that.

**The top praise is the jokes, the shooting and that it runs.** 9 times a reviewer said the writing and
characters are funny, 6 that the shooting hits hard, 6 that it is relaxing, and 6 that it runs
smoothly — every one of them thumbs up.

**The main design complaint is the floor key that gets used up.** 9 times a reviewer said the keys that
let you skip floors are spent when used, so a second death costs more than the first. 8 said runs are
too long — two to seven hours — and 3 more said you cannot save and come back.

**People who recommend it write half the complaints.** 47.5% of the complaints are on thumbs-up
reviews. The usual recommendation is: it is overpriced, it is repetitive, and I liked it.

**It got calmer after launch.** Complaints fell from 175.0 per 100 reviews in the launch quarter to
136.4 in early 2026, and a bug that changed your aim speed near traps was fixed.

**The surprise: a buyer who sorts by "most helpful" sees almost nothing about how it plays.** The seven
most-helpful English reviews are all the censorship claim, and five of them are under ten words.

## 5. For Dominion — our reading

**These are suggestions drawn from what players said, not something the reviews said.** Dominion is a
third-person, four-player co-op sci-fi PvE extraction and arena shooter built on runs, hosted on one
player's machine (listen server), in Unreal Engine 5.

1. **A few short, angry reviews can become the whole store page.**
   Evidence: 12 reviews carry 52% of all helpful votes; the seven most-helpful are one claim.
   Dominion could: find out before launch what one-line complaint could top the store page, and
   either fix it or address it plainly on the page.
   Strength: medium (one game, but the numbers are stark).
2. **Do not make the reward for progress something you lose by using it.**
   Evidence: 9 say the floor key is used up, so smart players hoard instead of using it.
   Dominion could: make checkpoints, skips or saved progress in a run permanent once earned, or
   rebuyable at a fair cost, so using them never feels like a trap.
   Strength: weak to medium.
3. **Long runs need a way to stop and come back.**
   Evidence: 8 say runs are too long (two to seven hours), 4 ask for a save between floors.
   Dominion could: keep runs short, or allow the host to save the team's run between stages and
   resume with the same group; for a listen server, decide what happens if the host has to leave.
   Strength: medium.
4. **The game must never change the player's input settings on its own.**
   Evidence: 5 times it was said that sensitivity doubled near traps; patched.
   Dominion could: never scale mouse or stick input in special zones; test aim with every camera mode.
   Strength: weak (one game, one bug), but a cheap rule to keep.
5. **Price shapes the whole conversation.**
   Evidence: 22 too high (14 from recommenders), 10 sale only, 7 paid bundle prices and found it fair;
   price talk is 11.1% of everything said, the highest in the study.
   Dominion could: price for the hours and polish players will get; a full price on a budget-feel
   game is judged harshly even by fans.
   Strength: medium.
6. **Punchy shooting and a sense of humour win fans even in a cheap-looking game.**
   Evidence: hard-hitting shots 6, funny writing 9, all thumbs up.
   Dominion could: spend on hit feel and personality before spending on extra content.
   Strength: weak to medium.
