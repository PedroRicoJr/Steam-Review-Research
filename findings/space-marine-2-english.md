# Warhammer 40,000: Space Marine 2 — English

**A sample: 1,418 of 124,849 English reviews read, ±2.60% on any whole-sample share.** Grid month-sum
124,849 against Steam's own English total of 124,835 on the day the row was added — the grid matches
Steam to within 14 reviews. Figures are as of 2026-09-12.

**A third-person PvE co-op horde shooter from Saber Interactive, published by Focus Entertainment,
released 2024-09-09 at full price.** A licensed game — Games Workshop's Warhammer 40,000 — with a
short campaign, a co-op Operations mode built on six classes, and a PvP mode. Heavy melee: a parry, and
finishing moves on stunned enemies. Computer teammates (bots) fill empty spots; crossplay covers the
co-op but not the PvP. Sessions are missions picked from a hub, not runs that reset and not extraction;
there is no loot to find. Reviewers say the paid extras are only for looks and that a Siege mode came in
2026. The pages name no early access, and do not say how many players are in a squad, who hosts the
game, or what engine it runs on. 224,749 reviews in all languages, 84.0% positive, *Very Positive*.
**The second game in the large-games queue and the first licensed one in the corpus.** Close to Dominion
in shape: third person, co-op, PvE, sci-fi, built on classes, and it lives or dies on how strong the
combat makes you feel. It is not built on runs, has no extraction and no loot, has a story campaign and
a PvP mode Dominion does not plan, and leans on a famous licence Dominion does not have.

## 1. In plain words

**People love this game for what it is and complain about how little of it there is.** The most common
complaint is "not enough game": 84 times in 1,418 reviews, 5.9 per 100. And 66 of those 84 come from
people who still recommend the game. The middle reviewer who says it has played 17 hours. It is the
complaint of someone who finished it and wanted more, not of someone who wanted out.

**The thing said most of all is the game's own battle cry.** "For the Emperor" shows up 310 times, about
one review in five, and many of those reviews say nothing else. Helldivers 2 had the same thing with
"for Democracy". A fan with nothing to say shouts the slogan.

**The biggest real praise is that the game makes you feel super strong.** 64 times, and 63 of those
come from thumbs-up reviews. Next to it sit the story (74), the looks (64), being true to the Warhammer
world (62), a world worth exploring (54), and combat that hits hard (45).

**When that strong feeling breaks, people leave.** 13 say the game never makes them feel as strong as the
story says, and 12 of those 13 are thumbs down. The cause is always the same: a stun, a knock-down, a
grab or a swarm takes control away. For every five players who feel strong, one does not.

**The thumb never moved, but the complaints changed completely.** 88.5% thumbs up in the launch month,
87.8% in 2026. Crashes, a launch problem, fell to zero. "Only buy it on sale" rose about ten-fold, from
0.5 to 5.1 per 100. By 2026, the reviewer likes the game but thinks it is not worth full price two
years on, and argues with the one-of-each-class rule and the parry, not the servers.

**The most-voted review is a thumbs-down about one enemy side.** 632 people found it helpful. It says
the Chaos missions are much harder than the rest, not by design but because the screen is cluttered,
the warning signs mislead, and enemies stun you over and over. So players skip them, and half the
maps go unplayed.

**The surprise is how much of the talk is about the world, not the game.** Story and setting are 9.2%
of everything said, the most in the study. A famous licence brings reviews about the licence. Half of
the 62 reviews that explain the game by naming another one name Gears of War, and they mean it as
praise.

## 3. ⭐ The power fantasy: 64 say it lands, 13 say it does not, and the 13 are the thumbs-down

**`makes-you-feel-superhumanly-strong` is the game's biggest specific praise after the story (74) and
the looks (64), and it was built for this game, in batch 1.** **The tree had to learn to say it.** Its
inverse came four batches later (batch 5, cross-game section 17). *"Every fibre calls out for death to heresy"* (`218331878`); *"peak testosterone gaming"*
(`221527851`); *"the power fantasy of being a Space Marine done right"* (`222216060`). 63 of the 64
bullets come from thumbs-up reviews.

| Mode | Count | Thumbs |
|---|---|---|
| `makes-you-feel-superhumanly-strong` (+) | 64 | 63 up |
| `never-makes-you-feel-as-strong-as-the-fiction-says` (−) | 13 | 12 down |
| `stuns-take-control-away` (−) | 9 | |
| `the-parry-fails-when-you-need-it` (−) | 9 | |

**Its inverse is `never-makes-you-feel-as-strong-as-the-fiction-says`, 13 bullets, 12 of them
thumbs-down.** The complaint is always the same mechanism: a stun, a knock-down or a swarm takes
control away from a character the fiction says cannot be stopped. `stuns-take-control-away` (9)

## 5. For Dominion — our reading

**These are suggestions drawn from what players said, not something the reviews said.** Dominion is a
third-person, four-player co-op sci-fi PvE extraction and arena shooter built on runs, hosted on one
player's machine (listen server), in Unreal Engine 5.

The page says nothing about hosting, so nothing here speaks to a listen server directly.

1. **In a power-fantasy shooter, the moment you lose control is the moment you leave.** Evidence: 64
   say the game makes them feel superhumanly strong, 63 of them thumbs up; 13 say it never does, 12 of
   them thumbs down; the cause is always a stun, knock-down, grab or swarm: stuns take control away (9),
   the parry fails when needed (9). Dominion could keep hard stuns on players rare and short; give a way
   to break out (a dodge or shove with a cooldown); never chain-stun a player; make the parry or block
   window forgiving; test every enemy grab against "can I still act?". **Strength: strong.**
2. **One enemy faction that is hard through clutter, not design, empties half the game.** Evidence: the
   most-helpful review (632 helpful) says the Chaos missions are a tier harder "through clutter,
   misleading cues, flinching and stun-lock", players avoid them, and the map pool halves; the studio's
   own numbers, as the reviewer gives them, show Tyranid kills over triple Chaos ones. The sample backs
   it 4 ways: unreadable threats (5), stuns (9), ranged enemies hitting you while swarmed (5), one part
   far harder (4). Dominion could do a readability pass per enemy faction (clear warning signs before big
   hits, distinct looks for each enemy kind, less screen clutter); track how often each faction or map is
   picked and how often squads lose there; keep ranged enemies from hitting players who are locked in
   melee. **Strength: medium** (one review with huge votes, plus about 23 times in the sample).
3. **"Not enough game" can be the top complaint even from fans, and it comes back later.** Evidence: 84
   times, 66 from thumbs-up reviews, median 17 hours; six missions at launch; 7.6 per 100 in late 2024,
   down to 1.9 as missions were added, back to 5.1 in 2026 ("thirteen missions after three years").
   Repetitive: 37. Only 11 say it ends at the right time. Dominion could plan how much there is to do at
   launch against how many hours a fan will play; use runs, mixed objectives and mixed arenas to stretch
   the same content; keep a steady flow of new maps or enemy sets after launch. **Strength: strong.**
4. **The launch month sets the score, so launch stability matters most.** Evidence: the launch month
   holds 45.8% of all English reviews; the thumb was 88.5% then and 87.8% two years later. Crashes were
   3.2 per 100 in the launch month and fixing them later did not move the thumb — it just changed what
   people complained about. Dominion could spend on stability, loading and save safety before launch,
   not after. **Strength: strong.**
5. **A big update can break online play, so test networking after every update.** Evidence: disconnects
   jumped to 3.4 per 100 in early 2025 after the April update (servers broken, stutter, a false
   anti-cheat kick, which reviewers say was fixed 2025-04-24), then fell to 0.0. "A patch made it worse":
   16. Dominion could run a host-migration and listen-server smoke test (host, join, leave, rejoin,
   finish a run) before every patch. **Strength: medium.**
6. **Locking classes to one per squad makes someone lose the class they love.** Evidence: 13 times; it
   flips a review to thumbs down on its own (`202744724`); players asked for it off in the Siege mode
   (`219488514`). Dominion could allow duplicate classes, or lock only in a mode where roles really
   matter, or make the lock optional in the lobby. With four players, more people compete for the same
   class. **Strength: medium.**
7. **Price complaints rise as the game ages, even when players like it.** Evidence: "buy on sale only"
   rose from 0.5 to 5.1 per 100, 14 of 22 from recommenders; "the price is fair" fell from 1.3 to 0.0;
   "too expensive for what it is" steady at 28. Dominion could set the launch price against hours of
   content, and plan sales and content drops together so value keeps up with age. **Strength: medium.**
8. **Letting players change their look turns a complaint into praise.** Evidence: 10 asks to change
   their look (all before 2025-03, none in 2026); 13 thanks from 2025-03 on, rising to 3.6 per 100; "got
   my cape 10/10" (`226907394`); the shop selling only looks praised 11 times. But "paid content still has
   to be earned" and a paywalled colour were complaints. Dominion could ship a real look-customisation
   system early; keep paid items looks-only; never make a bought item still need grinding; do not sell
   colours. **Strength: medium.**
9. **Grind complaints grow with hours.** Evidence: "too grindy" 15 times, 0.7 per 100 at launch to 2.6
   in 2026 as mean hours went from 29 to 74; "unlocks feel good" fell from 1.2 to 0.0. Perk points and
   weapon mastery are named. Dominion could check unlock speed for a 70-hour player, not just a 20-hour
   one. **Strength: medium-weak.**
10. **Bots must stay useful on hard difficulty if solo play uses them.** Evidence: 10 say bots are
    useless in a fight, 2 say they help; "on higher levels the bots ... become dolts" (`222211857`).
    Dominion could, if bots fill empty slots, scale their skill with difficulty, or scale enemies down for
    smaller squads instead. **Strength: medium-weak.**
11. **The third-person camera must not shrink the view.** Evidence: the dark edge that narrows the view,
    9; slow-motion on every interaction ("it slows your perception, not the game", `206151338`).
    Dominion could keep screen effects for damage and low health light, and give a setting to turn them
    down. **Strength: medium-weak.**
12. **Dead time before every mission adds up.** Evidence: the hub walk (2: "select a mission, see the
    captain, go to the armoury, go to the launch bay, and it repeats", `222215927`); lifts and walks
    inside levels (4). Dominion could let players start the next run from a menu or a short walk; cut
    lifts and walks inside arenas. **Strength: weak.**
13. **A slogan players can shout is free marketing and glue.** Evidence: "For the Emperor" 310 times, one
    review in five; Helldivers 2 has the same with "for Democracy". Dominion could give the world a
    short, sayable battle cry and put it in the game (voice lines, emotes, the end-of-run screen).
    **Strength: medium** (two games show it).
14. **Comparing to Gears of War is praise; an old-fashioned, complete game is welcome.** Evidence: 31
    reviews name Gears of War; old-fashioned and better for it 21; made for its fans 18. Dominion could
    lean into weighty third-person shooting as a selling point. **Strength: medium.**
15. **A side mode that falls flat costs reviews.** Evidence: one mode falls flat, 15 — almost always the
    PvP; "PvP dead" (`224995971`). Dominion could stay with PvE and arenas rather than splitting work into
    PvP. **Strength: medium-weak.**
16. **Combat impact is easy to praise, but finishing moves need variety.** Evidence: 45 say combat hits
    hard, only 6 weightless; finishing moves are a highlight (3) but also "repeat until stale" (built
    from this game). Dominion could invest in hit feedback; if there are takedowns, make many and rotate
    them. **Strength: weak-medium.**
17. **Balance holes that players notice: dead weapons and health that does not refill.** Evidence: some
    options are useless, 9; health does not come back between fights, 9. Dominion could track weapon
    pick rates and buff dead picks; decide on purpose how health returns between arena waves or rooms
    and tell players. **Strength: weak-medium.**
18. **Loading times and lost progress are steady background complaints.** Evidence: long loads 20;
    progress not saved 8. Dominion could keep loads short between hub and run; save run rewards for
    every player when the run ends, even if the host drops. **Strength: medium-weak.**
19. **Let players start their own lobby.** Evidence: "you cannot start your own lobby" was built from
    this game (2 on the 2026-10-03 re-run). Dominion could: a listen-server game naturally lets any player
    host; keep a "host my own" button next to quick match. **Strength: weak.**
20. **Silence on a known problem shows up in the top review.** Evidence: "Saber has stayed silent despite
    overwhelming feedback" in the 632-vote review; "a studio that listens" was praised 11 times; patches
    that fixed what mattered 25. Dominion could answer big community threads in public, even when the fix
    is not ready. **Strength: weak-medium.**
21. **Clutter makes it hard to track things; flashing light hurts some players.** Evidence: two sightings
    toward a memory-and-attention problem (the story lost during combat, `189003013`; tracking on a
    cluttered screen, `196658368`); flashing lights seen once. Dominion could keep key story lines out of
    heavy fights; offer a reduce-flashing option. **Strength: weak.**
22. **A rich world makes people care — but Dominion has no licence to lean on.** Evidence: story is 9.2%
    of everything said, the most in the study; world worth exploring 54; story worth following 74; but
    story thin or forgettable 18. Dominion could not expect the world to carry reviews the way a famous
    licence does; keep the setting clear and the story short and strong. **Strength: weak for Dominion**
    (the licence does the work here).

## 7. Confidence and limits

1. **This is a 1.14% sample, not a census.** ±2.60% on a whole-sample share; wider on any month; 21
   of the 25 months are under 40 reviews and a single review moves 2026-09 by ten points.
2. **The launch month is the sample.** 591 of 1,418 reviews are 2024-09; 921 are 2024. Anything said
   about 2025 or 2026 rests on 301 and 196 reviews. The five-period table is the honest resolution.
3. **155 reviews are dated by creation, not by their last edit.** In this game the edits run both
   ways, so the direction of the error is unknown — unlike ARC Raiders, where it was one-way.
4. **No patch notes were pulled.** The reviews name the ultrawide patch (2024-10), the April 2025
   *Trygon* update and its 2025-04-24 fix, the weapon and parry changes, and the 2026 Siege mode;
   this page cannot confirm any of them from the studio's side. The studio's kill counts in the Chaos
   review, the paid extras being looks-only, and *"PvP dead"* are also what reviewers say. Whether the
   Chaos problem was fixed, or the players who cared left, cannot be told; whether the PvP is dead cannot
   be counted from reviews.
5. **One language.** About 100,000 reviews in other languages are unpulled. Reviews written in another
   language under the English tag were not counted.
6. **19% of bullets are `unknown`**, 363 of them a wordless thumbs up — lower than ARC Raiders' 31%.
   The direction audit found 0 disagreements and 0 unfitted observations across all 29 batches.
7. **Two counts.** The page's figures are from 2026-09-12; the 2026-10-03 re-run of `findings_tables.py`
   differs by a few bullets (2,816 bullets, 495 `unknown`, 397 distinct tags, 27 game-only, long loads 19,
   old-fashioned 19) and counts praise and complaints per period without `review.*` bullets. Both are
   shown; neither was weighted.
8. **Slurs and personal details.** This page quotes no slur and no personal or health detail.
9. Squad size, hosting, engine and how the game scales with players are not on the page.
