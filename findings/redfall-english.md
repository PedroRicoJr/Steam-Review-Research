# Redfall — English

**A sample: 1,015 of 3,071 English reviews read, ±2.52 points on any whole-sample share.** Every
sampled review was tagged: 1,015 of 1,015 summarised. The read covers 40 months, 2023-05 to 2026-08.
The pull date and the planned quota are in `raw/redfall/english/MANIFEST.md`. **The sample reads every
month at a similar depth, and 65% of all English reviews came in the launch month, so the sample reads
higher than Steam (section 7).**

**A first-person shooter for one to four players, played online** (Steam store page: *"an open-world,
single player and co-op FPS"*, *"up to four player co-op"*, checked 2026-10-03). Vampires take over a
small island town and you clear it in an open world. Made by Arkane Austin, a studio owned by Microsoft.
Paid, and also shipped on Game Pass and given away with graphics cards; a "Bite Back Edition" was sold
too. Released 2 May 2023. The game checks in with a server when it first starts; an offline mode was
added on 30 May 2024. Microsoft closed the studio on 7 May 2024. The engine is not named on these
pages. All languages: **4,716 reviews, 38.5% positive, "Mostly Negative"**; 3,071 of them are English.
**It is row 2 of `GAMES-TODO.md`, the corpus's disaster case.** It is close to Dominion in the parts
that hurt most: a four-player co-op shooter with PvE enemies, a paid game built for friends to play
online, and it failed. It differs in camera (first person, not third), shape (an open world, not runs,
arenas or extraction), setting (vampires in a modern town, not sci-fi) and size (a big studio owned by a
giant company).

## 1. In plain words

**Redfall's reviews are mostly about a purchase, a name and a company, not about how the game plays.**
The two things said most often are "buy it on sale" (172 times) and "it costs too much for what it is"
(143 times today, 144 when first counted). Three of the five most common things said are about what to
pay and what other people think. The first thing about the game itself is dumb enemies, 126 times.

**The top praise is not about the game either.** 124 reviewers argue that the game is better than its
bad name. Only 9 say the bad name is fair. After that, people like the place: the town, its mood and
its look are four of the ten most-praised things.

**The game got much better, and the score did not move.** Reviews from May to August 2023 are 34.7%
thumbs up; reviews from September 2023 on are 60.7% up. The turn came in October 2023, with the second
big update, not the first. Steam still shows one score, built mostly out of the launch month.

**Who complains changed with time.** At launch people said it cost too much, ran badly and had stupid
enemies. Later buyers say buy it cheap, the owner closed the studio, and it crashes. Crashes are more
common in later reviews than at launch (1.7% of launch-half reviews, 5.5% after). People who gave a thumbs down played a median of 5 hours;
people who gave a thumbs up, 13 hours.

**The game often will not start now, and nobody is left to fix it.** It waits for a server on first
start. One in ten of the 2026 reviews read say so. Of 18 reviews that could not reach the server, 13
came after the studio closed and 7 were written by people with zero hours played. Buyers now teach each
other the key presses that get past the stuck screen.

**Players barely mention each other.** Only 2.7% of everything said is about other players or the
community, against about 28% in the two big co-op hits read so far. Yet "much better with friends" is
one of the top praises (54 times).

**The surprise: the most helpful-voted complaint, per time said, is about a free copy.** "It came free
with my graphics card" is said only 50 times but carries 79 helpful votes each time, the highest rate
of anything said in this read.

## 3.4 ⭐ The game does not start, and there is nobody left to fix it

`engineering.servers.cannot-connect` holds **18 reviews**. They fall into two groups:

| Period | Reviews | Zero hours played | Helpful votes |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2023 launch | 3 | 0 | 56 |
| **After the studio closed (Nov 2024 →)** | **13** | **7** | **593** |

**Seven people wrote a review of a game they were never able to launch.** One in ten of every 2026
review read says the game will not start.

> *"Don't buy, game just is stuck on a connecting to server page."* — **39 helpful, 0 hours**
> *"offline mode doesn't exist? it won't let me start the game just gets stuck trying to connect to
> non existent servers"* — **54 helpful, 0 hours**
> *"It still tries to connect to a server that isn't there anymore and doesn't boot up cuz of it…
> **Was refused a refund and the game is still for sale despite being unplayable.**"* — **77 helpful**

**The mechanism, from the reviews themselves:** the first launch overlays a server-connection dialog
on top of the licence agreement. **The agreement must be accepted to connect, and the connection
window sits on top of it.** The offline mode that would bypass the whole thing is behind the same
screen for anyone who did not enable it before the servers went quiet.

**And the buyers are doing the support work.**
`community.player-conduct.players-teach-each-other-the-fix` (**8 bullets**) is people publishing the
workaround inside their reviews:

> *"**DON'T use the mouse is the key, use the keyboard and hit enter.**"*
> *"press the space bar multiple times while pressing cancel and voilà — you're in."*
> *"if you **spam enter**, you can get it to work. if you do not do this, it will never connect."*

**The same pattern shows on the crashes.** `engineering.stability.one-setting-causes-the-crashes` was
built during this run because two reviewers found the single option behind them and published it —
*"switching off DLSS cured ALL the crashing"*, *"lower your shadow quality and turn on FXA."*

⚠️ **This is the practical finding for a studio.** An always-online check outlived the company that
answered it. **It does not stop the game selling** — `store-page-hides-a-dealbreaker` holds 8 bullets,
refunds were refused (`wanted-to-but-could-not`, 34), and two reviewers ask outright for the game to be
delisted — **it just turns every new buyer into a zero-hour thumbs down.** 101 reviews in this corpus
(10.0%) have zero hours; **89 of them are negative** — a permanent, self-renewing supply of one-star
reviews from people who never saw the game.

## 3.5 ⭐ The reputation argument

`marketing.reputation` is the **largest subject in the Redfall corpus** at 356 bullets (9.3%). The
cross-game page says it is nowhere near the top in any other game. **And the argument is one-sided:**
`judged-unfairly` **124** against `reputation-deserved` **9** — **a 14-to-1 argument. In the

pile-on (13.0% of launch-half reviews), not only years later (11.6%).

## 5. For Dominion — our reading

**These are suggestions drawn from what players said, not something the reviews said.** Dominion is a
third-person, four-player co-op sci-fi PvE extraction and arena shooter built on runs, hosted on one
player's machine (listen server), in Unreal Engine 5.

1. **Do not lock the game behind an online check that can die.**
   Evidence: 18 reviews cannot reach the server; 13 came after the studio closed, 7 at zero hours,
   593 helpful votes; one in ten 2026 reviews say it will not start; 101 zero-hour reviews, 89 of them
   thumbs down. The offline mode came three weeks after the studio closed, behind the same broken
   screen.
   Dominion could: let the game start and reach the menu with no server at all. Let solo and
   friends-only (one player hosts) play run with no online service needed. If any online check is
   added (Steam, EOS, store), make it fail open: show a clear message and keep going. Test a start with
   the network cable pulled before every release.
   Strength: **strong** for the outcome; the cause is told by reviewers.
2. **The first screen must never block itself.**
   Evidence: the "connecting" box sits on top of the licence agreement you must accept; 8 reviews teach
   others to press Enter or Space to get through.
   Dominion could: never stack two pop-ups that both need an answer. Every first-run screen should
   work with mouse, keyboard and pad, and time out to something you can use.
   Strength: **medium** (8 reviews plus the 18 cannot-connect reviews).
3. **Smart enemies are the design fix that matters most in a PvE shooter.**
   Evidence: dumb enemies is the top complaint about the game itself, 126 times, 15.2% weighted,
   3,448 helpful votes. It halved after patches but stayed. *"how are vampires so stupid in A VAMPIRE
   GAME"*.
   Dominion could: give each enemy type a clear plan (rush, flank, hold, ambush) and test that the
   team sees it happen. Avoid enemies that only run forward or stand still. Budget for AI before
   launch, not after.
   Strength: **strong**.
4. **Launch is the review that sticks. A late fix changes reviews, not the score or the sales.**
   Evidence: 65% of English reviews came in launch month; the score went from 34.7% to 60.7% up after
   October 2023, but Steam still shows 38.5%. The first big patch (June 2023) did nothing. The
   cross-game page says few people were playing by the time the fix landed.
   Dominion could: hold the launch until frame rate, stability and enemy AI are solid. Use an early
   access or a playtest with honest labels instead of a big launch of an unfinished game.
   Strength: **strong**.
5. **A big patch must fix what players actually feel, not a list of systems.**
   Evidence: v1.1 ("enemy AI, graphics, stability") left June-August flat; Game Update 2 (a
   performance mode and an open-world rework) turned the score at once and for good.
   Dominion could: aim patches at the top two or three complaints players name, and say in the notes
   exactly what changes for them.
   Strength: **medium** (one game, clear timing).
6. **Fixing frame rate is not the same as fixing crashes.**
   Evidence: frame rate complaints fell from 8.5% to 1.7%, but crashes rose from 1.7% to 5.5%; two
   players found one setting (DLSS, shadows) behind the crashes.
   Dominion could: track crashes as their own number; test each upscaler and each high setting for
   crashes; give a safe-mode start (low settings, no upscaler) if the last run crashed.
   Strength: **medium**.
7. **Price must match what is in the box, on day one.**
   Evidence: "costs too much for what it is" 144 times, 18.1% weighted, 6,107 helpful votes; "only on
   sale" 172 times and 17.0 per 100 reviews, the highest in the study; too little content 41 times.
   Dominion could: set the launch price to the amount of content and polish there really is; a lower
   price at launch costs less than years of "wait for a sale".
   Strength: **strong**.
8. **Free copies bring the angriest, most-voted reviews.**
   Evidence: "came free with my graphics card" 50 times, 79 helpful votes per time said, the top rate;
   the most helpful review (1,070 votes) was a one-hour, thumbs-down review that says it came free
   with hardware.
   Dominion could: be careful with bundles and giveaways while the game is rough; a free player who
   bounces still writes a review.
   Strength: **medium**.
9. **Co-op needs to be easy to get into, or players stop talking about each other.**
   Evidence: only 2.7% of everything said is about other players, against 28.5-28.8% in the two big
   co-op hits; reviewers say there is no public matchmaking and only the host keeps progress; yet
   "much better with friends" is the third praise (54 times).
   Dominion could: offer quick join with strangers as well as friend invites; make sure every player,
   not just the host, keeps what they earn in a run (this matters doubly on a listen server where one
   player hosts).
   Strength: **medium** (the 2.7% is strong; the takeaways entry gave no count for the host-progress
   and matchmaking claims — the script now counts no public matchmaking 14 and only the host keeps the
   progress 8, section 4).
10. **A strong world and look will not save weak play, but players do notice them.**
    Evidence: world worth exploring 52, mood pulls you in 51, art well directed 44, places worth
    looking at 39; praised even in thumbs-down launch reviews.
    Dominion could: keep investing in a clear sci-fi look and mood, but never in place of enemy AI,
    stability and feel.
    Strength: **medium**.
11. **A story that matters to players needs good cutscenes.**
    Evidence: story worth following 53 vs thin and forgettable 72; cutscenes badly made 41.
    Dominion could: if it tells a story, keep cutscenes short and well staged, or tell it in play
    instead.
    Strength: **medium**.
12. **Have a pause and an offline mode from day one.**
    Evidence: Game Update 4 added a pause and offline mode; "it plays offline" went from 0 to 21 times
    said, all praise.
    Dominion could: pause in solo; offline solo play from launch.
    Strength: **medium**.
13. **Write the store page honestly, and keep it honest as things change.**
    Evidence: "the store page hides a deal-breaker" 8 times; refunds refused 34 times; two ask for
    delisting.
    Dominion could: if support ever ends or servers change, say so at the top of the store page.
    Strength: **weak to medium**.
14. **The climax must be the hardest fight, not the easiest.**
    Evidence: "the final fight is a pushover" was first named in this game (the takeaways entry gave
    no count; the script counts 2 bullets).
    Dominion could: make sure the last arena or boss of a run is the peak of the run.
    Strength: **weak**.
15. **A bad name sticks, and the people who come later spend their review arguing with it.**
    Evidence: reputation is the biggest subject (356 times, 9.3%); 124 say it is judged unfairly vs 9
    who say it is fair.
    Dominion could: protect the launch reputation above all; a strong launch is cheaper than any
    rescue.
    Strength: **strong** for the pattern.

## 7. Confidence and limits

### ⚠️ Read this before any number on this page

*(On the page before the 2026-10-03 rebuild this part opened the page and was numbered 0.)*

**The sample says 48.6% up. Steam says 38.5%. That 10-point gap is not sampling error** — the
interval is ±2.52 points and cannot stretch that far.

**It is the shape of the pull, and it is the method working as designed.** SAMPLING-RULES reads every
month at a similar depth so that a game's whole life is visible. Redfall's negatives are not spread
across its life. **They are stacked in one month:**

| | Reviews on Steam | Reviews read | Depth |
|---|---|---|---|
| **2023-05, the launch month** | **1,986** (65% of the group) | 404 | **20.3%** |
| Every other month (39 of them) | 1,085 | 611 | **56.3%** |

The launch month is two thirds of everything ever written about this game in English, and it is the
month read least deeply. **An even read across months therefore under-weights the launch and
over-weights the long tail.**

**Weighting each month back to its true Steam volume (Rule 6) gives 42.9%.** That is 4.4 points from
Steam's own figure, and the remainder is the same effect one level down: **within** May 2023 the
sampler spread across weekly windows, and the very first days — the worst days — are still read
thinner than the rest of the month.

**So use the numbers this way:**

- **The direction of every finding on this page is sound.** It rests on 1,014 reviews and a third of
  the population.
- **Any single headline percentage is not Steam's percentage** and should never be quoted as one.
- **Where a number matters, the weighted figure is given beside the raw one.**

### What this corpus cannot tell you

1. ⚠️ **It cannot give you Steam's 38.5%.** See above. The even-month read is a deliberate choice
   that makes the game's whole life visible and its launch month under-weighted.
2. **Per-period margins:** the launch half (470 reviews) reads 34.7%, 95% interval 30.4 – 39.0; the
   later half (544) reads 60.7%, 56.6 – 64.8. **Thin months:** September 2023 is 16 reviews.
3. **It cannot tell you how many people never wrote a review.** Redfall shipped into Game Pass, and
   `came-free-with-hardware` (50) shows a large bundled audience. **Most people who played Redfall
   never paid for it and never reviewed it. Reviewers are the ones who cared enough to type.**
4. **It cannot separate "the game improved" from "the audience changed."** Both happen after October
   2023: patches landed, and the people arriving afterwards paid a tenth of the price and had read
   the reviews first. **`low-expectations-made-it-better` (18) is in the tree for exactly this
   reason.** Nothing in review text splits the two cleanly.
5. **English only.** 3,071 of the game's 4,716 reviews are English. The other 1,645 are unread.
   Nothing here speaks for them.
6. **Edited reviews.** The 2026-09-03 page found **175 reviews (17.3%) carrying more than one date** —
   an original and an edit; the script's edit marker finds 102 (10.1%). Every one is filed under its
   **creation** month, on standing instruction. **A review written at launch and rewritten in 2026
   counts as a launch review here.** This is an open question with Rico and is not settled.
7. **Steam's language tag is the reviewer's client language, not the language they wrote in.** At
   least one review in this group is written in Portuguese. It was read and tagged on what it says.
8. **What was checked and what was not.** The patch dates and the studio closure date come from
   Steam's own news feed. Everything else a reviewer says happened — server behaviour, refunds refused,
   delisting, prices paid, the bundle — is **what the reviewer says**. The report on Arkane Lyon is
   not checked.
9. **Slurs and personal details.** None are quoted on this page; any jab in a review is recorded as a
   jab, not repeated.
