# Immortal: Unchained — English

**This is a census, not a sample. Every English review of this game was read — 435 of 435.**
**There is no margin of error, because nothing was left out to be wrong about.** There is also no
month-coverage caveat: all 77 months are covered at 100%. Rule 12 still binds for the report month —
the figures below are the corpus as it stood on **2026-09-10**.

**It is also the first non-co-op game in the corpus.** The eight games read before it are co-op
shooters. This one is a single-player soulslike third-person shooter. **Section 4 is about what that
did to the tag tree, and it is the most important part of this document** — it is evidence about the
tree, not about the game.

## 1. ⭐ The people who recommend this game are the ones cataloguing its faults

**A reviewer who recommends Immortal: Unchained writes 1.53 complaints while doing it.** That is the
second-highest figure in the corpus, behind only Redfall.

| Game | Complaints per recommending review | Praise per recommending review |
|---|---|---|
| Deep Rock Galactic | 0.17 | 1.80 |
| Helldivers 2 | 0.21 | 1.24 |
| Back 4 Blood | 0.77 | 1.61 |
| Terminull Brigade | 0.96 | 1.25 |
| Aliens: Fireteam Elite | 0.97 | 1.44 |
| The Anacrusis | 1.16 | 1.93 |
| DRG: Rogue Core | 1.17 | 1.53 |
| **Immortal: Unchained** | **1.53** | **1.85** |
| Redfall | 1.80 | 2.10 |

**45.5% of every complaint in this game was written by somebody who told you to buy it** — 431 of
948 negative bullets.

**Look at what that does to the top complaints.** These are the modes named *most* often, and the
majority of each one sits on a thumbs **up**:

| Complaint mode | Bullets | Written by recommenders |
|---|---|---|
| `power-balance.some-options-are-useless` | 26 | **73%** |
| `power-balance.one-option-dominates` | 25 | **72%** |
| `publishing.price.too-high-for-what-it-is` | 38 | **63%** |
| `difficulty-tuning.badly-scaled` | 17 | **59%** |
| `engineering.bugs.buggy` | 18 | **56%** |

🔑 **A recommendation here is an argument, not a verdict.** People are not saying the game is good.
They are saying *the game is broken in these named ways and worth it anyway* — and the thumb records
only the second half of that sentence.

**Only four modes are genuinely one-sided complaints** — raised almost entirely by people who
refused to recommend it:

| Mode | Bullets | Written by recommenders |
|---|---|---|
| `enemy-design.no-counterplay` | 16 | 25% |
| `game-feel.combat.sluggish-weapon-handling` | 23 | **30%** |
| `game-feel.controls.stuns-take-control-away` | 13 | 31% |
| `enemy-design.unfair-spawns` | 30 | 37% |

**The dividing line is control.** Faults about *numbers* — bad balance, a dominant weapon, a fair
price — are forgiven by the people who like the game. Faults about **the game taking the controller
away from you** are not.

`marketing.reputation.explained-by-naming-other-games` is **76 bullets — the largest mode in the
game by a factor of 1.6**, and it appears in about one review in six.

| Rank | Mode | Bullets |

| 4 | `publishing.price.too-high-for-what-it-is` | 38 |

Add the three neighbouring reputation modes and the picture is sharper still:
`beaten-by-a-competitor` (25) and `unlike-anything-else` (27) — **and these two are the same

> *"If you want a soulslike with guns, this is it. There is no other option."* (`218154663`)
> *"Poor mans remnant... don't bother"* (`171161481`)

**Third place in the whole game goes to `review.grades-it-against-the-studios-size` at 34.** One
review in thirteen states outright that it is measuring this game against a small budget before it
gives a verdict. **That is not a claim about the game at all** — it is a reviewer telling you which
ruler they used.

## 3. The score does not move, because nothing about the game moved

**64.6% up across eight years.** Split by the year the review was written:

|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2018 | 118 | 45 | 163 | 72.4% |
| 2019 | 69 | 33 | 102 | 67.6% |
| 2020 | 16 | 19 | 35 | **45.7%** |

| 2022 | 25 | 11 | 36 | 69.4% |
| 2023 | 25 | 16 | 41 | 61.0% |
| 2024 | 4 | 10 | 14 | **28.6%** |
| 2025 | 5 | 4 | 9 | 55.6% |
| 2026 | 3 | 5 | 8 | 37.5% |

⚠️ **Do not read a trend into the tail.** 2024 through 2026 is **31 reviews across three years**.
A census removes sampling error; it does not manufacture reviews that were never written. **The
2024 figure is four people voting up and ten voting down**, and one more either way moves it eight
points.

**What is solid is the shape of the first two years: 163 reviews in 2018 and 102 in 2019 — 61% of
every English review this game ever received arrived in its first sixteen months**, and the rate has
never recovered.

🔑 **The live-ops division is 0.5% of this game's bullets against 4.1% for the co-op games.** There
was no patch cycle to argue about, no season, no roadmap. **The score is flat because the game is
finished and abandoned, and the only thing that changes is who wanders in.**

## 4. ⭐ The genre test: what a single-player game does to a tree built from co-op shooters

**This is the finding the game was chosen for.** The tree claims to be universal. Nine games in, one
of them is not a co-op shooter, and the claim can finally be measured.

### 4a. The tree held. 240 of the 245 modes it needed already existed.

**Only five modes in the whole 435-review census are used by this game and by no other game in the
corpus:**

| Mode | Bullets |
|---|---|
| `review.says-how-much-of-the-genre-they-have-played` | 8 |
| `engineering.performance.one-setting-causes-the-slowdown` | 6 |
| `game-design.game-feel.combat.the-lock-on-holds-the-body-not-the-weak-point` | 4 |
| `storefront.trading-cards.unknown` | 1 |
| `game-design.game-feel.camera.camera-gets-in-the-way` | 1 |

**A tree grown entirely from co-op shooters absorbed a single-player soulslike with five new
sightings and no structural change.** No new division was needed. No subject split was needed. That
is the strongest evidence for the universality claim that this study has produced.

### 4b. But 49 subjects were never touched, and they fall into two different kinds of absence

| Rank | Subject the other eight games use, and this one never does | Their bullets |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | `community.culture` | 1,325 |
| 2 | `community.playing-with-friends` | 983 |
| 3 | `game-design.co-op-design` | 623 |
| 4 | `production.content-variety` | 577 |
| 5 | `game-design.role-design` | 337 |
| 6 | `publishing.monetisation-practice` | 335 |
| 7 | `community.player-conduct` | 315 |
| 8 | `game-design.solo-viability` | 314 |
| 9 | `game-design.ai-teammates` | 312 |
| 10 | `live-ops.abandonment` | 281 |
| 11 | `production.scope-mismatch` | 272 |
| 12 | `engineering.matchmaking` | 262 |
| 13 | `live-ops.update-cadence` | 261 |
| 14 | `community.population` | 258 |
| 15 | `production.early-access` | 210 |

🔑 **These are not one cluster. They are two, and they are absent for different reasons.**

**Cluster one — other people.** `co-op-design`, `ai-teammates`, `solo-viability`, `role-design`,
`playing-with-friends`, `player-conduct`, `population`, `matchmaking`, `servers`, `netcode`,
`crossplay-and-platform-mix`, `social-features`. **These are empty because the genre changed.** A
single-player game has no teammates to describe.

**Cluster two — a live service.** `abandonment`, `update-cadence`, `early-access`, `launch-state`,
`scope-mismatch`, `monetisation-practice`, `refund`, `ownership`, `preorder`, `availability`,
`data-and-privacy`. **These are empty because the business model changed.** A finished game bought
years after release on sale has no roadmap to break and no currency to sell.

⚠️ **The tree cannot tell these two absences apart, and it should be able to.** Both read as a
subject with zero bullets. **A designer asking "what does this tree cover?" would see 49 gaps and no
way to know that 12 of them are about co-op and 11 are about live service.** That is a real
observation about the tree's shape, and it is **for Rico, not for me** — it points at how divisions
are organised, which is his call.

## 6. Where the guns meet the souls-like, and lose

**Three of the top complaint clusters are the same collision described three ways.**

`the-lock-on-holds-the-body-not-the-weak-point` (4, built in batch 9) ·
`shots-go-where-they-want` (14) · `enemy-design.all-fought-the-same-way` (18, and it is *"every
enemy has a head and a back"*).

🔑 **A soulslike inherits lock-on from a melee game, where the enemy is one target.** Bolt it onto a
shooter whose enemies have weak points and the assist fights the player.

> *"when you lock on, your reticule becomes suddenly glued to the enemy's torso, making targeting
> the weak spots nigh on impossible"* (`233349028`)
> *"Lock on does not autotarget weak points"* (`45153643`)

**39 word hits for lock-on and aim assist across the corpus, and 22 of them are this game alone.**
No co-op shooter in the eight games before this ever had to make the choice.
