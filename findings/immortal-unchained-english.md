# Immortal: Unchained — English

**This is a census, not a sample. Every English review of this game was read — 435 of 435.**
**There is no margin of error, because nothing was left out to be wrong about.** There is also no
month-coverage caveat: all 77 months are covered at 100%. Rule 12 still binds for the report month —
the figures written on 2026-09-10 are the corpus as it stood on **2026-09-10**; the script figures are
from 2026-10-03. ✅ **Steam's own English count is 281 up and 154 down. So is ours.** A census has to
match, and it does.

**A single-player "soulslike" third-person shooter from 2018: hard, lock-on combat in the style of
Dark Souls, but with guns.** Players level and customise weapons. The world is built from large,
linked areas ("biomes") full of paths and secrets. Paid, finished, and no longer updated: no seasons,
no roadmap, no shop. No co-op, no online play. The engine is not named on these pages. Steam, all
languages: **890 reviews, Mixed**; 435 of them English, 455 (51%) in other languages. **It is row 6 of
`GAMES-TODO.md` and the first non-co-op game in the corpus**: the eight games read before it are co-op
shooters, and it was chosen as **the genre test** — the question it answers is not "is this game good"
but **"does a tree grown from co-op shooters still work when the co-op is taken away?"** It is half
close to Dominion: it shares the third-person camera, shooting as the main action, enemies with weak
points, builds and weapon levelling, and a hard, skill-based tone. It does not share co-op, hosting,
runs, extraction or arenas: it is one player, one long campaign.

## 1. In plain words

**People who like this game still list its faults.** A reviewer who gives a thumbs up writes 1.53
complaints on average, and 45.5% of all complaints (431 of 948) come from people who recommend it.

**What they forgive and what they do not.** They forgive faults about numbers: bad balance, one weapon
that beats the rest, the price. They do not forgive the game taking control away from them: being
stunned, slow weapon handling, attacks you cannot answer, enemies that appear with no warning.

**The most-praised thing is the level design** (46 times), praised even by people who do not
recommend the game. Hard-but-fair difficulty is next (39), then the many ways to build your character
(31).

**Nobody can describe it without naming another game.** That is the single most common thing said,
76 times. 27 say nothing else plays like it; 25 say another game (Remnant) did it better.

**The aim help fights the guns.** The lock-on, borrowed from sword games, grabs the enemy's body
instead of its weak point.

**The score barely moved because the game never changed.** 61% of all English reviews came in the
first sixteen months. Thumbs up was 70.6% in 2018–2019 and 59.0% in 2020–2023; the last three years
hold only 31 reviews. Thumbs-down reviewers played a median of 4 hours, thumbs-up reviewers 16.

**The surprise is about the study, not the game.** A way of sorting reviews built only from team
shooters fitted a one-player game almost perfectly: 240 of the 245 labels it needed already existed.

**Look at what that does to the top complaints.** These are the modes named *most* often, and the

🔑 **A recommendation here is an argument, not a verdict.** People are not saying the game is good.
They are saying *the game is broken in these named ways and worth it anyway* — and the thumb records
only the second half of that sentence.

**Only four modes are genuinely one-sided complaints** — raised almost entirely by people who

| Mode | Bullets | Written by recommenders |

**The dividing line is control.** Faults about *numbers* — bad balance, a dominant weapon, a fair
price — are forgiven by the people who like the game. Faults about **the game taking the controller
away from you** are not. The cross-game page: *the eighth game showed that a thumb records whether
they would buy it again and the text records what they would change; the ninth sharpens where the line

## 3.6 ⭐ Where the guns meet the souls-like, and lose

**Three of the top complaint clusters are the same collision described three ways.**

`the-lock-on-holds-the-body-not-the-weak-point` (4, built in batch 9) ·
`shots-go-where-they-want` (14) · `enemy-design.all-fought-the-same-way` (18, and it is *"every
enemy has a head and a back"*).

🔑 **A soulslike inherits lock-on from a melee game, where the enemy is one target.** Bolt it onto a
shooter whose enemies have weak points and the assist fights the player.

> *"when you lock on, your reticule becomes suddenly glued to the enemy's torso, making targeting
> the weak spots nigh on impossible"* (`233349028`)
> *"Lock on does not autotarget weak points"* (`45153643`)

**39 word hits for lock-on and aim assist across the corpus, and 22 of them are this game alone.**
No co-op shooter in the eight games before this ever had to make the choice.

## 5. For Dominion — our reading

**These are suggestions drawn from what players said, not something the reviews said.** Dominion is a
third-person, four-player co-op sci-fi PvE extraction and arena shooter built on runs, hosted on one
player's machine (listen server), in Unreal Engine 5.

1. **Never take the controller away for long.**
   Evidence: stuns 13 (31% recommenders), slow weapon handling 23 (30%), no counterplay 16 (25%),
   unfair spawns 30 (37%). These are the complaints that make people leave; balance and price faults
   are forgiven.
   Dominion could: keep stuns and knockdowns short and rare, always with a tell and a way out (dodge,
   break-free, teammate save). Every enemy attack should have an answer by skill. Make reload, swap
   and ready times quick, and let actions cancel into a dodge.
   Strength: **strong** (clear split across several complaints).
2. **Aim help must respect weak points.**
   Evidence: lock-on holds the body 4 (only here); shots go where they want 14; every enemy fought the
   same way 18; 22 of 39 lock-on and aim-assist mentions in the study are this game.
   Dominion could: if it adds aim help or lock-on for pad players, let it snap to weak points or
   release near them; keep spread small enough that aim matters; give enemies weak points that need
   different answers, not just "head and back".
   Strength: **medium**.
3. **Spawns must give the player a chance.**
   Evidence: unfair spawns 30 times, mostly from people who left.
   Dominion could: spawn enemies out of sight with an audio or visual tell, never right behind a
   player with no warning; test spawn points in every arena.
   Strength: **medium to strong**.
4. **Third-person movement must feel quick.**
   Evidence: moving feels slow and heavy 10 times; the cross-game page says a third-person shooter is
   judged on how it moves, and a slow dash or slide is the complaint.
   Dominion could: tune sprint, slide and dodge for speed and quick response before release.
   Strength: **medium**.
5. **Hard is good when it is fair, and when learning pays off.**
   Evidence: satisfyingly hard 39; gets good once learned 24; but badly scaled difficulty 17.
   Dominion could: offer hard modes that reward skill, with even steps between levels, and teach the
   key trick (dodge, weak points) early so the "click" comes in the first session.
   Strength: **medium**.
6. **Teach the main trick early.**
   Evidence: *"I hated the game until I learned this trick"* (78807510); 24 say it only got good once
   learned.
   Dominion could: make the first run show the core moves clearly, so players do not quit before the
   game clicks.
   Strength: **medium**.
7. **Balance faults are forgiven, but they are said a lot.**
   Evidence: some choices useless 26 (73% recommenders); one choice dominates 25 (72%); many builds
   praised 31.
   Dominion could: test that every gun and ability has a use; nerf or buff in patches; do not panic,
   but do not ignore it.
   Strength: **medium**.
8. **Level design is a strong praise point, even for critics.**
   Evidence: well-built levels 46, the top praise, from both camps.
   Dominion could: make arenas layered with routes and secrets, and leave room to dodge and use the
   camera (the "spaces too small" complaint was first named here).
   Strength: **medium**.
9. **Show players what is left to earn.**
   Evidence: the "does not show what is left to earn" complaint was first named here; a checklist was
   asked for (the takeaways entry gave no counts; the script now counts 2 bullets for the complaint).
   Dominion could: give a codex or unlock list showing locked items and how to get them.
   Strength: **weak**.
10. **One graphics option should never wreck performance.**
    Evidence: one setting causes the slowdown, 6 times; players had to find it.
    Dominion could: test each high setting for cost; flag the expensive ones in the menu; give
    sensible defaults.
    Strength: **medium**.
11. **A third-person camera must not block the view or the shot.**
    Evidence: camera gets in the way 1; scenery swallows a shot 1 (later seen in more third-person
    games).
    Dominion could: test camera collision and shots near cover, rails and fences.
    Strength: **weak** here (stronger across the study).
12. **Expect to be compared; be clear what you are.**
    Evidence: describing by other games 76 (17.5 per 100); "nothing like it" 27 vs "Remnant did it
    better" 25.
    Dominion could: know the games it will be named next to, and be better at one clear thing.
    Strength: **medium**.
13. **Price faults are forgiven if the game is liked, but they still cost.**
    Evidence: too pricey 38 times, 63% from recommenders.
    Dominion could: price to content; know that even fans will say it.
    Strength: **medium**.
14. **Answering reviews and showing you listen is noticed even in a solo game.**
    Evidence: listens and acts 9; replied to my review 1.
    Dominion could: reply to store reviews and patch what players name.
    Strength: **weak**.
15. **Co-op and live updates fill a lot of what players talk about.**
    Evidence: with no other players, game-design talk rose to 53.6% (30.3% in co-op games), and talk
    about players fell to 0.8% (13.6%).
    Dominion could: expect its co-op, matchmaking and update talk to be a big share of its reviews,
    and plan for it.
    Strength: **medium** (one game, clear numbers).

⚠️ **The census removes sampling error. It does not remove small numbers.** Any figure on this page
that rests on a single year after 2023 rests on fewer than fifteen reviews, because that is how many
were written. **Section 3.3 says so on its own line.**

⚠️ **One review in the English group is written in Portuguese** (`177878863`). Steam labelled it
`english`. It was read and summarised from its content like any other. **This is a note about
Steam's language field, not about the corpus.**

**The game is finished and no longer updated.** Unlike DRG: Rogue Core, nothing here is a photograph
of a moving thing. **These numbers will not change unless somebody writes a new review.**

**What it does not settle:**
- **Whether the genre finding generalises.** One soulslike is one soulslike. The ten roguelikes
  queued next (when this was written) are a different single-player genre and test the same claim
  again.
- **Anything about the last three years.** 2024–2026 is 31 reviews.
- **Co-op and live-service lessons** — none can come from a single-player, finished game.

**Not checked:** the free copy from the studio, the platform reward and every other claim about what
happened are what the reviewers say. **Slurs and personal details:** none are quoted on this page.
