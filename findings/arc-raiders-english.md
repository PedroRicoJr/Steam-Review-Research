# ARC Raiders — English

**A sample: 1,473 of 251,588 English reviews read, ±2.69% on any whole-sample share.** Grid month-sum
251,588 against Steam's own English total of 251,586 on the day the row was added — the grid matches
Steam to within two reviews. Figures are as of 2026-09-11.

**A third-person PvPvE extraction shooter from Embark Studios, released 2025-10-30 at $40.** Players
fight both the world's robot machines (the ARC) and other players. They drop in solo, as a duo or as a
trio, loot, and try to get out alive; gear you bring can be lost, and you can also go in with a free
loadout. Matches are filled by a matchmaker that sorts players by how they behaved in past matches (how
often they attacked other players) instead of by skill. There is an optional reset you can choose (it
trades what you built for a reward), and the space between runs is a menu, not a shared place. Made and
published by Embark Studios; paid, with no shop named on the page; no early access named. The page does
not name the engine or say who hosts a match. 415,579 reviews in all languages, 82.0% positive, *Very
Positive*. **Row 1 of the large-games queue: the closest thing on Steam to Dominion's shape** — third
person, sci-fi, extraction, runs that end, shooting robot enemies — **and the first game in this corpus
where the other players can shoot you.** Dominion is PvE, four players on the same side, hosted on one
player's machine; reviewers mention duos and trios, and the page does not state the largest squad. No
arenas.

## 1. In plain words

**The game's best thing and its worst thing are the same thing: the other players.** The most common
real praise is that you never know if the next player will help you or kill you, and that this is
thrilling: 95 times, about one review in sixteen. Many of the top complaints are about other players
too: people who ruin games on purpose (42), the player who says "friendly" and then shoots you in the
back (38), cheaters (38), and players who wait by the exit to kill you (27). One review in five (305 of
1,473) is about how strangers behaved.

**The number one complaint is a wish for a mode without other players.** 55 reviews say a mode they
expected is missing, and 42 of those ask for a mode where you only fight the robots. That wish triples
over the year.

**Two groups of players each think they are describing the game.** 147 reviews only praise the other
players, 158 only complain about them, and just 11 do both. The people who love the tension and the
people who hate being betrayed are, in this sample, different people.

**The share of players recommending the game fell from 86% to 52% in eleven months, and what rose was
cheating.** Complaints about cheaters went from about 1 in 100 reviews at launch to more than 12 in 100
from May 2026 on. "The studio does nothing about the cheaters" followed one step behind. Complaints about
the design itself fell over the same time. By 2026 the reviewer argues with the lobby, not the game.

**The people who complain are the ones who stayed longest.** The middle thumbs-down reviewer has played
92 hours; the middle thumbs-up reviewer 41. By mid-2026 the average reviewer has close to 300 hours, and
the praise that comes from being new — the sound, the unlocks, "it won me over" — is gone.

**The game's own fix is a matchmaker that groups players by how they behave, and players found five
ways it fails.** 23 say it works. Others say shooting back once puts you with the killers, peaceful
players still meet killers, killers act nice to get put with easy targets, cheaters gather where the
fighting is, and it stopped working as fewer people played.

**The surprise: 40% of the sample is a thumbs up with no words at all** — 591 reviews, the highest share
in the study. A very popular game collects a lot of "good game".

## 3. ⭐ The trust mode: 95 people praise the thing 38 people call betrayal

**`not-knowing-who-to-trust-is-the-thrill` is the biggest specific signal in this game and it does
not fall over time** — 5.7, 7.5, 7.4, 4.4 per 100 across the four periods. Reviewers write *"be careful
who you trust"*, *"gave me permanent trust issues"*, *"I love being a rat - 10/10"*, *"met a lot of cool
people and betrayed a lot of people too."*

**The same event, seen from the other side, is `says-friendly-then-shoots-you-in-the-back`** — 38
bullets, 21 of them thumbs down. *"I was sharing blueprints and healing a man, then hunted and gunned

### Read the volume, not the sample

**Three quarters of everything ever written about this game in English was written in the first
three months.** 157,893 of 251,588 reviews — 62.8% — arrived in 2025-10 to 2025-12, and 42% in
2025-11 alone. **The sample's 86% thumb in November is the game's real reputation; the 50% in
2026-09 is 811 people.** Steam's *Very Positive* is the launch, and it will stay the launch for years.

### The line bends in 2026-04

**Four things happen in the same month.** The thumb drops from 76% to 64%; complaints per reader
pass praise for the first time (88 against 78); the mean reviewer has 175 hours; and `cheaters-spoil-matches`
becomes a top-three complaint. **From 2026-05 on, complaints per reader are above 100 in every
month** — the average negative reviewer lists more than one thing.

**The reviewer changes as much as the game.** Mean hours at review time go 58 → 105 → 175 → 293 across
the year. **A November review is written after a week; a June review after a season.** Praise that
depends on freshness (the sound, the progression, the AI) goes with the fresh reviewers.

response rises with it, one step behind. `exploit-ruins-the-game` — the duplication bug and the
unprotected API — is 0.1 per 100 in 2025 and 1.2 in 2026. The most-helpful review after the launch
window says it in one sentence — `226905811` (30 helpful, 936 hours): *"In the beginning it was good, but
now there are a lot of cheaters."* The late reviews add the mechanism: accounts at *"fifty cents to four
dollars"* (`221530335`), an unprotected API that let anyone *"mass-duplicate loot with a single
request"*, wipes that took honest players' currency with the duplicated (`223877518`, `230568877`), and
*"they refuse to put an anticheat and only ban a small fraction"* (`234982159`, 887 hours). **Not
checked:** no patch notes were pulled; the account prices, the API, the wipe and the anti-cheat claim are
what reviewers say.

**Betrayal peaks in 2026-02 to 04 and then falls to 0.9** — not because the backstabbing stopped, but
because the reviewer who would have written it has either left or has moved on to the cheaters.
**The demand for a PvE mode triples over the same span**, and by the last period it is the most-said
complaint after cheating.

**A different arc from Helldivers 2's** (cross-game section 16). Helldivers 2's relationship collapsed
while its game improved; here the thumb fell while cheating rose and design complaints fell. In
Helldivers 2 the late reviewer argued with the publisher; here the late reviewer argues with the lobby,
and the studio is blamed for not policing it.

## 3. ⭐ The conduct matchmaker: one system, six things said about it

**ARC Raiders sorts players by how they behaved in past matches — how often they attacked other
players — instead of by skill.** Reviewers call it aggression-based matchmaking, ABMM, the karma
system, or "carebear lobbies". **The tree had no mode for it in 2025-11. It has six now, and all six
came from this sample.**

non-PvP player - do not bother playing with friends, family or kids"* (`223340527`). **A player's team
size predicts the other players' conduct better than the matchmaker does.**

## 5. For Dominion — our reading

**These are suggestions drawn from what players said, not something the reviews said.** Dominion is a
third-person, four-player co-op sci-fi PvE extraction and arena shooter built on runs, hosted on one
player's machine (listen server), in Unreal Engine 5.

1. **Players who come for extraction tension often do not want other players shooting them.** Evidence:
   the top complaint is the missing mode (55), 42 asking for PvE, tripling to 8.0 per 100 by the last
   period; five of the top eight complaints are about other players; Escape from Duckov players praise
   having no PvP 51 times. Dominion could treat being PvE-only as a selling point and say it plainly on
   the store page; get the risk and tension from the world and the clock, not from other squads.
   **Strength: strong.**
2. **Cheating kills a game slowly, and "the studio does nothing" follows it.** Evidence: cheaters 0.9 to
   12.4 per 100 in eleven months, the thumb 86% to 52%; "does nothing" 0.2 to 7.1; exploits 0.1 to 1.2;
   the wipe that hit honest players. Dominion could: in PvE on a listen server, cheating mostly hurts the
   host's own squad — but any shared progress, trading or leaderboards need server-side checks; never
   trust the client for item grants; if a fix wipes items, protect players who did nothing wrong.
   **Strength: strong** (for anything shared between players).
3. **Gear risk must sit in the middle: too cheap and nothing matters, too dear and nobody uses it.**
   Evidence: dying costs nothing 11; too afraid to use the good gear 8; stakes worth the risk 32 (the
   most-helpful review: "a successful extraction is a real adrenaline rush"); failing costs too much 8;
   quick recovery praised 7. Dominion could let players lose some of what they carry, but keep a safety
   net (an insured slot, a cheap standard kit that still feels good, rewards for using good gear); test
   how often players actually bring their best gear. **Strength: medium-strong.**
4. **Runs that turn into stories are the praise to aim for.** Evidence: runs become stories you retell 26;
   keeps pulling you back 65; "like scenes from a sci-fi movie" (`220132764`). Dominion could build runs
   around surprises (a big machine arrives, a squad member goes down at the exit) and make it easy to clip
   and share. **Strength: medium.**
5. **Smart, readable robot enemies win praise, and a shared threat bonds players.** Evidence: good AI 25;
   "robots that move like nothing he has seen"; a shared threat turns strangers into allies 8. No
   counterplay complaints 9. Dominion could invest in enemy movement and behaviour; give every big attack a
   way to dodge, block or answer it. **Strength: medium.**
6. **Do not lock players in long animations when danger is near.** Evidence: "interacting freezes you and
   that is when you die" built from this game (no count). Dominion could let players cancel looting,
   reviving and healing, or make them short; give a squad mate a way to cover. **Strength: weak** (no
   count), but cheap to act on.
7. **Joining a match in progress and spawning beside others both annoy.** Evidence: dropped into a match
   already underway 13; players spawn on top of each other (no count); smaller teams matched against full
   squads (no count on the page; 3 on the 2026-10-03 re-run). Dominion could, if late joining exists, tell
   the joiner how far along the run is and give them a fair start; spread spawns; scale enemies to squad
   size. **Strength: medium-weak.**
8. **Launch praise fades as reviewers get hundreds of hours; plan goals for the long tail.** Evidence:
   mean hours went 58 to 293; sound, unlocks and "won me over" fell to 0.0 in 2026; nothing left to chase
   18 and rising; updates too slow 12 and rising. Dominion could plan end-game goals and a steady update
   pace before launch, aimed at the 200-hour player. **Strength: medium.**
9. **Easy to start brings in new players.** Evidence: easy to start 25, rising to 2.4 per 100; won over
   someone who avoids the genre 15; many say it was their first extraction shooter. Dominion could keep
   the first runs simple and safe, teach extraction step by step. **Strength: medium.**
10. **A system that sorts players needs to be clear and hard to game.** Evidence: the behaviour
    matchmaker: works 23; misreads self-defence 7; puts cheaters with fighters 6; peaceful players still
    meet killers 5; stopped working as the player count fell 5; gamed by killers 2; 4 did not know it
    existed. Dominion could, for public PvE matchmaking, keep any sorting (skill, play style, voice chat)
    simple, shown to the player, and safe at low player counts. **Strength: medium.**
11. **Sell the game's community feel early; strangers teaming up is a launch effect.** Evidence: strangers
    team up 4.1 per 100 at launch, 0.9 by mid-2026; welcoming community 17; much better with friends 25.
    Dominion could reward strangers for helping (shared rewards for revives, emotes, pings).
    **Strength: medium-weak.**
12. **Inventory and between-run spaces matter.** Evidence: inventory is a chore; no shared place between
    runs; a private base asked for (3). Dominion could keep inventory quick with sorting and bulk actions;
    consider a hub where the squad meets between runs. **Strength: weak.**
13. **Sound must tell up from down in 3D spaces.** Evidence: cannot tell above from below (built here, no
    count on the page; 2 on the re-run); punchy sound praised 17 at launch. Dominion could test sound
    positioning on multi-level maps. **Strength: weak.**
14. **Generated voices get noticed.** Evidence: "the voices are generated and it shows" (four voices,
    built here); an ethics complaint left open. Dominion could use recorded actors for key voices, or
    expect the comment. **Strength: weak.**
15. **Support and bans must explain themselves.** Evidence: support replied with a form letter; banned
    with no reason given; what a cheater took is never given back (all built here). Dominion could give
    real replies and a ban reason; restore items lost to bugs or cheats. **Strength: weak.**
16. **The fiction and the rules should agree.** Evidence: "the rules contradict the story" built here.
    Dominion could make what earns rewards match what the story says the squad is there to do.
    **Strength: weak.**
17. **A strong launch sets the score for years.** Evidence: 62.8% of English reviews in the first three
    months; Steam's Very Positive "is the launch, and it will stay the launch for years" even as the late
    thumb fell to about 50%. Dominion could put the most polish into the first three months.
    **Strength: strong.**

## 7. Confidence and limits

1. **This is a 0.59% sample, not a census.** ±2.69% on a whole-sample share; wider on any month; the
   last five months are 113 reviews and a single review moves 2026-09 by ten points. They are reported
   only because all five point the same way.
2. **The launch month is two days and 19 reviews.** 2025-10-30 to 31. It came in one under the
   20-review floor because the pull got 19 of the planned 20. Nothing about launch day should be read
   from it; 2025-11 is the launch month in practice.
3. **172 reviews are dated by creation, not by their last edit.** The trend is understated by this,
   not overstated: late complaints are filed on early dates.
4. **No patch notes were pulled.** Section 3's line cannot be set against the patch record the way
   Helldivers 2's was. The reviews name two events — the 2026-03-31 patch (`222215525`, *"too hard for
   solo players just starting out"*) and a currency wipe around 2026-04 — and this page cannot confirm
   either from the studio's side. The duplication exploit, the account prices and the anti-cheat claim
   are also what reviewers say. Whether the matchmaker broke or the player count fell below what it needs
   cannot be told apart; the tree records what the player felt, not the root cause (settled 2026-09-10).
   All six matchmaker modes rest on this one game.
5. **One language.** 164,000 reviews in other languages are unpulled; the review text mentions
