## 1. The short version

**The game's best thing and its worst thing are the same thing: the other players.**

- **The single most-said specific thing is praise for not knowing whether the next player will help
  you or kill you** — `not-knowing-who-to-trust-is-the-thrill`, 95 bullets, 6.4 per 100 — one review
  in sixteen.
- **Four of the top six complaints are also about the other players**: griefers (42), the friendly
  who shoots you in the back (38), cheaters (38), exit campers (27). **Together with the praise, one
  review in five (305 of 1,473) is about how strangers behaved.**
- **The number one complaint is a wish for a mode without them**: `game-design.modes.expected-mode-missing`,
  55 bullets, almost all of them *add a PvE-only mode*.
- **The thumb fell from 86% to 52% over eleven months, and what rose was cheating.** `cheaters-spoil-matches`
  goes from 0.9 per 100 in the launch months to **12.4 per 100 from 2026-05 on** — a fourteen-fold rise.
  `does-nothing-about-the-cheaters` follows it from 0.2 to 7.1. Section 3.
- **The game's own defence against all of this is a matchmaker that sorts players by conduct, and the
  sample says six different things about it.** Section 4.

### ⭐ The trust mode: 95 people praise the thing 38 people call betrayal

**`not-knowing-who-to-trust-is-the-thrill` is the biggest specific signal in this game and it does
not fall over time** — 5.7, 7.5, 7.4, 4.4 per 100 across the four periods. Reviewers write *"be careful
who you trust"*, *"gave me permanent trust issues"*, *"I love being a rat - 10/10"*, *"met a lot of cool
people and betrayed a lot of people too."*

**The same event, seen from the other side, is `says-friendly-then-shoots-you-in-the-back`** — 38
bullets, 21 of them thumbs down. *"I was sharing blueprints and healing a man, then hunted and gunned
down for three matches straight"* (`223339137`, 7 helpful).

**Only 11 reviews carry both a positive conduct mode and a negative one.** 147 reviews carry only the
praise, 158 only the complaint. **The sample does not contain many people who enjoy the tension and
resent the betrayal. It contains two populations who each think they are describing the game.** The
tree records what the player felt (settled with Rico 2026-09-10), so both stand.

### Read the volume, not the sample

**Three quarters of everything ever written about this game in English was written in the first
three months.** 157,893 of 251,588 reviews — 62.8% — arrived in 2025-10 to 2025-12, and 42% in
2025-11 alone. **The sample's 86% thumb in November is the game's real reputation; the 50% in
2026-09 is 811 people.** Steam's *Very Positive* is the launch, and it will stay the launch for years.

### The line bends in 2026-04

**Four things happen in the same month.** The thumb drops from 76% to 64%; complaints per reader
pass praise for the first time (88 against 78); the mean reviewer has 175 hours; and `cheaters-spoil-matches`

**The reviewer changes as much as the game.** Mean hours at review time go 58 → 105 → 175 → 293 across

depends on freshness (the sound, the progression, the AI) goes with the fresh reviewers.

## 4. ⭐ The conduct matchmaker: one system, six things said about it

**ARC Raiders sorts players by how they behaved in past matches — how often they attacked other
players — instead of by skill.** Reviewers call it aggression-based matchmaking, ABMM, the karma
system, or "carebear lobbies". **The tree had no mode for it in 2025-11. It has six now, and all six
came from this sample.**

| Mode | | Count | What the player said |
|---|---|---|---|
| `sorted-by-how-you-play-and-it-works` | + | 23 | *"if you are stuck in lobbies that do not fit your playstyle, rate the match poorly and the aggression-based matchmaking changes who you play against"* (`220131270`) |
| `shooting-back-once-sorts-you-with-the-killers` | − | 7 | *"500 hours with almost no PvP beyond self-defence, and one kill puts you instantly in kill-on-sight lobbies"* (`223340527`, 9 helpful) |
| `the-fighters-lobbies-are-where-the-cheaters-are` | − | 6 | the hunters' lobbies are where the cheaters concentrate, so playing PvP means playing cheaters |
| `peaceful-players-still-land-with-the-killers` | − | 5 | *"you will only be put in PVE lobby's if you do not do any PVP, well that's a lie"* (`232925246`) |
| `the-conduct-sorting-used-to-work-and-no-longer-does` | − | 5 | *"something has changed recently. Matchmaking no longer works"* (`227450454`, 556 hours, 8 helpful) |
| `killers-play-nice-to-get-sorted-in-with-the-peaceful` | − | 2 | *"people abuse it to get put in easy, friendlier lobbies just to shoot friendly people in the back of the head"* (`220786008`) |
| `wants-players-sorted-by-how-they-play` | − | 4 | the wish, from reviewers who did not know it existed or did not believe it did |

**The praise and the five complaints are all about the same rule, and none of them contradicts the
others.** It works for people whose conduct is consistent. It misreads a peaceful player who shoots
back. It cannot tell a hunter who behaves for a few matches from a peaceful player. It concentrates
the cheaters in the lobbies where the fighting is. And as the population falls there are fewer
lobbies to sort into, so it degrades. **The sample contains the design's whole failure analysis,
written by its users, one mode at a time.**

**The solo-versus-squad split is its own finding.** `solo-queue-is-friendly-and-squads-shoot-on-sight`
(12, neutral): *"solo you can find a friendly lobby... duo and trio are instantly kill-on-sight even for a
non-PvP player - do not bother playing with friends, family or kids"* (`223340527`). **A player's team
size predicts the other players' conduct better than the matchmaker does.**

## 5. What the sample says about the design itself

**The design praise is the extraction loop working as designed.** `stakes-worth-the-risk` (32): *"a
successful extraction is a real adrenaline rush"* (`209802131`, 87 helpful, the most-helpful review in
the sample). `the-runs-turn-into-stories-you-retell` (26): *"incredibly cinematic - players, ARC
machines and chaos everywhere, like scenes from a sci-fi movie"* (`220132764`). `good-ai-behaviour`
(25): *"robots that move like nothing he has seen"* (`223339137`). **The shared threat is what makes
the cooperation happen** — `the-shared-threat-turns-strangers-into-allies` (8) was built for this game.

**The design complaints are the cost side of the same loop.** `too-afraid-to-use-the-good-gear` (8):
*"why would he risk a Bobcat when he can lose a Stitcher for a quarter of the price"* (`223877027`).
`dying-costs-nothing` (11) is its opposite: free loadouts mean *"nobody tries to progress, the group
always runs free loadouts, looting loses its appeal"* (`219490113`). **Eleven people say death is too
cheap and eight say gear is too precious to use — both in a game with one punishment model.**

**The wish list is short and repeated**: a price on attacking other players (`wants-a-price-on-attacking-other-players`,
8 — a karma flag, a bounty, a penalty), a way to see a hostile player before they strike (2), a PvE-only
mode (55), and a private base where progress shows (gap 343, 3 sightings on the social-features home).

## 6. What the tree gained

**38 modes built during this run — the most of any game.** 37 of the 279 modes used here appear in
no other game, and 33 of those 37 are about the other players or the conduct sorting. **Before this
game the tree had no way to say that a stranger could shoot you.** The player-conduct subject went
from a handful of co-op modes — welcoming, trolls, cheaters, quitting — to a subject that separates betrayal,
camping, swooping, turning hostile over time, and the solo-versus-squad split.

The modes, by subject:

- `community.player-conduct` — `strangers-team-up-instead-of-fighting` (+), `not-knowing-who-to-trust-is-the-thrill` (+),
  `says-friendly-then-shoots-you-in-the-back`, `players-camp-the-exit`, `a-third-player-swoops-on-your-fight`,
  `the-players-turned-hostile-after-launch`, `slurs-and-abuse-over-voice-chat`, `solo-queue-is-friendly-and-squads-shoot-on-sight` (~)
- `engineering.matchmaking` — the six conduct-sorting modes in section 4, plus `dropped-into-a-match-already-underway`
  and `smaller-teams-are-matched-against-full-squads`
- `game-design.co-op-design` — `the-shared-threat-turns-strangers-into-allies` (+), `wants-a-price-on-attacking-other-players`,
  `wants-hostile-players-flagged-before-they-strike`
- `game-design.punishment-model.too-afraid-to-use-the-good-gear`; `game-design.power-balance.the-gear-gap-decides-the-fight`

## 7. Confidence and limits

1. **This is a 0.59% sample, not a census.** ±2.69% on a whole-sample share; wider on any month; the
   last five months are 113 reviews and a single review moves 2026-09 by ten points.
2. **The launch month is two days and 19 reviews.** 2025-10-30 to 31. It came in one under the
   20-review floor because the pull got 19 of the planned 20. Nothing about launch day should be read
   from it; 2025-11 is the launch month in practice.
3. **172 reviews are dated by creation, not by their last edit.** The trend is understated by this,
   not overstated: late complaints are filed on early dates.
4. **No patch notes were pulled.** Section 3's line cannot be set against the patch record the way
   Helldivers 2's was. The reviews name two events — the 2026-03-31 patch (`222215525`, *"too hard for
   solo players just starting out"*) and a currency wipe around 2026-04 — and this page cannot confirm
   either from the studio's side.
5. **One language.** 164,000 reviews in other languages are unpulled; the review text mentions
   Oceania lobbies and a China-first content release (`228603070`) that an English sample cannot weigh.
6. **31% of bullets are `unknown`**, 591 of them a wordless thumbs up. That is a fact about how people
   review a very popular game, not a fault in the tree — the direction audit found 0 disagreements and
   0 unfitted observations across all thirty batches.
