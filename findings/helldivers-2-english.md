# HELLDIVERS 2 — English

**A sample: 1,626 of 819,840 English reviews read (0.20%); no margin of error was computed for this
page.** The finished read of one group: one game, one language, every sampled review tagged. The date
the grid was pulled and Steam's own English count on that day are not on this page; the cross-game page
gives Steam's English thumbs-up as 83.3%, the same as the sample.

**A co-op PvE shooter with friendly fire and a satirical sci-fi setting** — players fight "for Super
Earth" and "for Democracy". The Steam store page calls it *"a fast, frantic, and ferocious third-person
shooter"* with *"squads of up to four"* (checked 2026-10-03, as recorded in `DOMINION-TAKEAWAYS.md`). Sold
as a paid game with a paid currency that players can also earn by playing, and run as a live service with
frequent patches and content updates (Escalation of Freedom, an Illuminate invasion, Machinery of
Oppression). Reviewers say a publisher account became required in May 2024 and that the game then stopped
being sold in many countries; the page calls it "the publisher-account fight" and does not check the
details. Some players object to extra software it installs. Reviews start in February 2024; the Steam
patch record starts in September 2023. The page does not give who hosts a match, the engine, the price,
the developer or publisher by name, the all-languages review count, or how missions are structured.
**Very close to Dominion in what players do** — third person, four-player co-op, sci-fi, PvE against
alien hordes, friendly fire, and a strong in-world voice. It differs in being a big live service with a
big publisher, a required outside account and a paid currency, and nothing here speaks to a listen
server directly. Most of what this game teaches is about running a game after launch, not about its
design.

⚠️ **This sample is five times thinner than Deep Rock Galactic's.** That group read 1.0% of its
reviews; this one reads **0.20%**, because the population is four times larger. Every month is only
partly read, so month weights run to ×579 and **a single observation in a quiet month can move a
weighted share by a full percentage point.** Treat weighted shares as direction, not as measurement.
**The counts below are unweighted unless a table says otherwise.**

⚠️ **One month dominates the volume, and it is not the launch.** May 2024 — the publisher-account fight —
has 309,103 reviews against the launch month's 147,461. And 2026 rests on 232 reviews for a whole year.

**How to read the words below.** A **bullet** is one thing one person said. A **tag** is where that
bullet was filed. Tags have three levels: a **division** (who at a studio owns it — game design, art,
engineering), a **subject** (the thing being talked about), and a **mode** (what actually happened).
`unknown` means the person raised a subject and never said what about it.

## 1. In plain words

**Almost nobody is complaining about the game. They are complaining about the people running it.** Four
of the five most common complaints in 1,626 reviews are choices somebody made: a patch made the game
worse (47 times), the publisher puts players off (45), it is not sold in my country (39), and the studio
weakened things players liked (39). The fifth is crashes (37). The first complaint about how the game
plays, "it repeats itself", is seventh, at 22.

**Most players still like it, but far fewer than in the best-loved co-op game.** 83.3% of reviews are
thumbs up, against Deep Rock Galactic's 97.1%. One review in six is negative here; one in thirty-five was
negative there.

**What players love is the people they play with and the joke the game tells.** The game's battle cry,
"FOR DEMOCRACY" and the like, is written 398 times — one review in four says nothing else. After that:
the studio listens and acts (78), much better with friends (62), players calling themselves by the game's
own names (54), the satire lands (53), it keeps pulling you back (47).

**The biggest surprise: players love being able to shoot their friends.** 37 reviews praise friendly fire
for the funny stories it makes; only 3 in the whole study complain about it. The praise is about doing it
to your friends, by your own hand, for a laugh. The complaints are about strangers doing it to you, over
and over.

**The game got better while the relationship broke.** From 2024 to 2026, crash complaints fell from 2.5
to 1.7 per 100 reviews. In the same time, thumbs down tripled, to 36.2% in 2026. In 2024, good things
said about the studio beat bad things 3.3 to 1. In 2026, bad things beat good 9 to 1.

**The people who complain are the people who stayed.** The middle thumbs-down reviewer has played 73
hours; the middle thumbs-up reviewer 40. The complaints that rise in 2026 are about the studio weakening
favourite weapons and fighting with its own players, not about a broken game.

**The loudest month was not the angriest.** May 2024 had more than twice as many reviews as the launch
month, all about a required publisher account and the countries the game stopped being sold in. But only
30.9% of that month's reviews were negative. The angriest months came later: August 2024 (48.1%), May 2025
(50.8%), and spring 2026, with April 2026 the worst of all at 62.3%.

## 3. ⭐ The finding: the game got better and the relationship collapsed

**Normalised per 100 reviews read**, because the three years are read at very different depths (1,096
reviews in 2024, 297 in 2025, 233 in 2026):

| | 2024 | 2025 | 2026 |
|---|---|---|---|
| `developer-communication.listens-and-acts` (+) | **4.8** | 7.1 | **1.7** |
| **All four negative dev-comms modes** (−) | **1.5** | 2.0 | **15.5** |
| `patch-quality.nerfs-what-players-liked` (−) | 1.6 | 1.7 | **7.3** |
| `engineering.stability.crashes-repeatedly` (−) | **2.5** | 2.0 | **1.7** |
| Ratio, praise to criticism of the studio | **3.3 : 1** | 3.5 : 1 | **1 : 9.0** |

**Read the last rows together.**

- **Crashes fall 30%** (32% on the master page). The engineering complaint that dominated the launch
  year is quietly going away.
- **The weakening complaint rises 4.7×.**
- **In 2024, positive things said about the studio outnumbered negative ones 3.3 to 1. In 2026,
  negative outnumber positive 9 to 1.** That is a **thirty-fold swing in the ratio**, in a game whose
  technical problems were improving. **2025 is the studio's best year and 2026 its worst** — praise falls
  to a third of its 2024 rate while criticism rises ten-fold. **The reversal is in the same subject, one
  year apart.**

⛔ **A live-service game is not judged on its build quality after the first year. It is judged on
whether the studio is felt to be on the player's side.**

### The evidence that this is a relationship problem and not a quality problem

- **`punishes-criticism` goes 0 → 1 → 6.** The 2026 uses are **four independent reviewers**, none of
  them involved, reporting the same incident: a player who offered the studio help was doxxed.
  **Nobody reported it in 2024 because it had not happened.** This is hearsay in every instance (section 7).
- **`the-fanbase-puts-me-off` reaches 22**, and by 2026 it is used from **both sides** — one reviewer
  objects to the players attacking the studio, another to the players defending it.
- **Three reviewers in one 2026 batch gave three incompatible readings of the same community.**
- **`marketing.reputation.judged-unfairly` (21) becomes the most common opinion-carrying mode in the
  final batch.** **The argument itself has become the subject of the reviews.**
- **A mode had to be built for it:** `review.the-controversy-drove-me-away`, from *"I game to relax
  and enjoy myself and I find there is more drama with this game than anything else I do in my life."*

### What the game still gets credit for, three years in

**These are stable across all three years and are the game's real assets:**

| Mode | Count |
|---|---|
| `community.playing-with-friends.much-better-with-friends` | 62 |
| `narrative.tone.satire-lands` | 53 |
| `game-design.replayability.keeps-pulling-you-back` | 47 |
| `marketing.reputation.best-in-its-category` | 46 |
| `game-design.co-op-design.friendly-fire-makes-stories` | 37 |

⭐ **`friendly-fire-makes-stories` is 37 against 3 for its negative twin**, and pulling the raw text of
all three negatives showed the split is **not** about the mechanic:

- **The positives are self-inflicted, against friends, and comic** — *"dropping a bomb on his own
  squad and then apologising insincerely."*
- **The negatives are done TO the reviewer, by strangers, repeatedly** — *"I get teamkilled about 85%
  of my games."*

**The axis is agency and relationship, not the mechanic and not the severity.** Removing friendly fire
would delete the game's most-loved feature; **the lever is who can do it to you and how often.** In the
whole corpus, 37 people praise it and 3 file it as a cost; nothing about friendly fire appears in this
game's complaint list. Cross-game section 5: 2.3 per 100 here, 0.6 in Deep Rock Galactic, 0.0 in Back 4
Blood.

## 3. ⭐ The patch notes, lined up

**799 Steam news items, 2023-09-14 to 2026-08-25, of which 103 carry a version number.** Pulled
2026-09-02. **Unlike the Deep Rock group, the record covers every month of the sample.** This is the
primary source for every patch date and title on this page: `raw/helldivers-2/patchnotes/index.jsonl`.

### The launch quarter: patches every two to three days

**Eleven versioned patches in February 2024 alone**, from `01.000.004` on the 9th to `1.000.13` on the
28th. **That month is the least negative of the launch period, at 14.1%.**

### Then the record and the sentiment move together

| Date | Patch | What the record says | Negative share that month |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2024-03-06 | `1.000.100` | *"nerfs its best weapons, adds new hazards"* | 14.1% → **25.1%** |
| 2024-03-12 | `1.000.102` | *"nerfs hardest enemy and spawn rates"* | |
| 2024-03-20 | `1.000.103` | *"sadly doesn't fix the highest priority issue"* | |
| 2024-04-09 | `01.000.202` | *"finally fixes those many crashes"* | **35.2%** |
| 2024-04-29 | `01.000.300` | *"makes some massive changes to ammo"* | |
| 2024-08-06 | `1.001.002` | Escalation of Freedom | **48.1%** |
| 2024-08-21 | `1.001.005` | **Emergency Hot Fix** | |
| 2025-05-13 | `01.003.000` | Illuminate invasion — **6 patches that month** | **50.8%** |
| 2026-03 → 2026-05 | `6.1.0` → `6.2.5` | Machinery of Oppression — 6 patches | **52.3% → 62.3% → 56.2%** |

**The two headline titles in March 2024 both contain the word "nerfs", and the negative share almost
doubles in the same month.** That is the earliest dated instance of the complaint that becomes the
game's defining one.

### What the patch record does not explain

**It does not explain 2024-08.** Escalation of Freedom was a content release, not a removal, and it
still produced the worst month of the launch year. **A studio can ship the thing it promised and lose
the month anyway.**

**It also does not explain the 2026 slide.** The Machinery of Oppression patches are ordinary cadence
— one to three a month, the same rate as 2025 — and the sentiment falls anyway. **By 2026 the
complaint has stopped being about any individual patch.** The finding section above is why.

## 4. What the sample says about the design itself

- **Co-op and online play.** Much better with friends: 62 (3.8 per 100) — lower than Back 4 Blood (7.5)
  and Deep Rock Galactic (10.1). Fine with strangers 14; a welcoming community 33; trolls and griefers 11;
  other players put me off 22 (by 2026 mostly about the fight over the studio). **Friendly fire is the
  eighth best thing about the game** (37 praise, 3 against in the whole study; section 3). The mission
  falling apart is the fun: 13. **Solo:** works solo 15, punishing solo 11. **Every role needed:** 0.0 per
  100 here (4.6 in Deep Rock Galactic). **Disconnects:** 14. **Account:** a required account or platform
  link, 15 (13 in May 2024). The re-run finds one bullet on `co-op-design.the-host-draws-the-enemies`, a
  mode used in no other game. Hosting, joining a running game, rejoining, scaling, matchmaking, crossplay
  and loot sharing: **nothing else said** on this page.
- **Combat and feel.** Combat hits hard: 22 (1.4 per 100), lower than Back 4 Blood's 5.8. The March 2024
  patch notes say a patch "nerfs its best weapons"; *the studio weakened things players liked* is 39 and
  rises to 7.3 per 100 by 2026; an ammo change in April 2024 ("massive changes to ammo"). Silly actions
  with no use (emotes and the like): 6 (0.4 per 100), against 111 in Deep Rock Galactic. Shots stopped by
  scenery: seen once.
- **Movement and controls.** **Nothing said** on this page; the re-run finds one bullet on
  `controls.too-many-buttons-to-manage`, used in no other game.
- **Enemies, bosses and difficulty.** Hard in a good way: 16. The difficulty ladder is well built: 14. A
  March 2024 patch "nerfs hardest enemy and spawn rates" and another "adds new hazards" (patch notes). New
  enemy factions arrive as content: the Illuminate invasion in May 2025 (50.8% negative that month).
  Bosses and unfair attacks: **nothing said** on this page.
- **Progression, loot and randomness.** Builds deep and varied 16; barely change anything 11 — both sides
  of one design choice. Grind: 11 (0.7 per 100), the same low rate in all three of the first games (0.9,
  0.7, 0.7). Paid currency earnable by playing: 20 praise. Loot drops, luck and time gates: **nothing
  said** on this page.
- **Runs, content and replay.** Keeps pulling you back: 47, steady all three years. Repeats itself: 22
  (1.4 per 100) — 1.8, 1.4, 1.4 across the first three games, the tightest match in the whole study.
  Updates add little: 10; a steady stream of updates: 14. Nothing else plays like it: 10. Content updates
  did not protect a month: Escalation of Freedom (August 2024) and the Illuminate invasion (May 2025) both
  sit in the worst months.
- **Money and price.** Fair price: 25 (1.5 per 100). Paid currency earnable by playing: 20. Selling more
  while the game is broken: 11. Buy on sale only: 0.0 per 100 (Back 4 Blood 8.6). Free updates after
  launch: 0.1 per 100 say so (Deep Rock Galactic 1.5). Money-practice complaints: 1.8 per 100. Region
  lock: 39 "not sold in my country" (30 in May 2024) — a publisher decision, not a price.
- **Tech.** Engineering is 9.3% of everything said and 21.7% of all complaints, more than game design.
  Crashes again and again: 37, falling 2.5 to 1.7 per 100; patch `01.000.202` on 2024-04-09 "finally fixes
  those many crashes" (patch notes). A bug breaks the mission or loses progress: 28. Buggy: 19. Known bugs
  never fixed: 11. Damages the computer or system: 12. Unwanted extra software (anti-cheat, a launcher or
  a companion app — the page does not say which): 12. Disconnects: 14. Required account or platform: 15.
  An Emergency Hot Fix followed the August 2024 content patch. The engine is not named.
- **The studio and the community.** Listens and acts is the second biggest praise (78), yet the bad studio
  modes add up to 51 (misreads 19, fights its players 17, ignores feedback 15) plus punishes criticism (7,
  mostly 2026). Patches: made it worse 47, weakened favourites 39, fixed what mattered 26, made it better

## 5. For Dominion — our reading

**These are suggestions drawn from what players said, not something the reviews said.** Dominion is a
third-person, four-player co-op sci-fi PvE extraction and arena shooter built on runs, hosted on one
player's machine (listen server), in Unreal Engine 5.

1. **Do not weaken what players love; it becomes the complaint that defines the game.** Evidence: "the
   studio weakened things players liked" 39 times, rising from 1.6 to 7.3 per 100 by 2026; the March 2024
   patches that "nerf its best weapons" and "nerf hardest enemy" came in the month the negative share
   nearly doubled (14.1% to 25.1%); "a patch made it worse" is the top complaint (47). Dominion could
   balance by raising weak options rather than cutting strong ones; when a cut is needed, explain it in
   the patch note and give something back in the same patch; test balance changes with players first.
   **Strength: strong.**
2. **After the first year, players judge the studio, not the build.** Evidence: crash complaints fell (2.5
   to 1.7 per 100) while thumbs down tripled (11.8% to 36.2%) and good-to-bad talk about the studio swung
   from 3.3 : 1 to 1 : 9. "The studio listens and acts" is still the second biggest praise (78). Dominion
   could keep a visible loop — "you said, we did" in patch notes; never argue with players in public;
   admit mistakes fast. **Strength: strong.**
3. **Friendly fire is loved when you do it to friends and hated when strangers do it to you.** Evidence:
   37 praise it, 3 complain in the whole study; the praise is self-inflicted and funny, the complaints are
   strangers teamkilling "about 85% of my games"; trolls and griefers 11; "the mission falling apart is
   the fun" 13. Dominion could keep friendly fire as a feature; give the host a setting for it; soften or
   limit it in public matchmaking; let the squad vote out a griefer; make funny friendly kills easy to
   share. **Strength: medium-strong.**
4. **A required outside account or a region lock can cause the biggest review wave the game ever gets.**
   Evidence: May 2024 had 309,103 English reviews, twice launch; 30 of 45 publisher complaints, 30 of 39
   "not sold in my country", 14 of 21 protest votes and 13 of 15 account complaints landed that month.
   Dominion could use only Steam login; if an outside account is ever needed, make it optional and decide
   it before launch, never after; sell everywhere Steam sells. **Strength: strong** (one event, huge
   volume).
5. **Crashes and game-breaking bugs lead the engineering complaints, and some bugs hurt the player's
   computer.** Evidence: crashes 37, bugs that end a mission or lose progress 28, buggy 19, damages the
   system 12, known bugs never fixed 11; engineering is 21.7% of all complaints. Dominion could: on a
   listen server a host crash ends the run for all four, so save each player's run rewards often and let
   the squad recover after a host drop; keep a public known-bugs list and close it out. **Strength:
   strong.**
6. **Unwanted extra software (anti-cheat, a launcher, a companion app) is a complaint of its own.**
   Evidence: 12 times. Dominion could: a PvE co-op game may not need deep anti-cheat; if it uses any, say
   what and why on the store page. **Strength: medium.**
7. **A slogan and an identity give players a voice, and it spreads.** Evidence: the war cry 398 times (one
   review in four); players take on the game's identity 54; the satire lands 53; two reviewers wrote
   another game's chant by mistake. It faded from 26.3 to 16.3 per 100 as the relationship soured. The
   cross-game page ties having this culture to the games that lasted. Dominion could give the player's side
   a short war cry and a name players can call themselves, and put both in voice lines, emotes and the
   end-of-run screen; a tone with some humour helps. **Strength: medium-strong.**
8. **A content update can still lose the month if it lands badly.** Evidence: Escalation of Freedom
   (August 2024) came with the worst month of the launch year (48.1% negative) and needed an Emergency Hot
   Fix; the Illuminate invasion month was 50.8% negative. Dominion could test new content with a small
   group before release; do not pair new content with big balance cuts; have a hotfix plan ready.
   **Strength: medium** (the months are thinly read).
9. **Fast patching at launch helps.** Evidence: eleven versioned patches in February 2024, the least
   negative month of the launch period (14.1%). Dominion could plan a launch team that can ship fixes every
   few days in the first month. **Strength: medium-weak** (the months line up; cause is not proven).
10. **Play with friends is the core, and strangers need to be safe too.** Evidence: much better with
    friends 62; fine with strangers 14; welcoming community 33; trolls and griefers 11. Dominion could make
    inviting friends and joining friends' games one click; give public lobbies a kick vote and a mute.
    **Strength: medium.**
11. **Solo play is a real trade-off: some say it works, some say it punishes.** Evidence: works solo 15,
    punishing solo 11; both sides show up in all three of the first games. Dominion could decide on purpose
    how a solo run scales (fewer enemies, or help), and say so in game. **Strength: medium.**
12. **"It repeats itself" shows up at the same rate in every co-op game.** Evidence: 22 here (1.4 per 100);
    1.8, 1.4, 1.4 across the first three games. Dominion could plan mission and arena variety from the
    start, mixing objectives, places and enemy sets. **Strength: medium-strong** (across games).
13. **A fair price and a currency you can earn keep money complaints low.** Evidence: fair price 25; paid
    currency earnable by playing 20; money-practice complaints only 1.8 per 100; "buy on sale only" 0.0.
    But "selling more while broken" 11. Dominion could let any paid currency be earned in play; do not
    launch a new paid item in a week when a big bug is still open. **Strength: medium.**
14. **Builds must feel different, or players say they do not matter.** Evidence: deep and varied 16;
    barely change anything 11; grindy 11. Dominion could make every loadout choice change how a fight
    goes, not just a number. **Strength: weak-medium.**
15. **Fights among players about the studio can drive people away.** Evidence: other players put me off
    22, from both sides by 2026; "the fights drove me away" built from "there is more drama with this game
    than anything else I do in my life"; the doxxing story told by four reviewers (hearsay). Dominion could
    keep official spaces moderated and calm; never single out a player in public. **Strength:
    weak-medium.**
16. **Marketing claims get checked.** Evidence: "a marketing claim was untrue" 10. Dominion could promise
    only what is in the build. **Strength: weak.**
17. **Updates that add little are noticed, and a steady stream is praised.** Evidence: updates add little
    10; steady stream 14; "patches fixed what mattered" 26, "made it better" 20. Dominion could make each
    update carry one thing players can feel. **Strength: weak.**

## 7. Confidence and limits

1. ⚠️ **0.20% is a thin sample.** Five times thinner than the Deep Rock group. Every month is partly
   read and weights reach ×579. **Weighted shares are direction, not measurement.** A mode with 10
   bullets is 10 people out of 819,840. 2026 rests on 232 reviews for a whole year. No margin of error was
   computed for this page.
2. **English only.** Twelve other language groups for this game are unread; four of them — Simplified
   Chinese (116,313), Russian (30,779), European Spanish (29,201), LatAm Spanish (7,950), 184,243 reviews in
   all — were pulled and never read. **The account-region fight and the country-availability complaint are
   exactly the subjects where a non-English sample would read differently**, and the 39
   `not-sold-in-my-country` bullets here are from people writing in English about countries they mostly do
   not live in. Reviews written in another language under the English tag were not counted.
3. **Nothing here measures how many people play.** Reviews are written by people moved to write. **The
   2026 months with the worst sentiment are also the months with the fewest reviews**, and this corpus
   cannot say whether that is a smaller audience or a quieter one.
4. **The doxxing incident is hearsay in every instance.** Four reviewers report it; none was involved.
   **The corpus records that four people believed it, not that it happened as described.** No personal
   detail of anyone involved is given on this page.
5. **Reviews carrying several dated updates across years are flattened onto one date** — the report
   month, `max(created, updated)` on this page's earlier wording; the master page says the date the review
   was created. **This is an open method question with Rico and was never decided.** Across the run the
   count ran from 2 to 15 per batch of 50. The re-run counts 299 edited reviews (18.4%). The 2026 numbers
   are affected.
6. **`community.developer-communication` is defined as "what the studio says", and in this game the
   publisher was often the actor.** Publisher reversals were tagged `.listens-and-acts` throughout, per
   the standing instruction. **Whether that subject needs splitting is an open method question with
   Rico and was never decided.** The sharpest case is one review carrying **two `listens-and-acts`
   bullets about two different actors.**
7. **What was checked and what was not.** Patch dates, numbers and titles come from Steam news (799 items).
   The publisher account requirement, the country count (176 to "over 200"), the doxxing story and the
   extra software are what reviewers say and were not checked.
8. **Two counts.** The page's own counts and the 2026-10-03 re-run differ by a few bullets (2,901 against
   2,908; 1,626 reviews against 1,622 kept; a handful of modes by one to three). The year table gives
   1,093 / 297 / 232 reviews on the master page and 1,096 / 297 / 233 in the finding table; the crash fall
   is 30% here and 32% on the master page. Both are shown.
9. **The pages are tags, not quotes;** few direct quotes are given.
